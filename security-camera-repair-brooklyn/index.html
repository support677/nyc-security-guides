Work from the recorder outward

Start at the box and move toward the cameras. Each step rules out a whole category of failure.

  1. Is the recorder on and showing a picture on its own monitor? If the phone app is dark but the monitor plugged into the recorder shows live video, the cameras and recorder are fine and the problem is the internet connection or the app login.
  2. Did every camera drop at once? Then look upstream of the cameras: the PoE switch or multi-channel power supply, a tripped outlet, or a new router from your internet provider that changed the network.
  3. Is the recorder beeping? Hikvision and Dahua boxes do this when their internal drive flags a problem; open the drive status page in the menu to confirm.
  4. Only one camera is out? Swap its cable or port with a working camera's. If the problem follows the camera, it is the camera; if it stays on the port, it is the cable or the channel.

"Live but not recording" is its own problem

A system that shows live video but has gaps in playback is usually one of three things: a hard drive that has worn out after three to five years of constant writing, a recording schedule that was reset by a firmware update, or motion settings that skip the area you actually care about. Check the drive status and each channel's schedule before anything else. If you are replacing the drive anyway, the free camera storage calculator shows how many days of footage a given size will hold.

Brooklyn-specific wear and tear

Glare in brownstone vestibules

A camera that looks fine by day and turns into a white blur at night is often not broken. Its infrared light is bouncing off a glass storm door, a tiled vestibule floor, a marble stoop or brass mailboxes. Moving or re-angling the camera, or switching to a model with separate lighting, fixes it without new parts.

Salt air on the southern shore

From Bay Ridge around to Brighton Beach, Coney Island and Sheepshead Bay, weather seals and connectors give out sooner. Fogging or water droplets inside the lens mean the seal has failed. The camera body usually has to be replaced, and connectors on the rest of the system should be checked at the same visit.

Street-level vandalism

Storefront and walk-up cameras get spray-painted, knocked off and have cables cut. Paint on a dome cover can sometimes be solved by swapping just the cover; paint on the lens or a snapped mount usually means a new camera.

When a camera is not worth fixing

  • Consumer cameras from Ring, Nest, Arlo or Wyze are closed units with nothing inside a technician can swap. Go through the maker's warranty or buy a replacement; Abstract Enterprises Security Systems does not service these.
  • Old analog systems with working cameras are often worth keeping. A hybrid recorder can run the existing coax cameras and add IP cameras later.
  • Systems where the manufacturer no longer makes a compatible camera can still take a generic replacement of the same resolution, so one dead camera rarely forces a full replacement.

Before you call, have this ready

  • The recorder's brand and model, from the label on the box.
  • Whether you still have the admin password, or whether the original installer set it.
  • Which cameras are out, and when each one stopped.
  • Anything that changed: a new router, a power outage, building work, a storm.

Frequently asked questions

Do my cameras still record if the internet goes down?

A system with a local DVR or NVR keeps recording to its hard drive during an internet outage. You just cannot see it remotely in the app until the connection is back.

Why are all my cameras offline at the same time?

When every camera drops together the cause is almost always upstream: the PoE switch or shared power supply, the recorder itself, or a new or reset router that changed the network.

Why is my camera live but not recording?

Check the recorder's hard-drive status first, since drives commonly fail after three to five years. Then check each channel's recording schedule, which firmware updates can reset, and the motion settings.

Why does my camera turn white or blurry at night?

Usually the infrared light is reflecting off a nearby surface such as a glass door, tile floor or stoop. Re-angling or relocating the camera often fixes it without replacing anything.

Can you fix a Ring or Nest camera?

No. They are sealed consumer products with no repairable parts, so the options are a manufacturer warranty claim or replacing the device.
