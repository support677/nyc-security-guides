Guarding Vision App Support Repair Hudson Valley calls often involve a recorder that has been sitting in a closet, a barn office or a basement for six or eight years without a single update. Guarding Vision is a dealer-branded version of Hikvision's viewing app, so the box behind it is Hikvision or a Hikvision OEM unit, and that family of hardware has a public security history that owners of older systems should know about. Old firmware is also one of the most common reasons the app stops connecting after years of working. This guide covers how to tell what firmware you have, why the update file matters on dealer-branded units, and how to decide between updating, walling the recorder off, or replacing it. Why an old recorder stops talking to the app

The app reaches your recorder through the maker's cloud service. That service changes over time, and recorders on very old firmware can lose the ability to register with it, which shows up in the app as the device going offline for no obvious reason. Before chasing routers and cables, check the firmware version and build date on the recorder's own screen (usually under system or maintenance information). A build several years old is a strong hint.

The 2021 flaw every older box should be checked for

In September 2021 a command-injection flaw in the web server of many Hikvision cameras and recorders was published as CVE-2021-36260. It scores 9.8 out of 10 for severity, it is on the federal CISA list of known exploited vulnerabilities, and CISA later named it among the flaws most used by state-sponsored attackers. Hikvision released fixed firmware. Units that never received it, especially ones reachable from the internet through port forwarding, are the ones at risk.

That does not mean every older system is compromised. It means the version number matters, and a recorder with an open port to the internet and old firmware should be dealt with rather than ignored.

Dealer-branded firmware: why the file matters

Because Guarding Vision systems are sold under other names, the right firmware is not always the file on Hikvision's own site. CISA's advisory on Hikvision devices warned that on grey-market units, updating can convert the interface back to its original state, and a mismatched file can leave a recorder that will not boot.

Update, wall it off, or replace

  • Update when fixed firmware exists for the exact model and the recorder is otherwise healthy.
  • Wall it off when no update exists but the cameras still serve you: close any forwarded ports, change default passwords, and put the recorder on its own network segment so it reaches only the cloud service it needs.
  • Replace when the hardware is failing as well, when no supported firmware exists, or when the recorder has to meet a procurement rule. A newer recorder can often reuse the cameras and cabling already in place.

What the federal rules do and do not mean

In November 2022 the FCC stopped approving new equipment authorizations for Hikvision and Dahua video gear marketed for public safety, government facility security and critical infrastructure, carrying out the Secure Equipment Act. A federal appeals court upheld most of the order in April 2024 while finding the FCC's definition of critical infrastructure too broad. Separately, the 2019 defense authorization act bars federal agencies from buying such equipment.

For a homeowner or a small business, those rules govern new authorizations and federal purchasing; they did not order owners to remove cameras already on the wall. A town building, fire district or contractor working under public money may face stricter rules, and should check with whoever writes its procurement policy before spending on repairs.

Doing the update on a valley property

A firmware update that loses power halfway can leave a recorder dead, and rural lines across the valley flicker in storms. Practical habits:

  1. Export the recorder's configuration and note the admin password before anything else.
  2. Run the update with the recorder on a battery backup, not during a storm watch.
  3. Update the recorder first, then the cameras, one at a time.
  4. Confirm the app reconnects and playback still works before leaving.

For a weekend house or a remote barn, it is often worth having the update done in person rather than remotely, so someone can recover the unit if it does not come back.

Frequently asked questions

Why did my Guarding Vision app stop connecting after years of working?

A common cause is very old recorder firmware that can no longer register with the maker's cloud service. Check the firmware build date on the recorder before changing anything else.

Is my Hikvision-based recorder affected by CVE-2021-36260?

Many Hikvision cameras and recorders on firmware older than the 2021 fix were affected. Compare your model and firmware version against Hikvision's advisory, and close any internet-facing ports until it is updated.

Can I use Hikvision firmware on a Guarding Vision recorder?

Sometimes, but only if the hardware model and region match exactly. On dealer-branded or grey-market units, the wrong file can change the interface or stop the recorder booting.

Is it illegal to keep using Hikvision cameras in New York?

No. The FCC's 2022 rule covers new equipment authorizations and federal law covers federal purchasing; neither ordered owners to remove equipment already installed. Public bodies may have stricter procurement rules.

Should I update or replace an old Guarding Vision recorder?

Update when fixed firmware exists for the exact model and the unit is healthy. Replace when no supported firmware exists or the hardware is failing; existing cameras and cabling can often be reused.
