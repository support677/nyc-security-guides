Smart Lock Installation Long Island homeowners ask about is rarely about one door. Picture an ordinary Nassau or Suffolk house: the formal front door visitors knock on, the side or kitchen door where the family really comes and goes, the inside door off the attached garage, a sliding patio door out back, and maybe a bilco hatch to the basement. Deciding which of those get a smart lock, and which lock suits each one, is most of the job. Why older Long Island doors fight back

The post-war Capes and ranches across Levittown, Hicksville and much of western Suffolk have doors that have been re-hung and re-drilled several times in seventy years. Common findings when the old lock comes out: a filled-in second bore, a backset that doesn't match the new lock, a strike plate set slightly too high so the bolt drags, or a door that has swelled and binds in humid August weather. Older North Shore houses add mortise locks, which no retail deadbolt drops into without a different lock body or careful adaptation.

The test before any smart lock goes on is simple: with the door closed, the deadbolt has to throw smoothly with one finger on the thumb-turn. If you have to lean on the door to lock it, the motor will stall and the lock will be blamed. That door work belongs in the quote, which is why the residential side is priced after someone has looked at the doors.

Storms, outages and shore exposure

Battery-powered deadbolts keep working when the grid goes down, and this matters here. In 2020, close to half of all PSEG Long Island accounts went dark at the worst point of Tropical Storm Isaias. What you lose in an outage is remote control: a Wi-Fi lock can't report in until the router is back, and a hub-based lock depends on the hub's own backup. Every lock should keep a mechanical key or override, and batteries should be changed on a calendar.

Doors within sight of the bays or the ocean are a separate problem. Salt air pits cheap finishes and corrodes exposed screws, so shore houses do better with coastal-rated finishes and stainless fasteners, at a small extra cost.

Second homes and fortnightly renters

The East End runs on second homes and summer rentals. Southampton Town's rental rules set a 14-day minimum rental period, which in practice means a new tenant, cleaner and pool service every two weeks or so in season. A keypad lock that lets you issue a code for a set window and cancel it from the city solves the lockbox problem. Two things to plan for: the house's Wi-Fi often gets switched off or replaced over winter, so locks can drop offline; and someone should test every code on every door before the first tenant arrives.

Frequently asked questions

Will a keypad deadbolt fit behind my storm door?

Often, but measure first. The exterior keypad sticks out further than a plain deadbolt, and a storm door that closes tight against the entry door can hit it. Check the gap with the storm door shut; if it is too tight, a slimmer keypad, a retrofit lock that keeps your outside cylinder, or adjusting the storm door closer usually solves it.

My new door's deadbolt is so stiff the smart lock stalls. Is the lock defective?

Usually not. A smart lock motor has far less force than a hand on a key, so a bolt that rubs in the strike plate will stall it. The fix is on the door: re-align or file the strike, deepen the bolt hole, or shim the hinges so the bolt throws freely by hand before the lock goes back on.

What happens to a smart lock when the power goes out?

Residential smart deadbolts run on batteries, so a grid outage does not lock you out. What you lose is Wi-Fi or hub control until the router comes back. Keep a mechanical key or override for dead batteries, and swap batteries every set number of months instead of relying on the low-battery alert.

Does the installer need a Nassau or Suffolk licence?

Ask. Home-improvement work is licensed at the county level here: Nassau handles it through its Office of Consumer Affairs and Suffolk through its Department of Labor, Licensing and Consumer Affairs. A licensed installer should also be able to issue a certificate of insurance if an HOA or landlord asks for one.

Wi-Fi, Z-Wave or Matter: which should I pick?

Let the system you already have decide. If your alarm panel or hub speaks Z-Wave, a Z-Wave lock can arm and disarm with it. Wi-Fi locks need no hub but use batteries faster. Matter-over-Thread locks need a border router in the house. Buying the lock first and the hub second is how people end up with two apps.
