A recurring thread on the Reolink subreddit asks whether WiFi cameras can record to an NVR. Many can, but a camera that keeps dropping off WiFi will leave gaps in the recording no matter where the video ends up. In masonry buildings, the wired PoE option is usually the one that holds up long term.

Test the signal where the camera will sit

Before buying WiFi or battery models, take your phone to the exact mounting spot, connect to the network the camera will use, and run a speed test. If the result is weak or the connection drops, a camera there will struggle too. Moving the router or buying a bigger one rarely fixes a signal blocked by two brick walls. Better options are a wired access point closer to the camera, or switching that location to a PoE camera.

Work out who has to approve it

Permission is the step people most often skip, and the one that gets cameras taken down. Use this ladder:

  1. Inside your own apartment. Generally your call, though a lease may restrict drilling. Battery and WiFi models are the usual choice.
  2. Your apartment door or a shared hallway. The hallway belongs to the landlord or the co-op or condo board. Ask in writing before mounting anything.
  3. Lobby, mailroom, cellar or rear yard of a building you own or manage. Your decision as owner, but tell residents ahead of time and post notices; it avoids conflicts.
  4. The street facade of a building in a historic district. The Landmarks Preservation Commission has rules for cameras on designated buildings. Placement at the rear, inside a vestibule or in an areaway, small and finished to match, is the usual route.

Whatever the location, aim cameras at your own property rather than into a neighbor's window, and be careful with audio recording. For legal questions specific to your property, talk to an attorney.

Keep the Reolink app connected

"My NVR and four PoE cameras won't stay connected to the app" is one of the most common complaints in Reolink forums. In the city a few causes come up again and again:

  • Your internet plan does not allow inbound connections. Some providers and many 5G home internet plans share public addresses between customers. Reolink's own UID/P2P connection usually works where manual port forwarding cannot; a VPN to the building is another route.
  • The recorder is on a different network segment than you think. A second router, a mesh system or a building-provided network can isolate it.
  • Power blips. When the lights flicker the router and recorder reboot, and sometimes one comes back without the other. A small battery backup on both keeps local recording going and lets the app reconnect.
  • Bandwidth. Viewing several 4K streams remotely over a slow upload connection stutters. Switching the app to the sub-stream for live viewing helps a lot.

Remember that local recording keeps working even when remote viewing does not. Check that the recorder is recording, not just that the app shows live video.

A typical install day in a walk-up

Picture a small mixed-use building with a store on the ground floor and apartments above. The owner wants the front door, the mailbox area, the cellar entrance and the rear yard covered. The recorder goes in a locked spot in the cellar near the electrical service, with a battery backup. Cable for the rear and cellar cameras runs along the cellar ceiling. The front door camera is fed up through an existing chase rather than across the facade. The hallway camera is a small dome painted to match. At the end, the owner and the super each get their own app login, and a test clip is exported together so both know how to pull video when a tenant asks.

Frequently asked questions

Will a Reolink WiFi camera work through brick walls?

Often not reliably. Pre-war masonry and plaster absorb wireless signal, and every extra wall in the way weakens it more. Test the signal at the exact mounting spot first; if it is weak, use a wired access point nearby or a PoE camera.

Can a WiFi Reolink camera record to a Reolink NVR?

Many Reolink WiFi models can be added to a Reolink NVR, but a camera that drops off WiFi will still leave gaps. For continuous recording in a masonry building, wired PoE cameras are more dependable.

Why does my Reolink NVR keep disconnecting from the app?

Common causes are an internet plan that blocks inbound connections, the recorder sitting behind a second router or mesh network, power blips that reboot equipment out of sync, and slow upload speed. Reolink's UID connection, a battery backup and viewing the sub-stream usually help.

As a renter, am I allowed to mount a Reolink camera at my apartment door?

The hallway generally belongs to the landlord or board, so ask in writing first. A battery camera or doorbell that needs no drilling is the usual renter choice once permission is given.

Can I install Reolink cameras on a landmarked brownstone?

Usually yes, but the Landmarks Preservation Commission has rules for cameras on designated buildings. Rear walls, vestibules and areaways, with small cameras finished to match, are the common approach rather than the main facade.

Does Reolink need a monthly subscription?

Not for local recording. Cameras can record to a microSD card or a Reolink NVR, and the app works without a plan. Reolink's cloud storage is optional.
