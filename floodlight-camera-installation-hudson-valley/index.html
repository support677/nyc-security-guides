Floodlight Camera Installation Hudson Valley is mostly a question of behaviour after dark. Mounting the fixture takes an hour or two; deciding what should switch the light on, how far the beam should reach and what the unit does after a long outage takes longer, and it is what separates a light the household is glad of from one that blazes at every deer and annoys the neighbours across the road. In the valley, where nights are genuinely dark and wildlife is constant, those settings matter more than in a city. Decide what should turn the light on

Most floodlight cameras let the light and the recording be triggered separately. That is the setting to get right first.

What moves in front of itLightCamera
A person walking to the door or garageOn, full brightnessRecord and send an alert
A car pulling into your own driveOn, so you can see to parkRecord; alert optional
Deer crossing the lawnOff or dimRecord on infrared, no alert
A bear at the trash cansOn briefly, which often moves it alongRecord and alert, so you know before you step outside
Cars passing on the roadOff; mask the road out of the motion zoneIgnore
Branches moving in windOffIgnore; tighten the zone or use person detection

Person and vehicle detection does most of this work on newer models. On older ones, it comes down to careful motion zones and a sensitivity setting tested on a windy night, not a calm afternoon.

Keep the beam inside your own lot

Several valley municipalities already regulate glare. The Village of New Paltz's outdoor lighting standards, for example, require lighting to be shielded so it does not cast direct glare beyond the property. Rules vary from town to town, and some proposed local laws have stalled, such as one in the Town of Ulster in 2023, so check your own code before a fixture goes up.

At state level, Assembly bill A4615, the proposed dark sky law, would require outdoor fixtures on residential, commercial and municipal buildings to be shielded after January 1, 2028. It was still in committee at last check, but it is a reason to choose a unit whose lamp heads tilt well down, or a separate shielded fixture with a camera beside it, rather than a model that can only point at the horizon.

In practice: mount high enough that the heads aim down at the ground you want lit, angle them so the bright spot stops short of the property line, and walk to the road at night to see what a neighbour sees.

Color at night or infrared?

When the light comes on, the camera records in color, which is the main reason to buy a floodlight camera at all: clothing, a car's paint and a face are all easier to make out. When the light stays off, it falls back to black-and-white infrared. On a big rural lot the sensible split is usually color near the house and the drive, where people actually approach, and infrared for animal traffic on the lawn and the field edge, so the light is not cycling all night. Infrared also reaches less far than people expect, so a lamp out by the end of a long drive should not be relied on to identify anyone at the road.

After the power comes back

The valley is served by several utilities, among them Central Hudson, NYSEG, Orange & Rockland and Con Edison in Westchester, and long storm outages are part of life on wooded lots. A floodlight camera runs on house power, so it goes dark with everything else. What matters is what happens when the power returns.

  • Check that the light is not stuck on or off; some units reset to factory light schedules.
  • Open the app and confirm the camera rejoined Wi-Fi. If the router came back slower, the camera may have given up.
  • Look at the clock on the recordings. A wrong timestamp makes footage hard to use later.
  • If you have a generator, confirm the outdoor circuit is one of the ones it feeds.

A small battery backup on the router, and a camera that records to its own card, mean the system recovers on its own more often than not.

Frequently asked questions

How do I stop my floodlight camera from turning on for deer?

Set the light and the recording to trigger separately, use person detection if the model has it, and draw the motion zone around the door and drive rather than the open lawn. Animals can still be recorded on infrared without the light firing.

Does New York require shielded outdoor lights?

Some villages and towns already require shielding against glare, such as the Village of New Paltz. A4615, a state dark sky bill, would make shielding mandatory from 2028 on, though it had not left committee.

Is color night vision better than infrared on a floodlight camera?

Color recording with the light on makes people and vehicles easier to identify. Infrared is better for routine animal traffic because the light does not have to cycle all night.

Will my floodlight camera come back on after a power outage?

Usually, but check that it rejoined Wi-Fi, that the light schedule did not reset and that the recording clock is correct. A router on battery backup helps it reconnect.
