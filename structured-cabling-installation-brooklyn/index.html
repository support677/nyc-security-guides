Structured Cabling Installation Brooklyn is easiest to get right when it is planned around your move-in date instead of squeezed in the week before. Brooklyn businesses move into everything from Downtown office floors to Navy Yard and Industry City buildings that were never designed for networks, and the cabling schedule has to fit the landlord, the general contractor and the internet provider. This guide lays out a timeline, then covers the decisions that come up along the way. Abstract Enterprises Security Systems lists the service, cable types and project ranges on its Structured Cabling Installation Brooklyn page.

From signed lease to first login

  1. Right after the lease is signed. Order the internet circuit. Provider installs in commercial buildings often take weeks, and the circuit has to land where your network equipment will live.
  2. Before the floor plan is final. Walk the space with the cabling contractor. Mark every desk, printer, access point, camera, door reader and display. Decide where the rack goes.
  3. While the walls are open. Pull cable through open studs and set boxes. This is the cheapest moment in the whole project; the same drop fished through finished walls later can cost several times as much.
  4. After paint. Terminate, test and label every run, mount the patch panel and dress the rack.
  5. Move-in week. Patch cords, switch and access points go live, and phones and cameras get power over the same cables.

On the Abstract Enterprises Security Systems page, a small office typically takes one to two days of cabling work, an office in the 15-to-50-drop range two to five days, and larger commercial jobs one to three weeks.

What the last tenant left above the ceiling

Most Brooklyn commercial spaces come with someone else's cable. Some of it may be good; much of it is unlabeled, mixed categories and cut short. The useful step is to test and tone what is there before deciding. Runs that pass and can be identified can stay and save money. Everything else should come out, because dead cable fills the pathway, confuses the next technician and is generally expected to be removed under the electrical code once it is abandoned. Removal is usually quoted as its own line.

Size the rack for the office you will be in two years from now

A small Brooklyn office often fits in a wall-mounted cabinet; a larger one needs a floor rack. Either way, plan the space before buying:

  • Patch panels for every drop plus spare ports.
  • A switch with enough powered (PoE) ports for cameras, phones and access points.
  • The internet provider's equipment and your firewall.
  • A battery backup (UPS) so a short outage does not reboot everything.
  • Room to add a second switch without rebuilding the cabinet.

The location needs power, some airflow and a lockable door. A closet that gets hot in July shortens the life of everything inside it.

Floors, wings and buildings: the backbone

Single-floor offices need only horizontal cable. Once a company spreads across two floors of a Downtown building, or across two buildings at the Navy Yard or Industry City, the floors need to be linked. Fiber is the usual answer between closets: it is not affected by electrical noise and it covers distances copper cannot. In multi-tenant buildings the riser belongs to the landlord, so the route, sleeves and fire-stopping between floors need building management's approval before anyone drills.

Signs a cable plant will age well

  • Every jack and panel port carries the same label, and a printed map matches them.
  • Test results exist for every run and are handed over at the end.
  • Plenum-rated cable wherever the space above the ceiling is used for return air.
  • Data cable kept apart from power and from motor loads on working industrial floors.
  • Spare drops at the spots most likely to change, such as conference rooms and reception.

Frequently asked questions

Should we keep the cabling the previous tenant left?

Only the runs that test well and can be identified. Test and tone everything first; unlabeled, failing or mixed-category cable usually costs more to live with than to replace.

When should the cabling contractor start on a fit-out?

Before the floor plan is final and before the walls close. Pulling cable through open studs is far cheaper than fishing it through finished walls later.

Do we need plenum-rated cable in a Brooklyn office?

Yes, wherever the space above the ceiling doubles as the air return. In converted Brooklyn buildings that is not always obvious, so it gets confirmed on the walkthrough.

How long does structured cabling take for a small office?

Per the Abstract Enterprises Security Systems page, a 5-to-15-drop office is usually wired in a day or two; an office with 15 to 50 drops needs two to five.

How do we connect two floors or two buildings?

Usually with fiber between network closets, routed through the building riser with the landlord's approval and fire-stopping at each floor.

Can cameras and door readers use the same cabling?

Yes. IP cameras, door readers, phones and access points all run on the same structured cable and can draw power from a PoE switch, so one crew can install all of it.
