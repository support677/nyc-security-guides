Burglar Alarm Installation NYC: Audit Every Entry Point, Then Choose the Panel, Sensors and Monitoring

By Abstract Enterprises Security Systems — licensed NYS low-voltage contractor, in security since 2000 · Updated 2026-11-08

Burglar Alarm Installation NYC quotes can look wildly different for the same apartment or storefront, and the gap almost always comes down to coverage: which doors, windows and hatches end up on the system and which get skipped. An alarm with a gap is mostly a noisemaker. Before comparing panels or monthly fees, list every way into the property. Then match sensors to that list, decide on monitoring, and make sure the siren follows city noise rules. Published prices and booking are on the Burglar Alarm Installation NYC page run by Abstract Enterprises Security Systems (AESS).

Step one: the entry-point audit

Walk the property from the outside in, the way someone looking for an easy way inside would. These are the openings people most often forget, by building type:

Building typeEasy-to-miss openings
Brownstone or rowhouseGarden-level door under the stoop, rear parlor windows over the yard, roof hatch, skylights
Upper-floor apartmentFire-escape windows, any window next to a roof setback, the apartment door itself
Garden or ground-floor unitRear yard door, window wells, a storage door to the cellar
Detached house (Staten Island, eastern Queens, the north Bronx)Basement windows, the door from an attached garage, sliding patio doors
StorefrontRear delivery door, sidewalk cellar hatch, roof access, a basement wall shared with the next shop

Match a sensor to each opening

  • Door and window contacts trip when an opening is moved. They are the backbone of the system.
  • Motion detectors cover rooms and hallways an intruder has to cross, catching anyone who got past the perimeter.
  • Glass-break sensors listen for shattering glass, useful for storefront windows and big panes you cannot contact individually.
  • Sirens, indoor and optionally outdoor, make the alarm hard to ignore.
  • A cellular communicator keeps the panel reporting if someone cuts the internet line or the power drops.

NYC’s 15-minute rule for building sirens

The city’s noise code (Administrative Code §24-238) says an audible burglar alarm on a building must be able to shut itself off automatically within fifteen minutes of going off. The city’s penalty schedule starts at $220 for a first violation. In a building where a dozen neighbors share your wall, a siren that wails until you get home is also a quick way to lose goodwill. Ask the installer to confirm the bell cutoff time in the panel settings and show it to you.

Outside the city, rules shift. AESS points out that certain towns on Long Island and in Westchester want monitored alarms registered or permitted, and many suburban towns bill for repeat false alarms. If you own property in both places, do not assume one set of rules covers both.

Keep the old wiring, or go wireless?

Plenty of older city houses still have contacts and cable left from an alarm installed decades ago. If those sensors test good, swapping only the panel and communicator is one of the most cost-effective upgrades around; AESS offers a $250 diagnostic visit that is credited toward the work. Panels that dial out over a landline are worth replacing regardless, since many homes no longer keep one. Where there is no usable wiring, wireless sensors avoid opening plaster walls and crown molding.

Questions to ask any alarm installer

  • Will you walk every floor, including the cellar and roof?
  • Which openings are you leaving off, and why?
  • Is the communicator cellular, and how long does the backup battery last?
  • Is the siren set to cut off within 15 minutes?
  • Is monitoring optional, and is there a contract term?
  • Do I own the equipment if I stop monitoring?
  • How do I give a dog walker, cleaner or employee their own code?

Frequently asked questions

What does burglar alarm installation cost in New York City?

AESS publishes wireless home systems from $600 installed, larger or hardwired homes typically $2,000–$6,000, and small-business systems from $1,500. Monitoring is optional at $19.99 monthly, and you are not locked into a multi-year term.

Do I need professional monitoring, or is a siren enough?

For many apartments a loud siren plus instant phone alerts handles most situations. Monitoring adds someone who reacts when you cannot, which matters more for stores, empty properties and frequent travelers.

How long can a burglar alarm siren ring in NYC?

Under the city’s noise code, an audible burglar alarm on a building must shut off automatically within 15 minutes of being triggered. Fines start at $220 for a first violation.

Can I keep my old alarm sensors and just replace the panel?

Often. If the existing wired contacts and motion sensors still test good, replacing only the panel and communicator saves money. AESS charges $250 for a diagnostic visit, credited toward the job.

Does an alarm system need a permit?

According to AESS, registration or permit rules mostly apply in towns outside the city, especially on Long Island and in Westchester, and mainly to monitored systems. Inside the five boroughs, the rule owners usually run into is the 15-minute siren limit. Ask your installer about your specific address.

Will the alarm still work if the internet or power goes out?

Yes, if it has a cellular communicator and a backup battery in the panel. Ask how many hours the battery is rated for.
