Capture Camera Installation Brooklyn quotes usually hinge on one decision that comes before any model number: are you keeping the cable already in the building, or replacing it? Capture Advance is ADI's own-label line of professional cameras (ADI being the big security distributor), and it comes in two families, one that runs over coax and one that runs over network cable. Brooklyn has plenty of both kinds of building. This guide walks through that choice, the four-camera prices on the published list, which camera fits which spot, and what to settle before you sign. Full details sit on the Capture Camera Installation Brooklyn page.

What the word "Capture" on a quote means

If an installer hands you a list of part numbers starting with R2-, you are looking at Capture Advance: domes, turrets, bullets and eyeball cameras, coax DVRs, network NVRs and boxed kits. ADI markets the range as NDAA compliant, which some commercial tenants and insurers ask about. Owners view and play back footage in the Capture ADV phone app or a PC client, and spec sheets and manuals are published on capture-adv.com. It is not sold off the shelf at big-box stores; it comes through security installers.

Look inside the walls first

Many Brooklyn walk-ups and older shops still carry the coax from an analog system put in years ago: a run to each landing, the vestibule, the yard, or stapled across a store ceiling. If those runs test clean, the HD family swaps the cameras and the recorder and leaves the plaster alone. Where cable is being pulled anyway, or the job needs analytics and more resolution, the IP family over Cat6 is the better long-term build.

Which camera for which Brooklyn spot

  • Stoop and vestibule: a turret, which has no dome bubble to glare at night. On coax the 5MP HD turret is the usual pick; on Cat6 the R2-5MPFXTUR, listed at $359 installed.
  • Shop ceilings and lobby ceilings: a dome, which is harder to knock out of aim.
  • Side alleys and driveways: a bullet looking down the long, narrow space.
  • Long rear yards and deep stores: a motorized zoom model, so a face at the far end is still big enough to recognize.
  • Behind vestibule glass, or at a gate lit by a streetlamp: the 4MP full-color turret, which keeps color with a white light instead of infrared that bounces off the glass.

How many days should the drive hold?

Every listed package is sized to keep two weeks or more of round-the-clock footage, which is why some four-camera packages carry a larger drive than the recorder ships with. Higher resolution fills a drive faster, so an 8MP upgrade can mean a bigger drive. A good setup records continuously in the background and marks motion or person events on the timeline, so when a package disappears you jump to the moments someone was on the stoop instead of scrubbing hours of empty steps. To check the days for your own camera count, use the security camera storage calculator.

Co-ops, rentals and multi-unit buildings

In a co-op, the managing agent usually asks for an insurance certificate that lists the co-op corporation, and the board may want to approve each camera location. A written list of locations with a simple drawing gets that approval faster. In a building with several apartments, a lobby camera will not stop a determined package thief, but it captures who it was, when, and which way they left, and it settles whether the box was ever delivered.

Five things to settle before you sign

  1. Every camera and recorder named by model number, so you can check each line against the price list.
  2. For a coax job, each existing run tested before it is reused, and what happens if one fails.
  3. The number of days the drive holds at your actual resolution.
  4. Who keeps the admin password: it should be you, written down and stored with the building papers.
  5. A small battery backup under the recorder and router. It is not part of the listed packages, so ask for it if you want brief outages covered.

On warranty, ADI does not publish one length for the whole line; the data sheet for the R2-5MPFXTUR turret lists 36 months, so ask for the figure model by model. For an inherited or different-brand system, the Brooklyn AVYCON guide covers taking over a recorder you cannot log into.

Frequently asked questions

Is Capture a real camera brand?

Yes. Capture Advance is the professional line the security distributor ADI sells under its own name. It is sold through installers rather than retail stores, and ADI markets it as NDAA compliant.

Can Capture cameras reuse the coax from my old camera system?

Yes, if the runs test clean. The HD family of Capture cameras and DVRs works over existing coax, which keeps finished walls closed in many Brooklyn walk-ups and shops.

What app do I use to watch Capture cameras?

The Capture ADV phone app, where the recorder is added for live view and playback. ADI also offers a PC client.

How much does a four-camera Capture system cost in Brooklyn?

On the published Brooklyn list, four-camera packages run from $1,334 for HD 2MP turrets to $2,138 for IP 5MP turrets or domes, plus sales tax. The price covers the recorder, the drive, mounting, cable runs of up to 100 feet each and app setup.

How long will a Capture recorder keep footage?

The listed packages are sized for two weeks or more of round-the-clock footage. Once the drive is full the oldest video is overwritten, so match the drive to the number of days you need.

Does the package price include a battery backup?

No. A battery backup for the recorder and router is not part of the listed packages, but it can be added to the plan.
