One Island, four kinds of rental property

Rentals on Long Island are scattered among single-family blocks rather than stacked block after block, and most alarm companies here sell to homeowners. A multifamily property needs something different: doors that close and latch for dozens of people, credentials that can be cancelled when someone moves, and a way to see what happened at a shared entrance. Paperwork that changes at the county line

The New York City intercom duty that most online articles quote, Multiple Dwelling Law §50-a, applies only where a city has at least 325,000 residents, so Long Island buildings answer to the Multiple Residence Law, and the day-to-day lock rules come from the state's Property Maintenance Code. Its §304.18 calls for security devices on unit doors, windows and hatchways, deadbolts that throw at least one inch and open from inside without a key, and sash locks on ground-level windows that are rented out. Your town, village or city enforces it.

  • Burglar alarm permits. Nassau County Police charge $100 (residential) or $200 (business) for a two-year permit. Inside the Suffolk County Police District, registration is $50 or $100, and a registered location gets two false alarms before service fees start. Shelter Island, Southold, Riverhead, East Hampton and Southampton sit outside that program, so East End owners check with the town police.
  • Rental permits. Islip, Brookhaven and Babylon all require one. In Nassau it depends on the town or village.
  • Rent stabilization. State housing regulators list sixteen Nassau localities under ETPA, covering buildings with at least six apartments that went up before 1974. No Suffolk locality is on the list. If your building is covered, talk to counsel before assuming an entry upgrade can be recovered through rents.
  • Fire alarm approvals. The Nassau County Fire Marshal in Nassau; the town or village fire marshal in Suffolk. Fire alarm work is quoted per building after a survey.

The gap we find most often is not a missing device but a monitoring account and alarm permit still registered to a manager who left years ago.

Planning for the outage, not the sunny day

When a July 2026 storm brought 84 mph gusts, PSEG Long Island still had more than four thousand customers dark the next morning. A building system that only works on utility power gives you an unlocked or unanswerable front door at exactly the wrong time. Ask any installer how each piece behaves when power is cut:

  • Magnetic locks release when they lose power. Many egress doors require that, so they need a battery sized for hours.
  • Electric strikes are usually fail-secure, so the door stays locked with a key override, but the entry panel and buzzer go silent unless the power supply has backup.
  • Recorders, switches and routers need a UPS.
  • Alarm communicators should report over cellular, so the signal still gets out when cable internet is down.

In South Shore buildings from Long Beach to Freeport, keep the equipment shelf well above the cellar floor. Batteries should carry an install date on the label and be checked every year before hurricane season.

Frequently asked questions

Does the New York City intercom law apply to an apartment building in Nassau or Suffolk?

No. The city rule in Multiple Dwelling Law §50-a stops at cities with 325,000-plus residents; every Long Island town, village and city is under the Multiple Residence Law instead. The state property maintenance code still requires security devices on unit doors, windows and hatchways, and the warranty of habitability still applies to entrances and halls.

Can a landlord put cameras in the halls and parking lot of a Long Island rental?

Common areas such as entrances, halls, lots and mail areas are generally fair game, but cameras should never look into apartments or be placed where people expect privacy, such as bathrooms. Cameras that record sound raise a separate eavesdropping issue. Confirm the specifics with your attorney.

We own buildings in both counties. Does each one need its own alarm permit?

Yes. Nassau County Police permit each location at $100 for a residence or $200 for a business for two years, and the Suffolk County Police District registers locations at $50 or $100. Buildings in East End towns outside that district register with the town instead.

Delivery drivers leave packages outside our front door. What actually fixes that?

Give carriers a way in that you control: a scheduled delivery code on the entry panel or a reader on a package room, plus a camera aimed at the spot where packages change hands. Locking drivers out without an alternative usually just moves the packages to the sidewalk.

What does a repair visit cost?

You pay a $250 service call for the visit, the testing and any simple fix. If the fault needs more, a written price comes before any work, and the $250 comes off it.
