Put the portfolio on a map before you compare hardware

Intercom brands and camera resolutions matter less than three facts about each address. Mark them on a map of the portfolio first, because they decide what kind of system can work there.

  • Distance from the office. A building fifteen minutes away can live with a controller that staff update in person. A complex two counties north should be managed from a laptop, so a fob is cancelled the same afternoon a tenant hands back keys.
  • Who is on site. A resident manager can read a panel light or reset a power supply while a technician listens on the phone. A townhouse cluster with nobody on site needs equipment that reports its own failures, or a dark camera will go unnoticed until someone needs the footage.
  • Internet and cell signal. River-city buildings usually have a cable line in the basement. Rural rentals in Ulster or northern Dutchess may have one slow connection and patchy cell service, which rules out some cloud intercoms and points cameras toward recording on site.

Set a repair ceiling so doors don't wait for meetings

A broken release that waits two weeks for an owner's reply leaves a door propped open for two weeks. A Hudson Valley maintenance calendar

  • January and February. Hard cold stiffens closers and shrinks latches on garden-building doors, so doors stop catching. Plow banks bury low entrance cameras, and plows clip call boxes on posts. Flag post-mounted panels with reflective stakes before the first storm.
  • March and April. Thaw water gets into conduit and buried splices. Check the cameras and readers farthest from the office once the ground softens.
  • May. Leaves fill in, and a wireless link between buildings that ran clean all winter starts dropping video. Book a link check after leaf-out, not before.
  • June through August. Thunderstorm season. Long outdoor camera runs need surge protection at both ends, and recorders in rooms without air conditioning run hot. Utilities across the region reported large storm outages in 2026, so test battery backups in June.
  • September through November. Close out pool and amenity schedules on the fob system, clear credentials left over from summer turnover, and get ahead of nor'easter season.

Questions to put in your request for proposals

  1. Will you test the existing cable before recommending new wire?
  2. Is each property priced on its own line so the owner can approve one address at a time?
  3. At handover, whose name is on every account, app and password?
  4. Are equipment, labor and any platform subscription listed separately?
  5. How do you work with our elevator contractor and our electrician? (Abstract Enterprises Security Systems handles the low-voltage side and coordinates the rest.)

Scenario: one contract, two very different properties

A management firm picks up a 1960s elevator rental near a Metro-North station in lower Westchester and a Rockland garden complex of ten two-story buildings in the same month. At the elevator building nothing is broken, but the cloud entry panel and the camera app are both registered to the outgoing agent, so the first visit moves those accounts to the owner and changes every password. At the garden complex the office has been told "the intercom is down." Testing shows three of the ten vestibule panels failing and a shared transformer that has overheated; both fit under the manager's repair limit and are fixed on the first visit. Fob readers for the laundry building go into next year's budget as their own line, with a survey price attached.

Frequently asked questions

Is a service call in Ulster County priced differently from one in Westchester?

No. The published service call is $250 anywhere across the six counties, from Westchester north to Ulster. It covers diagnosis and simple repairs, and anything larger is priced before the work starts.

Does the New York City intercom rule apply to our Hudson Valley apartment buildings?

The rule usually quoted sits in the Multiple Dwelling Law, and that law only reaches cities with at least 325,000 residents. Hudson Valley cities, towns and villages are under the Multiple Residence Law instead, enforced by the local building department. Have your counsel confirm what applies to a specific building.

Can one proposal cover properties in several counties?

Yes. A single proposal can list every address, with each property priced on its own line so the owner can approve them one at a time.

How do you get a camera to a parking lot with no power nearby?

The usual options are a pole mount fed from a nearby building, a wireless link to the closest power, or a solar or cellular camera where cable cannot reach. The right one depends on distance, tree cover and how the footage needs to be viewed.

Can the pool gate and clubhouse fobs follow a summer schedule?

Yes. Fob and reader systems can hold schedules, so a pool gate or clubhouse door can be set to open for residents only during the season and stay locked the rest of the year.
