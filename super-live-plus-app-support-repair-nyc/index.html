The ten-minute self-check

Do these in order. Each one narrows the fault to a layer.

  1. Look at the recorder's own monitor. If every camera shows a live picture there, the cameras and cables are fine — the problem is the connection or the login. If some channels are black on the monitor too, that is a camera, cable or power fault and no app fix will help.
  2. Test on cellular, then on the shop or home Wi-Fi. Works on Wi-Fi only? The phone is reaching the recorder locally and the remote route is broken or was never set up.
  3. Check how the device was added. In the app's device list, edit the device. A serial number or MAC-style ID means it was added by QR code. An IP address or domain name with a colon and port number means it was added by address.
  4. Think about what changed. New internet provider, new router, a switch to 5G home internet, a power outage, a staff member leaving — the date the app went grey usually lines up with one of these.
  5. Count your remaining password attempts. If you are guessing at an admin password, stop before the recorder locks the account.

Reinstalling the app, the advice most forums give first, almost never fixes an offline recorder, because the app was not the part that broke.

Situation 1: the internet changed underneath the recorder

Many older city systems were set up by address: the phone dials the shop's public IP, and the router forwards a port to the recorder. That depends on the router having its own public address. Swap a cable line for a 5G home gateway and the router now sits behind the carrier's shared address, so nothing from outside can reach the forwarded port. The same is true in many buildings on bulk or program internet, including NYCHA homes on Big Apple Connect, where the resident does not control the provider's network.

The cure is to stop relying on incoming connections. When the recorder supports it and has NAT turned on in its network settings, phones can find it by QR through the maker's peer-to-peer relay, which only needs the recorder to reach out. The clean-up step people forget: delete the old port forward on the router. An open port on an old recorder is a liability, not a convenience.

Situation 2: nobody knows who holds the keys

Super Live Plus logs in with users stored on the recorder itself. That is good news when an installer has gone out of business: if anyone still knows an admin password, a new technician can log in, create fresh users and change the admin password without wiping footage. It is bad news when a roommate, ex-employee or former tenant had the shared login — removing the recorder from your own app leaves theirs untouched. Access has to be removed at the recorder.

Be wary of third-party "super password" generator apps that promise a reset code from a serial number. You are handing your recorder's details to an unknown publisher. The safer route is the maker's own recovery process, carried out by a licensed tech after you show you own or manage the property and after footage is exported.

Check the firmware before you celebrate

Versions of TVT's NVMS-9000 firmware older than 1.3.4 carry a published flaw, CVE-2024-14007, that lets someone who can reach the control port read admin credentials; it was targeted by a Mirai botnet variant in 2025. If your recorder ever had ports forwarded to it, read its firmware version and review the user list for accounts nobody recognizes. The published advice for an exposed unit is to export what you need, factory reset, update and keep it off the open internet.

An owner's handover sheet

Keep one page per recorder, whether it's one bodega or six buildings:

  • Recorder model, serial number and firmware version
  • Admin password, held by the owner or managing agent only
  • One named viewer user per person, and who has each
  • How phones connect (QR, address or VPN) and the internet provider and account
  • Where the recorder physically sits and who can unlock that room
  • How many days of footage the drive holds, and how far the recorder's clock drifts

With that sheet, cutting off a departing employee takes a minute, and a police or insurance footage request is a short job — export at the recorder to USB, note any clock offset, keep a second copy.

Fix it or replace it?

Keep the recorder when the pictures are good on its monitor, the drive is healthy and holds the days you need, and firmware can be brought current. Replace it when the drive is failing in an old box, the firmware can't be patched past a known flaw, or the unit was exposed and can't be trusted. Replacing a recorder rarely means new cameras; a hybrid recorder takes most existing coax cameras.

FAQ

Why does Super Live Plus say my device is offline when the cameras still work?

Because the phone can't reach the recorder over the internet. The recorder usually keeps recording; the usual causes are a new router or provider, a switch to 5G home internet, or NAT being turned off at the recorder.

Why does Super Live Plus work on Wi-Fi but not on cellular data?

Your phone only finds the recorder while it's on the same network. The remote route — QR peer-to-peer or a port forward — is broken or was never set up.

Will reinstalling the Super Live Plus app fix it?

Rarely. An offline device, a lockout or missing playback is a recorder, drive or network issue that a fresh app install doesn't change.

How do I remove a former roommate or employee from Super Live Plus?

Remove or disable their user on the recorder itself, and change the admin password if they had it. Removing the recorder from your own app leaves their access untouched.

I forgot the Super Live Plus recorder password. Can it be recovered?

Often, through the maker's or seller's documented recovery process or a contractor-led reset after you show you own or manage the property. Export any footage you need before a reset, and avoid third-party password generator apps.

Does Super Live Plus work on 5G home internet?

Usually, if the recorder is added by QR code with NAT enabled. Setups that rely on a public IP address and port forwarding stop working behind a 5G gateway.
