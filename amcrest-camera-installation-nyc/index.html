Amcrest Camera Installation NYC questions rarely start with megapixels. They start with "will these keep working?" Amcrest is popular with people who want local recording without a monthly fee, and its PoE cameras are good value, but owners who set them up themselves tend to hit the same three walls: cameras that vanish from the recorder, a doorbell that won't join the NVR, and confusion about the brand's Dahua connection. Add New York's brick, limestone and co-op boards, and a weekend project can turn into a month. Below is how to get those decisions right before mounting anything. Borough pricing lives on the Amcrest Camera Installation NYC page, published by Abstract Enterprises Security Systems.

Decision 1: where the video is stored

Storage options for Amcrest include an onboard microSD slot, FTP upload, NAS drives, a PC, its own NVRs, and Blue Iris software. In practice, city buildings come down to two choices:

Amcrest NVRBlue Iris on a PC
SetupOne box built for the job; PoE ports power the camerasWindows computer plus a separate PoE switch
Who looks after itA super or busy owner can live with itSomeone who will keep the PC on, patched and secured
FlexibilityFine for most layoutsMore control over rules and mixing brands
Best fitSmall co-ops, shops, two-family housesOwners who already run a home server

Either way, the recorder belongs somewhere locked and on battery backup, not on a shelf by the front door where a thief can take the evidence with them. Use the security camera storage calculator to size the drive for the number of days you want to keep.

Decision 2: the network, which is where most Amcrest trouble starts

On r/amcrest, the typical complaint is a camera that has power but the recorder can't find it, and the owner doesn't know its IP address. Blue Iris users describe cameras that worked for days, then disappeared. The cause is usually the same: the router handed out new addresses after a reboot, and the recorder kept looking for the old ones. Three habits prevent most of it:

  • Fixed addresses. Give every camera a static IP or a router reservation, and write each one down.
  • Tested cable. A run that "mostly works" causes freezes and choppy playback that look like a camera fault. Test before you blame the camera.
  • A camera segment. Keep cameras on their own network so only the recorder reaches the internet for remote viewing. That also answers the worry some owners raise about cameras contacting servers outside the home.

The doorbell is its own case. Owners on Reddit report Amcrest doorbells that are listed as ONVIF/RTSP compatible but still won't play properly on an NVR. Plan to bench-test the doorbell with the recorder before it goes on the door frame, and accept that it may be viewed in the app rather than on the recorder.

Decision 3: the brand question, in plain terms

Amcrest is often described as a US-distributed brand built on Dahua hardware; Amcrest says its products are designed in Houston. Dahua is on the FCC Covered List for uses tied to public safety, government buildings, critical infrastructure and national security. Here is what that means for different buyers (this is not legal advice):

  • Private home or privately funded building: no source we read says installed equipment has to come down or that private use is banned.
  • Building paid for with federal grants or loans: NDAA Section 889 governs federal purchasing, and Amcrest has been reported as not NDAA compliant. Ask the funding agency in writing before buying, and get an alternative brand quoted.
  • Everyone: July 2026 FCC action on Covered List equipment may make exact replacement models harder to find later. Record model numbers and firmware on day one, and use standard network cable so another brand can drop onto the same run if it ever comes to that.

Wiring Amcrest through New York walls

The cable is the expensive, long-lived part of the job, and it doesn't care what logo is on the camera. In a brownstone the route usually runs from the cellar up to the parlor floor and out to the yard. In a walk-up it climbs the stair hall landing by landing. In houses in Queens or Staten Island, the soffit over a driveway is often the easiest mount. Brick and stone need anchors made for the material, every exterior penetration gets sealed, and in a landmarked district the front facade comes last, after the vestibule, the rear and the sides have been considered.

Time on site depends more on the walls than on the camera count. A four-camera system with a clear cellar or attic route is commonly a one-day job; long masonry drilling, high facades or a co-op that allows work only during set hours can spread it over two visits.

Already bought the cameras?

That's common, and it's fine. Before booking anyone, check three things: that every camera works with the same recorder or app, that the recorder has enough PoE ports for all of them, and that you have photos of each mounting spot and the recorder location. Equipment you buy yourself is covered by the manufacturer's warranty, not the installer's, so keep the receipts. Amcrest Camera Installation NYC: FAQs

Do Amcrest cameras need a cloud subscription?

No. They can record locally to an Amcrest NVR, a network drive, a computer running Blue Iris or a microSD card. A cloud plan is optional, useful mainly as an off-site copy in case the recorder is stolen or damaged.

Why do my Amcrest cameras disappear from the NVR?

Most often the router assigned them new IP addresses after a reboot, so the recorder is looking in the wrong place. Give each camera a fixed address or reservation. Damaged or weak cable runs are the next most common cause.

Is it legal to use Amcrest cameras at my NYC home or building?

For private homes and privately funded buildings, the sources we reviewed do not say installed equipment must be removed or that private use is banned. Buildings using federal grant or loan money should ask the funding agency first.

Should I use an Amcrest NVR or Blue Iris?

An NVR is simpler and easier for a super or busy owner to live with. Blue Iris gives more control but needs a Windows PC that stays on and updated. Small buildings and shops usually do better with the NVR.

How long does an Amcrest installation take?

A four-camera system with an easy cable route through a cellar or attic is often done in a day. Masonry drilling, long stair-hall runs, landmark finishing or restricted building work hours can stretch it to two visits.
