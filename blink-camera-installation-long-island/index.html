Blink camera installation Long Island jobs rarely fail because of the camera itself. They fail because of distance, walls and power: a Sync Module stuck in a basement office, a garage at the back of a deep Suffolk lot, an original 1950s transformer too weak for a modern doorbell, or a storm that takes the router down while the batteries are still full. This guide is about planning around those four things on a Nassau or Suffolk property before anything is screwed to the siding. For what we offer and how to book, see Blink Camera Installation Long Island.

Draw the property as a signal map, not a camera list

Most people start with "I want four cameras." A better starting point is a rough sketch of the lot with the router marked on it. Blink's battery outdoor cameras and its battery doorbell do not talk to your Wi-Fi directly; they report to a Sync Module, a small plug-in hub that relays them to the router. Blink rates that link at up to 100 feet, but owners on the Blink forums describe the practical range as shorter once a block foundation, a brick veneer or a metal garage door sits in the path. On a sketch, three zones show up fast:

  • Easy zone — front porch, side door, the driveway beside the house. One Sync Module on the main floor usually covers these.
  • Stretch zone — a backyard fence line, a pool gate, an attached garage behind a laundry room. These work if the module moves up from the basement or closer to that side of the house.
  • Far zone — a detached garage, a shed or a boathouse 60 to 100-plus feet away. These need a plan of their own: a second Sync Module out there with Wi-Fi reaching it, a mesh Wi-Fi point, or a decision that the building deserves a wired camera instead.

Compact postwar lots in western Nassau rarely leave the first two zones. Bigger Suffolk and North Fork lots with outbuildings are where the far zone, and most offline complaints, show up.

The doorbell question on older Long Island houses

A lot of Nassau housing went up in the 1940s through the 1960s, and many of those houses still have their original doorbell button, chime and a small transformer tucked in the basement or by the panel. That matters because Blink's doorbells differ in how they use that wiring. According to Blink's own support pages, the Wired Doorbell 2K+ runs only on wired power, needs 10 to 24 volts AC, has no battery backup, and uses a bypass cable at the chime — so the old mechanical chime stops ringing and alerts come through the app, an Alexa device, a Blink Mini set up as a chime or a Blink Chime instead. Earlier Blink video doorbells work the other way: they run on AA lithium batteries even when the wires are connected.

So before choosing, we measure the transformer voltage and look at the chime. A weak or failing transformer is a common cause of a doorbell that reboots or drops off. Sometimes the right answer is a battery doorbell and leaving the old wiring alone; sometimes it is a transformer replacement so the wired model has steady power.

Storms, outages and what keeps recording

Battery cameras sound outage-proof, and the cameras are. The problem is everything they report to. ABC7's coverage of Tropical Storm Isaias in August 2020 put nearly half of PSEG Long Island's customers in the dark at its worst. During an outage of that size the Sync Module, the router and the modem all go dark, so the cameras have nowhere to send clips. Two practical fixes:

  • Put the modem, router and Sync Module on a small battery backup (UPS).
  • If you use a Sync Module 2 with a USB drive for local storage, check how your setup behaves when the internet is down so you know what to expect rather than finding out after a storm.

Battery life is the other weather issue. Hard January cold shortens any battery's life. Cameras that face a busy sidewalk or a street with constant headlights also burn through cells faster because they wake up more. Tightening the motion zones and sensitivity often does more for battery life than anything else.

What happens on install day

  1. Walk the lot with a phone. We check Wi-Fi strength at every planned spot and decide where the Sync Module lives — usually the main floor, central, away from the router's back side and metal appliances.
  2. Mount for the material. Vinyl siding, cedar shingle, brick and stucco each take different anchors. Where possible, cameras go under an eave or porch roof so rain is not hitting the lens.
  3. Set the app up with you. Every household phone is added, motion zones drawn around the driveway rather than the street, and cloud plan or local storage chosen.

Renters, two-families and permission

Long Island has many two-family houses and rented floors. If you rent, the landlord usually owns the siding and the doorframe, so ask before drilling; Blink's Minis and removable mounts let tenants cover their own entry without holes. On a shared driveway or a two-family porch, aim cameras at your own door and steps and keep neighbors' windows and yards out of frame. Camera rules are local here — permits, where needed, come from the town, village or city, not from New York City agencies — and New York's audio recording rules are worth reading before you turn on the doorbell microphone at a shared entrance.

When Blink is the wrong tool

Blink suits doors, porches and modest yards. For a store needing continuous recording, cameras that must ride out long outages, or an outbuilding only a cable can reach, a wired PoE system with a recorder costs more up front but ends the offline alerts and battery swaps. Frequently asked questions

The garage sits roughly 100 feet behind our house. Can a Blink camera on it keep its connection?

Often not reliably from a Sync Module inside the house. Blink rates the Sync Module link at up to 100 feet, and walls, brick and metal garage doors shorten it. The usual fixes are moving the module closer to that side of the house, putting a second Sync Module in the garage with Wi-Fi reaching it, or adding a mesh Wi-Fi point.

Does a wired Blink doorbell still need batteries?

It depends on the model. Blink says earlier video doorbells run on AA lithium batteries even when wired, while the Wired Doorbell 2K+ runs only on doorbell wiring at 10 to 24 volts AC and has no battery backup.

Will my old mechanical chime still ring with a Blink doorbell?

Not with the Wired Doorbell 2K+. Blink installs a bypass cable at the chime, so the mechanical chime stops working and alerts come through the app, an Alexa device, a Blink Mini set up as a chime, or a Blink Chime.

Do Blink cameras keep recording during a power outage?

The battery cameras keep their charge, but the Sync Module, router and modem need power. In a long outage they go dark and the cameras cannot send clips. A small battery backup on the network gear covers the first hours of a storm.

Why do my outdoor Blink camera batteries drain so fast?

The most common causes are a weak signal back to the Sync Module, a camera facing a busy street or sidewalk that wakes it constantly, and very cold weather. Tightening motion zones and sensitivity, and improving the signal, usually help most.

Can I put Blink cameras up if I rent a floor of a two-family house?

Ask the landlord before drilling into siding or doorframes. Blink Mini cameras and removable mounts let tenants cover their own entry without holes. Aim cameras at your own door and steps, not at neighbors.
