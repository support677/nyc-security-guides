AVYCON Camera Installation Hudson Valley work is mostly a distance problem. The recorder sits in the house; the things worth watching, a barn, a gate at the road, a pool house or a detached shop, are often hundreds of feet away across lawn, gravel or ledge rock. Getting a reliable signal and power to those spots, and keeping the system running when a storm takes the grid down, decides whether the cameras are still useful in year three. This guide covers those choices. Pricing for each county is on the AVYCON Camera Installation Hudson Valley page.

The 328-foot rule

An AVYCON IP camera gets both power and data over one network cable from the PoE ports on the recorder or a PoE switch. AVYCON's own support material says a camera plugged straight into the recorder's PoE port shows up on screen in about a minute. That simplicity has a limit: standard Ethernet is rated for 100 meters, roughly 328 feet, per run, including the patch cords at each end. Past that, the picture may freeze, the camera may reboot, or it may not power on at all.

On a suburban lot in Rockland or lower Westchester, every camera is usually well inside that limit. On a farm in Orange County or a long drive in Putnam, the gate camera can sit beyond it. Measure the actual route, not the straight line: cable follows walls, crosses the basement and climbs to the soffit before it ever leaves the house.

Any copper cable that runs between two buildings should have surge protection at both ends. A nearby lightning strike can travel down a long outdoor run and take out the recorder's PoE ports along with the camera.

Keeping the system up through an outage

Outages are a fact of life north of the city, from February ice to July downpours, and a dark night with no power is exactly when footage matters. A battery backup (UPS) should carry three things: the recorder, any PoE switches, and the networking gear. A unit sized for a few hours covers a typical storm; for multi-day outages, a generator circuit that feeds the camera equipment is the real answer. Remember that remote viewing in the AVYCON ONE app also depends on your internet service staying up, so the modem belongs on the backup too. Test it by switching off the breaker and watching the cameras keep recording.

Where a PTZ makes sense

AVYCON's NDAA-compliant line includes long-zoom pan-tilt-zoom models. A PTZ can sweep a pasture, a dock on the river or a long driveway and zoom in on a plate at the gate. The catch is that it only records what it is pointed at. So it works best alongside fixed cameras covering entrances and the driveway, with the PTZ assigned a patrol route or parked on whichever field or gate matters most.

Reusing cameras you already own

If you already have AVYCON cameras, or another brand that supports ONVIF, they can often go onto a new AVYCON recorder. Each one needs to be tested first: some cameras only join another maker's recorder after ONVIF is switched on and a user is set up in the camera's own settings, and smart features like person detection may not carry across brands. According to the website, gear you supply yourself can be fitted at labor-only rates, provided it works and is right for the location.

Checklist before anyone digs a trench

  • Call 811 so buried utilities are marked before excavation.
  • Walk the cable route and note driveways, septic fields and rock outcrops to avoid.
  • Decide whether the outbuilding has, or will get, a powered outlet.
  • Pick the recorder spot: dry, ventilated, near the router and on backup power.
  • Agree how many days of recording you need, then size the drive to match.
  • Confirm who will have the AVYCON ONE login, and write it down somewhere safe.

Frequently asked questions

How far can a PoE camera be from the AVYCON recorder?

Standard Ethernet is rated for 100 meters, about 328 feet, per run including patch cords. Beyond that, use a PoE extender, a fiber link to a switch in the far building, or a point-to-point wireless bridge.

Why can I not see my AVYCON cameras remotely?

Remote view in the AVYCON ONE app depends on the recorder being online and added to the app. Check that the recorder has internet, that it was added by its device ID or QR code, and that the router and modem have power during an outage.

Will my old cameras work with a new AVYCON recorder?

Many ONVIF cameras do, but test each one. Some only connect after ONVIF is enabled and a user is created in the camera's settings, and smart detection may not carry over between brands.

Does the system keep recording in a power outage?

Only when the recording and network equipment sits on a battery backup. A battery covers a few hours; for longer outages a generator circuit should feed the camera equipment.

Is AVYCON NDAA compliant?

AVYCON publishes an NDAA-compliant product list, including long-zoom PTZ models. If compliance is required, check each model number against that list rather than assuming the whole catalog qualifies.
