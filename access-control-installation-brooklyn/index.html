Access Control Installation Brooklyn projects usually begin with a key problem, not a technology wish list: nobody knows how many copies of the front-door key are out there. A brownstone vestibule key gets copied for every tenant, dog walker and contractor over the years; a walk-up in Bushwick or Canarsie turns over apartments every year or two and the old keys never all come back. Swapping that cylinder for a reader means each person gets a credential the owner can switch off in seconds. Abstract Enterprises Security Systems covers the systems, brands and installed price ranges on its Access Control Installation Brooklyn page. Below is the order of decisions that keeps a Brooklyn job from going sideways.

Decision 1: which doors, and in what order

Count the doors that actually control who gets in, then rank them. In most Brooklyn buildings the list is short:

  • The street or vestibule door. Almost always first. In a two- or three-family house this one reader often solves the whole problem.
  • Service, basement and package-room doors. These are where deliveries and trash pickup leave things propped open.
  • Elevator floors and amenity spaces. Only relevant in the taller condo and rental towers in Downtown Brooklyn, Williamsburg and Greenpoint, where a lobby fob alone lets anyone ride to any floor, the roof or the gym.
  • Tenant suites in shared industrial buildings. Navy Yard, Industry City and Gowanus-area lofts often need per-company permissions on shared docks and freight elevators.

The Brooklyn page prices a single door with reader and electric strike at $1,500–$2,500 installed, including wiring and ten programmed fobs. A lobby plus service entrance plus basement runs $4,000–$15,000, and a full building with elevator restriction and parking runs $15,000–$40,000 and up.

Decision 2: the credential

CredentialGood fitWatch out for
Older 125kHz proximity fobNothing new; you may already have itCopy kiosks and cheap cloners duplicate these easily, so the system cannot tell a copy from the original
Encrypted 13.56MHz fob or card (DESFire, iCLASS Seos)Rentals, co-ops, condosCosts a little more per fob; plan a changeover weekend with multi-technology readers
PIN keypadStockrooms, kitchens, warehouse personnel doorsCodes get shared; use schedules and change them on staff turnover
Phone credentialOwners managing several buildings remotelyOlder residents may still want a physical fob as backup

If your building put in fobs before roughly 2018, ask whether they are the older 125kHz type. Moving to encrypted credentials is a common Brooklyn upgrade on its own, and readers that accept both kinds let you swap residents over gradually.

Decision 3: landmark blocks and visible hardware

Brooklyn Heights was New York City's first historic district, designated in 1965, and Fort Greene, Clinton Hill, Cobble Hill, Park Slope and parts of Crown Heights and Bed-Stuy have districts of their own. Changes to the visible face of a landmarked building fall under the Landmarks Preservation Commission, so a surface-mounted reader and exposed conduit on a stoop can become a problem. The approach described on the Brooklyn page is to keep the outside quiet: wireless lock cylinders that fit the original mortise lock, magnetic locks concealed in the frame, and wiring routed through the interior and basement. If your block is landmarked, raise it before the site visit so the design starts there.

Decision 4: getting residents on board

On renter forums, the usual objections to fobs are that they log who comes and goes, that replacements cost money, and that a frozen or offline system can lock people out. Answer those up front:

  • Tell residents in writing what the system records and who can see the log.
  • Decide the replacement-fob policy before handing out the first one.
  • Keep a mechanical key override for the owner or super.
  • Ask about battery backup. Abstract Enterprises Security Systems sizes it to roughly 4 to 8 hours, which matters in parts of southeast Brooklyn like Canarsie and Marine Park where overhead lines go down in storms.

Decision 5: what happens when the power fails

Every controlled door gets set one of two ways. Fail-safe means the lock releases when power is lost, which is what exit doors need so people can get out in an emergency. Fail-secure means it stays locked, which suits a stockroom or server closet. Make the installer walk you through which setting each of your doors gets, and test it before they leave.

Questions to ask any installer

  1. Is the software cloud-based with a monthly fee, or managed locally? What does it cost in year three?
  2. Can I add the camera above the door and the intercom to the same system later?
  3. What happens to my fobs and data if I switch companies?
  4. Who programs new tenants at move-in, and how fast is a lost fob shut off?

Frequently asked questions

What does a fob reader on a Brooklyn brownstone door cost?

One vestibule door with a reader, electric strike, wiring and ten programmed fobs runs $1,500 to $2,500 installed, according to Abstract Enterprises Security Systems' Brooklyn pricing.

Can someone copy my building's key fob?

Older 125kHz proximity fobs are easy to duplicate at kiosks and with cheap cloners. Encrypted 13.56MHz credentials such as DESFire or iCLASS Seos are much harder to copy.

Can I put a card reader on a landmarked brownstone?

Yes, but visible exterior changes in a historic district fall under the Landmarks Preservation Commission. Concealed hardware, lock cylinders that fit the existing mortise lock and interior wiring keep the facade unchanged.

Why do some tenants push back on key fobs?

Common worries are that entries are logged, that replacements cost money and that a system failure could lock people out. A written log policy, a fair replacement policy and a mechanical override address most of it.

What happens to fob doors during a blackout?

Each door is set fail-safe (unlocks without power, used for exit paths) or fail-secure (stays locked). Battery backup keeps the system running for several hours before either setting matters.
