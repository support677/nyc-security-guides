This guide is written mostly for the second pile: owners, office managers and facilities people who have to pick hardware, software and placement for a lot, a lobby and a loading dock. Start at the curb cut, not the building corner

An Island business lot is usually wide, flat and unevenly lit after dark. One wide-angle camera bolted to the corner of the building will prove that a car pulled in at 2:14 a.m. It will rarely tell you who stepped out or what was on the bumper. Plan the system as a set of separate jobs, each with its own camera:

JobWhere it goesWhat decides success
Read platesEntrance lane, on a pole or wall set back from the curb cutLuminys says its plate readers need the angle to the farthest lane edge kept at 30 degrees or less, so a wide or curved entrance often needs two
See the whole lotHigh on the building, aimed down the long axisEven night lighting and a mount above arm's reach
Identify facesEach customer and staff doorA fixed, short distance where people walk straight at the lens
Watch the dockWall or ceiling inside the bay, plus one on the apronGlare from open doors and trucks backing into the lens
Cover the register or stockroomCeiling, behind and above the counterAngle that shows hands and the drawer, not just heads

Ask the installer to stand where cars actually slow down and show you the planned view on a phone before anything is drilled. The place a car brakes, not the property line, is where a plate gets read.

Which Luminys software will you actually live in?

The hardware choice gets most of the attention, but the software is what staff touch every day. Luminys runs three layers, and a lot of disappointment comes from assuming one does the job of another.

LayerWho uses itWorth knowing
LumiViewer Mobile (phone app)Owners and managers checking alerts after closingFamilies or staff coming from DMSS on a Dahua recorder will need the new app once the recorder changes; give each person a separate login
LumiCenter (desktop and server)Front desk, security office, multi-site managersLuminys publishes a migration FAQ for DSS users and says LumiCenter accepts native, generic and ONVIF camera profiles
LumiCloudAnyone who wants remote access or an off-site copyA Lite tier covers remote viewing; paid plans add cloud storage, and Luminys states local access stays if a plan lapses

For a single storefront the phone app is usually enough. A building with a reception desk and a separate warehouse office usually wants LumiCenter on two or three computers, with permissions set so the dock lead sees the dock and nothing else.

Keep the cameras from crowding the office network

Cameras are heavy, constant network traffic. A thread on the r/Dahua forum describes a multi-building site where a Luminys recorder flooded the network until the IT team, with help from their Meraki support line, capped the traffic going to that recorder. The cure is planned in, not bolted on: cameras on their own switch or their own VLAN, the recorder in the network closet rather than on someone's desk, and remote viewing through the app instead of ports opened on the firewall. If your IT provider manages the office network, get them on the call before the camera count is final so the switch has the power and the uplink to carry it.

The compliance file for Hauppauge and Route 110 suppliers

Plenty of firms in the Hauppauge park and along Route 110 sell to government buyers, and their contracts can restrict which surveillance brands may sit on the premises. Luminys markets its cameras, recorders and software as NDAA-compliant, and ADI, the distributor, lists the lines that way. Do not accept that as a blanket statement. Ask for each model's published compliance document and keep it with the equipment list, so an auditor sees a paper for every device.

Two other records help. First, the regulatory history: according to SecurityInfoWatch, the FCC moved against Luminys equipment authorizations in 2025, then reversed course with a January 20 order that ended the case. Keep a note of where that stands on the day you buy. Second, the building's own rules: office landlords along Earle Ovington Boulevard and in the corridor parks typically want a certificate of insurance, set working hours and firestopping wherever cable crosses a rated wall or floor. Many Luminys models can record sound; leave audio switched off unless you have taken legal advice on recording conversations at your site.

If the police ask for video

Suffolk County Police run a camera registry where owners list where their systems are, so detectives can ask them to check a date and time; retrieval stays at the owner's convenience. In Nassau, Garden City Police offer a voluntary registration of private cameras and say plainly it gives them no access to the systems. Neither program hands anyone a live feed. What matters on the day is that someone on staff can export a clip with a timestamp in a few minutes, so have the installer walk two people through it at handover.

Five things to settle before you sign

  1. Which existing cameras and recorders will be tested for reuse, and what happens if a test fails.
  2. How many plate cameras the entrance geometry really needs, shown on a site sketch.
  3. Where the recorder lives, and whether it sits on a battery backup with the switch and the modem.
  4. Which software each user gets, with named logins rather than one shared password.
  5. Which compliance documents, insurance certificates and as-built notes arrive with the final invoice. The published terms carry 30 days of warranty cover, and longer paid plans can be added.

Frequently asked questions

Is a Luminys camera just a Dahua camera with a new label?

Installers still argue about it; one IPCamTalk thread asks whether it is 100 percent rebranded or closer to 85 percent. Luminys is the buyer of Dahua's old North American operation and releases firmware of its own, so treat it as its own product line and test any mix of old and new equipment.

Will a new Luminys camera work on my old Dahua recorder?

Often not. Forum users report that Luminys recorders tend to accept older Dahua cameras, while new Luminys cameras on an older Dahua recorder frequently fail. Have it tested before buying around it.

Can one camera read plates on every lane of our entrance?

Only on a narrow, straight entrance. Luminys asks for no more than 30 degrees between the lens and the far edge of the farthest lane, so wide or curved entrances usually need two cameras.

Do we lose our cameras if we cancel LumiCloud?

No. Luminys says you keep full local access to cameras and recorders when a plan expires; you lose the cloud feature itself, such as off-site storage.

Is Luminys equipment NDAA-compliant?

Luminys and its distributor ADI market the lines as NDAA-compliant. For a government contract, collect the published compliance statement for each model you install and keep it on file.
