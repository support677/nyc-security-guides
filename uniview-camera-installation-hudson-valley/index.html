Uniview Camera Installation Hudson Valley jobs go smoothly when three decisions are made in the right order. The recorder comes first, because it settles which cameras the system can take and whether the coax already inside an older farmhouse or village Victorian can be reused. The camera line comes second: Uniview's Prime full-color models, its standard infrared cameras, or a PTZ for open ground. The security settings come last, and they are the step most often skipped. This guide follows that order and adds two quirks Uniview owners tend to notice in their first year. Start with what is already in the walls

A lot of Hudson Valley housing predates network cameras. River-town houses in Kingston, Newburgh or Beacon, and plenty of rural homes in Dutchess and Orange, still carry the coax from an analog system someone put in fifteen years ago. Uniview builds for both situations. Its NVRs record IP cameras over network cable, and its hybrid recorders, sold as XVRs, take HD-analog cameras over existing coax alongside a few IP channels. Which one suits you depends on the cable you have and the buildings you want covered.

A power detail on hybrid recorders

One distributor that services Uniview gear documents a fault on some XVRs that arrive with a 12-volt, 2-amp adapter. With a large hard drive installed, the recorder and drive together draw more than the adapter supplies, so the drive drops out, the box beeps and recording stops, even though the recorder tests fine on a bench. The documented fix is a 12-volt supply rated 3 amps or more. If you are reusing coax with a hybrid recorder and a big drive, check that adapter before blaming anything else.

Prime, standard infrared, or a PTZ

Uniview Prime is the full-color line: a wide aperture and a warm supplement light keep the picture in color after dark. On a country road in Putnam or Ulster with no streetlight for a mile, that is the difference between a usable image and a gray outline. Standard infrared models cost less and are perfectly good where a porch light or a lit storefront already covers the scene, as on a Nyack or Cold Spring main street.

  • Prime for a dark driveway, a parking area behind a building, or a yard you need to see in color at 2 a.m.
  • Standard infrared for entries, porches, covered walkways and lit commercial frontage.
  • PTZ where one mount has to watch a long approach or an open field and follow whatever moves.
  • AI detection on any camera that faces trees or fields, so person and vehicle alerts are not buried under deer and branches.

Uniview's warm supplement light is visible, so think about where it points. Aimed down a neighbor's driveway, it can start a conversation you did not plan on.

Cameras where no cable can reach: the 4G option

Uniview sells cellular cameras that send video over a SIM card instead of a network cable. They suit an entry gate where an Ulster driveway meets the road, a farm stand, or a lot under construction. Two things to know. The SIM needs its own data plan, so this one camera carries a monthly cost even when the rest of the system does not. And the same distributor reports that some 4G models on a Verizon SIM connect and answer pings but stall when video starts; the documented cure is lowering the camera's MTU setting to 1228. A technician setting one up should know that before the truck leaves.

Lock it down before the technician leaves

Uniview equipment is widely sold and widely scanned on the internet, and a recorder left on factory settings is an easy target. A proper hand-off covers these points:

  1. Every camera and the recorder have their own strong passwords, not the defaults.
  2. Firmware is current on the recorder and each camera, with a note of the version.
  3. Remote access runs through the EZView app account, not through open ports on the router; UPnP is off.
  4. Family members and caretakers get their own viewer logins with only the cameras they need.
  5. Cameras sit on their own network segment where the router allows it, apart from laptops and phones.
  6. You receive the admin credentials in writing, and the recorder is registered to you.

Two things owners notice in the first year

The hour that plays twice. When daylight saving time ends in November, the clock falls back and the recorder holds two copies of the 1 a.m. hour. The timeline appears to jump backward. Nothing is lost; you pick the copy you want during playback.

Live view that will not start in the app. Uniview publishes troubleshooting for EZView live view failures, and in practice the cause is usually the recorder's internet connection or a weak cell signal on the phone, not the cameras. On rural lines with limited upload speed, viewing the substream instead of the main stream often fixes it. Desktop users can watch the same system in EZStation.

Frequently asked questions

Can Uniview cameras use my old coax wiring?

Yes, through a Uniview hybrid recorder (XVR). It takes HD-analog cameras over existing coax and also has network channels, so IP cameras can be added later.

Is Uniview Prime worth it on a rural Hudson Valley lot?

Usually, where the scene is dark. Prime keeps video in color at night with a wide aperture and a warm light. Where a porch light or storefront lighting already covers the area, standard infrared cameras are often enough.

Does a Uniview system have a monthly fee?

Not for local NVR recording and app viewing. The exception is a 4G cellular camera, which needs its own SIM data plan.

Why does my Uniview NVR show the same hour twice in November?

When daylight saving time ends the clock falls back, so the recorder stores two copies of the 1 a.m. hour. No video is lost; choose the copy you need during playback.

Which app do I use to watch Uniview cameras?

EZView on a phone or tablet, and EZStation on a computer. Each person should have their own viewer login rather than sharing the admin account.

What does Uniview installation cost in the Hudson Valley?

The page lists a four-camera system with recorder from $2,600 installed, and typical work at about $480 to $850 per camera, higher in Dutchess and Ulster. The final price follows a site visit.
