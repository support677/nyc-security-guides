You bought the house and the cameras came with it

Long Island homes change hands with camera systems still on the walls, and the recorder rarely comes with its password. Before anything else, find out what you have: read the model label on the NVR and on one camera, count the cables plugged into the back of the recorder, and note whether each camera has an Ethernet cable (network) or a round coax cable (older analog).

Then deal with access. Owners on camera forums describe buying used Uniview recorders with no working password and no obvious reset option. Many UNV recorders have no simple factory-reset button; recovery goes through the recorder's own password-recovery options or Uniview's support process, and a recorder registered to the cloud may still be tied to the seller's account. The cleanest fix is to ask the sellers, or the company that installed it, to release the account and hand over the admin password at closing. If that is not possible, a technician can recover the recorder through the manufacturer's process, clear the old settings, set a new admin password and register it to your own app account.

Once it is yours, check the hard drive's health and the date of the last recording. A drive that failed quietly while the house sat empty during the sale is a common discovery.

Storefronts and parking fields in strip centers

Much of Long Island's retail sits in strip centers along roads like Hempstead Turnpike, Jericho Turnpike and Sunrise Highway, with a shared lot in front and a service lane behind. That layout shapes a Uniview plan:

  • Inside: one camera over the register for faces and transactions, one down each aisle, and one on the stockroom or rear door.
  • The service lane: often dark, with deliveries and dumpsters. A full-color or deterrence model with lights and a siren earns its place here.
  • The lot: the landlord usually controls the poles and the facade. Before buying a long-range or plate-reading camera for the parking field, get written permission from the property manager for the mount and the cable route, and ask whether the center already records the lot.

Store glass faces the lot, so a camera near the entrance needs strong WDR, or should be turned to look inward toward the door rather than out into the glare.

Uniview or Ubiquiti?

Long Island buyers often weigh the two, and both record locally with no monthly fee. The practical differences are elsewhere. Uniview records to a standalone NVR, is sold and supported through installers and distributors, and its recorders accept ONVIF cameras from other makers. Ubiquiti's UniFi Protect runs on a UniFi console and fits best when the home network is UniFi too. Ubiquiti publishes retail prices online, while Uniview pricing usually comes through a dealer quote. Neither is wrong; the choice is whether you want cameras tied to your network gear or kept as their own system.

Five questions before you sign

  1. Will the recorder be registered to my account, with the admin password given to me in writing?
  2. How many days will the drive hold at the resolution being set?
  3. Which app will I use, and who else will have access to it?
  4. For a store: is the landlord's written approval in hand for every exterior mount?
  5. Who updates the firmware on the cameras and recorder, and how often?

Frequently asked questions

I bought a house with a Uniview NVR and no password. What now?

Ask the sellers or their installer to release the app account and hand over the admin password. If they cannot, a technician can recover the recorder through Uniview's process, clear the old settings and register it to your own account.

Can I reset a used Uniview recorder myself?

Often not with a simple button. Many UNV recorders handle recovery through their own password-recovery options or Uniview support, so keep proof that the equipment came with your property.

Is Uniview or Ubiquiti better for a Long Island home?

Both record locally with no monthly fee. Uniview uses a standalone NVR supported through installers and accepts ONVIF cameras; Ubiquiti suits homes that already run a UniFi network. Pick by how you want the system tied to your network.

Can I add cameras to an existing Uniview system?

Usually, if the NVR has free channels and PoE ports. If not, a PoE switch or a larger recorder adds capacity.

Why does my Uniview camera show offline?

Common causes are power or cable faults on a long outdoor run, a network address conflict, or firmware. If the camera is online but not recording, check the hard drive first.

What should I budget for Uniview cameras on Long Island?

Expect about $480–$850 for each installed camera, per the Long Island page, and roughly $3,200–$6,500 to cover a whole property's perimeter. Nassau runs above city rates and the East End higher, with the exact figure set after someone walks the property for free.
