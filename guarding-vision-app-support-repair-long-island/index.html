Guarding Vision App Support Repair Long Island requests are not always about a broken system. Just as often the cameras work fine but the wrong people can see them, the right people cannot, or a clip that matters will not come out of the app in a form anyone can use. The app is a relabeled edition of Hik-Connect, Hikvision's own viewer, and it has proper tools for sharing a system and exporting video; they just are not obvious. This guide covers who should get what kind of access, how to clean up access after a sale or a staff change, and how to get footage to a police officer or an insurer without losing the details that make it useful. Booking and how a visit works are explained on the Guarding Vision App Support Repair Long Island page.

One owner account, many viewers

The cleanest setup has one owner account, held by the person who actually owns the property or business, with everyone else added through sharing. In the app this lives under More, then Manage Sharing Settings, where you can share a device with another Guarding Vision user by email or phone number and choose what they are allowed to do. The common mistake is handing out the owner login or the recorder's admin password instead. That works until someone changes the password, leaves on bad terms, or loses their phone.

Cleaning up after a sale or a staff change

Long Island houses and storefronts change hands with cameras still on the walls, and the old owner's account often still controls the recorder. If you bought a property with a Guarding Vision system:

  1. Find out which account the recorder is registered to. If it is not yours, the device has to be released from that account or reset and re-added under yours.
  2. Change the recorder's admin password, not just the app password.
  3. Review the sharing list and remove anyone you do not recognize.
  4. Check the recorder's own user list too; local users can exist that never appear in the app.

The same steps apply when an employee leaves: remove their share the day they go, and change any password they knew.

Getting a clip out for police or insurance

When something happens, the footage is only useful if it is complete and the time on it can be trusted.

  • Check the clock first. If the recorder's time is off, or it never switched for daylight saving, note the difference before you export, and fix the time-sync setting afterwards.
  • Export from the recorder when you can. A file saved to a USB drive from the recorder keeps the original quality and timestamps. A clip saved from the phone app is convenient but often lower resolution.
  • Do not screen-record the phone as your only copy. It loses quality and the embedded time.
  • Save more than the moment. Include a few minutes before and after, and every camera that might show the person arriving or leaving.
  • Keep the original and give copies. Recorders overwrite old footage, so export quickly once you know there is an incident.

Suffolk County police operate a program where homes and businesses can list where their cameras are; investigators then know whom to ask for footage after an incident nearby. Registering only records the location; police reach out and ask rather than viewing your system.

Why sharing and exports break

  • A shared user sees the device as offline: usually the recorder itself lost its cloud connection, so the owner sees it too.
  • Playback fails for a shared user on cellular data while it works at home: often the share did not include playback, or the stream is set too high for a mobile connection.
  • Only the owner gets alerts: notifications are set per user and per phone, and each person has to allow them.
  • Export stops partway: the recorder's drive may be failing, which is worth checking before the next incident.

A typical Long Island situation

A shop owner on a busy turnpike spends part of the winter out of state. The manager needs to see the register camera, the owner wants alerts after closing, and the old alarm company still has a login from years ago. The fix is not a new system: the recorder is moved under the owner's account, the alarm company's access is removed, the manager gets a share limited to live view and playback, and the owner's phone is set for after-hours alerts only.

Frequently asked questions

How do I share my Guarding Vision cameras with someone else?

In the app, go to More, then Manage Sharing Settings, and share the device with the other person's Guarding Vision account by email or phone number. You can choose which permissions they get.

How do I remove a previous owner from my Guarding Vision system?

Find which account the recorder is registered to, have it released or reset and re-added under your own account, change the recorder admin password and remove unknown users from both the app and the recorder.

What is the best way to give camera footage to the police?

Export the clip from the recorder to a USB drive with the original timestamps, include a few minutes either side, note any clock error and keep the original copy.

Does Suffolk County have a security camera registry?

Yes. Suffolk County police keep a voluntary list of camera locations from homes and businesses, so detectives know whose door to knock on for video after something happens nearby.

Why can't my shared user see playback?

Usually the share did not include playback permission, or the recorder's stream is set too high for a mobile connection. Check the sharing permissions first.
