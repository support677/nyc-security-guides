Hanwha Camera Installation Brooklyn work rarely starts on a blank wall. More often someone opens a basement closet under the stoop, finds a recorder with a Samsung badge, a switch with a different logo and a bundle of grey cable nobody marked, and asks whether any of it can stay. This guide is built around that moment: how to tell how old the gear is, what to keep, what a landmarked facade allows, which recording option fits the people in the building, and what to ask before you sign. Abstract Enterprises Security Systems covers the work itself on its Hanwha Camera Installation Brooklyn page.

Read the badge before you price anything

Hanwha's camera line has carried three names, and Brooklyn closets often hold all three at once. That does not mean three different makers. It is one product family that changed owners and labels, so the name on the housing is mostly a clue to age.

What the label saysRough eraWhat it usually means for you
Samsung or Samsung TechwinBefore mid-2015Ten-plus years old. Drives, power supplies and outdoor connectors are the usual failures. Budget for replacing the recorder at minimum.
Hanwha Techwin or Wisenet2015 to early 2023Often worth keeping if the cameras still give a clean picture and the firmware can be updated.
Hanwha VisionMarch 2023 onwardCurrent hardware. Adding cameras to it is normally simpler than replacing it.

Write down the model number on each camera and on the recorder, and photograph the back panel of the recorder. Those three things settle more of the quote than any conversation about megapixels.

Keep, reuse or replace: triage for a system already on the building

The expensive part of a Brooklyn job is getting cable through brick, brownstone and plaster, so the cheapest system is often the one that keeps the cable that is already there. A good installer tests before deciding.

  • Cable that certifies clean stays. A Cat5e or Cat6 run that passes a cable test can carry a new PoE camera. Coax from an analog system can sometimes be reused with adapters, but it is usually the first thing replaced on an upgrade.
  • Cameras with a good picture can wait. If a 2018 Wisenet dome still shows a sharp, correctly colored image at night, there is little reason to pull it this year.
  • The recorder is usually the weak point. A unit that has run around the clock for a decade, sitting in a damp cellar, is the part most likely to quit next. Replacing it first protects the footage you have.
  • Unlabelled cable gets toned and labelled once. Paying for an hour of tracing now saves paying for it on every future service call.

If you already bought Hanwha cameras online, the Abstract Enterprises Security Systems page says it will install them labor-only, after checking model numbers and whether your switch can power them. Do that check before the visit, because a PoE switch that is short on power is a common reason a camera works in the shop and fails on the wall.

Front-of-building limits on landmarked blocks

Brooklyn has more historic districts than most people expect, from Brooklyn Heights and Cobble Hill to large parts of Park Slope, Fort Greene, Bedford-Stuyvesant and Crown Heights North. On those blocks the Landmarks Preservation Commission sets the rules for what goes on the street side of the house.

Under the LPC rules described on the service page, a smaller building, six stories or less with no more than 40 feet of street frontage, may carry up to two security cameras for each 25 feet of frontage; larger buildings get three per 25 feet. Cameras are supposed to be placed so they are as unobtrusive as possible. In practice that pushes the plan toward compact Hanwha models, mounts anchored in the mortar between stones instead of drilled through the stone itself, and cable that enters the wall behind the camera instead of running across the facade. Rear yards and areaways are usually far less restricted, which is why many brownstone plans put the bigger, longer-range camera in the back.

If you rent, ask the owner. If you own a co-op unit, ask the board or managing agent before anyone drills. Those approvals take longer than the installation.

NVR or WAVE: decide by who needs to watch

Hanwha gives you two ways to record, and the right one depends less on the cameras than on the people.

QuestionWisenet NVR (a recorder box)Wisenet WAVE (software on a server)
Who is it for in Brooklyn?A brownstone owner, a two-family landlord, a single storeA co-op with a super, a board and a managing agent; a business with several sites
LicensingBought once with the boxLicensed per camera, which adds cost as the system grows
ViewingPhone app plus a monitor on the recorderSeparate user roles, so the super sees the lobby and the board sees the reports
Where it livesA shelf in the cellar or a closetA small server or appliance, ideally on battery backup

For most houses and walk-ups, the recorder box is the sensible choice. WAVE earns its cost when several people need different levels of access, or when footage has to be pulled and documented for insurers or a funder.

When the request comes from a lender, a grant or a lease

A lot of Brooklyn Hanwha requests are not about picture quality. They arrive because a lease rider, an insurance review, a new owner's standards or a funding condition asks for equipment from a maker that is not on the FCC Covered List, where Hikvision and Dahua sit. Hanwha Vision is not on that list. Existing Hikvision or Dahua cameras do not switch off because of the federal rules, so the job is usually a planned replacement rather than an emergency: recorder first if it is the oldest piece, then cameras in the order the building cares about, keeping any cable that tests good. Ask for an equipment list with every make and model, because whoever asked for the change will usually want one for their file.

Questions to put to any installer before you sign

  • Which of my existing cables did you test, and which ones are you replacing?
  • Which Hanwha series is going at each position, and why that one?
  • How much PoE power does the switch have, and how much will the cameras draw?
  • If the block is landmarked, who is preparing anything the LPC needs?
  • At handover, whose name is on the admin login, and will I get it in writing?
  • Will I get a port map showing which cable goes to which camera?

Frequently asked questions

Do Hanwha cameras need a monthly fee or a license?

Not with a Wisenet NVR: the recorder is bought once and records locally, with free phone viewing. Wisenet WAVE software is licensed per camera, which suits larger buildings that need separate user roles.

Are Samsung, Wisenet and Hanwha cameras made by one company?

Yes. Samsung Techwin's camera business became Hanwha Techwin in 2015 and was renamed Hanwha Vision in 2023, so Samsung, Wisenet and Hanwha labels are one product line at different ages.

How many cameras can go on the front of a landmarked Brooklyn brownstone?

LPC rules allow up to two security cameras per 25 feet of street frontage on buildings of six stories or less with 40 feet of frontage or less, and three per 25 feet on larger buildings, mounted to minimize visible impact.

Can I keep Wisenet cameras an alarm company installed and drop their service?

Often, if the cameras and recorder are standard Wisenet units and you can get the admin credentials or have the recorder reset. An installer can check the model numbers and re-add the cameras to a recorder you control.

Can existing cable be reused for a new Hanwha system?

Yes, when a Cat5e or Cat6 run passes a cable test it can carry a new PoE camera. Old analog coax and runs that fail testing are usually replaced.
