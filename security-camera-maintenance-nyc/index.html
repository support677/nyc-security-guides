Every month: the five-minute owner check

None of this needs a ladder or a password reset. It needs someone with a login and a reminder on the calendar.

  • Play back, don't just watch live. Open playback on each camera and scroll to the oldest recording. A live picture on the lobby monitor says nothing about whether anything was saved.
  • Count the days held. Compare the oldest date with the retention you think you have. If you expected 30 days and see 9, something changed — a smaller drive, a new camera eating space, or a channel that stopped recording.
  • Look at night video. Haze, glare, cobwebs and spiders in front of infrared lenses show up at night long before they show in daylight.
  • Check the clock. A recorder whose time has drifted makes footage hard to use as evidence.
  • Open the app from outside the building. Use mobile data, not the building Wi-Fi, so you know remote viewing really works.

Every quarter or twice a year: the technician visit

How often depends on exposure. Street-facing cameras near traffic, construction dust or salt air on the shore need the shorter cycle; a small indoor system in a house can go longer. A real visit should leave a written record of these checks:

Between visits: NYC events that should trigger a check

A sidewalk shed or scaffold goes up

Façade work under Local Law 11 and other repairs put sheds over sidewalks across the city. A camera that watched the entrance may now film plywood. Lower it, move it under the shed or re-aim it, photograph the before-and-after, and put it back when the shed comes down. Ideally plan this when the façade contractor files, not after.

A power outage or voltage reduction

After a Con Edison outage, check that the recorder restarted, its clock is right and every channel is recording again. A tired battery backup is the usual reason a recorder comes back with gaps.

The internet provider swaps the router

New routers change local addresses and port settings, and remote viewing often dies silently. Test the app from outside the building the same day.

The cellar takes water

Plenty of recorders, switches and battery units sit on cellar floors near boilers and pipes. If a basement has flooded before, raising the equipment off the floor is cheap insurance.

Maintenance or repair?

Maintenance keeps a working system honest. If a camera is black, the app shows the whole building offline, or the recorder is beeping, that is a repair call first — book it as one and fold the system into a schedule afterward. Changes and additions are a third category; on systems Abstract Enterprises Security Systems installed, its maintenance page bills those at the $195 hourly rate, three hours minimum.

A walk-up scenario

A five-storey walk-up has eight cameras and a recorder in the cellar beside the boiler room. The super can see the lobby on a small monitor, so everyone assumes it works. On the first visit, playback shows only eleven days on a drive that was meant to hold a month, two hallway cameras with fogged domes, and a battery unit with no date on it. The fix list is short — clean, re-aim, investigate the drive, test and label the battery — and the super leaves with a one-page login sheet and a practiced export. The next visit is set for the shorter cycle because the façade cameras face a busy avenue.

Questions to ask before signing a maintenance plan

  • Do you play back the oldest recording on every visit and write down the days held?
  • Are parts included, or quoted before they are fitted?
  • Will you work on equipment you didn't install?
  • Who receives the visit log — me, the managing agent, the board, the insurer?
  • Can visits fit our building's permitted contractor hours?

Frequently asked questions

How often should security cameras be serviced in NYC?

Most houses and small buildings do well with two to four technician visits a year, with street-facing or dusty locations on the shorter cycle. Between visits, an owner or super should check playback, the days of footage held and night video once a month.

How do I know my camera system is actually recording?

Open playback, not live view, and find the oldest recording on each camera. If the number of days held is lower than expected, or a channel shows gaps, the drive, the settings or a camera needs attention.

Does a camera maintenance plan include replacement parts?

Not usually. On Abstract Enterprises Security Systems plans, parts such as hard drives, battery backup units, dome bubbles and cameras are priced separately on a written quote before they are fitted.

What should I do when scaffolding or a sidewalk shed blocks my cameras?

While the shed stands, get each blocked camera dropped lower, relocated beneath the deck or pointed somewhere useful, with before-and-after photos, and restored when it comes down. Plan it with the façade contractor before the shed goes up.

How much does security camera maintenance cost in NYC?

Abstract Enterprises Security Systems lists a one-time tune-up at $150–$350 in Brooklyn and the Bronx, rising by borough factor to $188–$438 in Manhattan, and annual home or small-building plans from $200–$500 at the base. Commercial and compliance plans start around $1,000 a year and are priced after a walkthrough.

Will a technician maintain a system someone else installed?

Many will, Abstract Enterprises Security Systems included. Expect the first visit to take longer, since it includes mapping every camera, recorder and login, and writing the owner-and-password record.
