Luminys Camera Installation NYC: A Decision Path for Buildings Still Running Dahua Gear

By Abstract Enterprises Security Systems — licensed NYS low-voltage contractor, in security since 2000 · Updated 2026-11-10

Luminys camera installation NYC questions almost always begin with the system already on the wall. Luminys took over Dahua's North American business when it was sold and rebranded in 2024, so thousands of New York lobbies, storefronts and brownstones now have Dahua cameras, an aging Dahua recorder and no clear idea what to buy next. This guide walks through the decision in the order it should be made: the recorder first, then the cameras, then the wire, then the people who need access. Pricing and booking are on the Luminys Camera Installation NYC page.

Question 1: Is your recorder healthy?

Look at the NVR before anything else. It is usually the first component to wear out: a spinning drive, 24 hours a day, tucked into a closet or under a desk with poor airflow.

  • Recorder failing, out of drive space or years past its prime? Plan a Luminys NVR first. It is the anchor of the new system.
  • Recorder fine and just a camera or two to add? Putting Luminys cameras on a Dahua recorder is the combination IPCamTalk users report trouble with, and firmware cannot be cross-loaded between the two brands. The practical move is to add cameras your current recorder supports now and switch to Luminys when the recorder is due.
  • Need NDAA-compliant equipment for a lender, insurer, grant or government-linked tenant? Go to Luminys hardware end to end. ADI, a major security distributor, lists Luminys cameras, software and kits as NDAA-compliant.

Question 2: Which old cameras can stay?

Reports from the field are mostly encouraging in one direction. Forum users and at least one integrator say Dahua cameras generally run on a Luminys NVR, while one early reviewer noted that some features came through only at a basic ONVIF level. Results vary by model and firmware, so the only reliable answer is a test.

A good installer hooks every camera you plan to keep onto the Luminys NVR before the Dahua box is retired. Each camera is checked for live view, recording and playback, and you get a written keep or replace list with a reason on every line.

Question 3: Is the wire worth keeping?

In most NYC buildings, cable pathway is the expensive part. That means the riser in a walk-up, the core through a brownstone party wall, the run along a storefront ceiling. If existing Cat5e or Cat6 runs test clean and end where the new cameras should go, keep them. If they were spliced in a hallway, crushed under a door saddle or run past distance limits, a camera upgrade is the cheapest time to fix them. Old coax can often serve as a pull line for new network cable.

Question 4: Who needs access, and how?

This is where the migration pays for itself. Many Dahua buildings run on one shared DMSS login that every past super, porter and managing agent still knows. With Luminys, the system moves to LumiCloud and the Luminys mobile app, with LumiCenter software for managers who watch several buildings. Use the switch to:

  • give the owner, the managing agent and the super their own logins with only the permissions each one needs;
  • retire the old shared password and remove former staff;
  • keep the primary copy of footage on the recorder in the building, so a lapsed cloud plan or an internet outage never leaves a gap;
  • check that the recorder's clock is correct and practice exporting a clip, which is the first thing police or an insurer will ask for.

Question 5: What will the building allow?

  • Co-ops and condos: the board controls common areas, so the camera plan and cable routes need its approval before work is scheduled.
  • Rentals: lobbies, halls, mailrooms and elevators are common camera locations. New York law forbids cameras in places where people would reasonably expect privacy. On whether tenants must be told in advance, the sources the Abstract Enterprises Security Systems page cites disagree, so ask your attorney. Giving notice and writing down the security reason is the cautious route, especially in rent-stabilized buildings.
  • Landmark districts: Landmarks Preservation Commission guidance favors cameras off the primary facade, finished to match the building. The areaway, the underside of the stoop and the vestibule are the usual first choices.

Install day, in order

  1. Photograph the old recorder, every cable landing and the model label, and note the clock.
  2. Pull any footage you need from the old drive before anything is unplugged.
  3. Mount the Luminys NVR, connect kept cameras and test each one.
  4. Pull and terminate new runs, then mount new cameras.
  5. Set recording, detection zones and privacy masks, and confirm retention with the storage calculator.
  6. Create user accounts, set up the app on each phone and do a test export.

Questions to ask any Luminys installer

  • Are you an authorized Luminys partner, and how are warranty claims handled?
  • Which of my current cameras will you test, and will I get a written keep or replace list?
  • How will you preserve footage on the old recorder?
  • Will each person get a separate login, and who keeps admin?
  • Is any cloud plan optional, and what happens to my cameras if it lapses?

FAQ

Is Luminys the same as Dahua?

Luminys came out of the 2024 sale and rebranding of Dahua's North American business. It sells its own cameras, recorders, software and cloud services, and distributors list its line as NDAA-compliant.

Will my Dahua cameras work on a new Luminys recorder?

Field reports say most do, but some models connect with fewer features. Test each camera on the new NVR before the Dahua box goes.

Can I add Luminys cameras to my existing Dahua NVR?

Forum users report that Luminys cameras do not reliably work on Dahua recorders, and firmware cannot be swapped between them. Plan on a Luminys recorder if Luminys cameras are going in.

What happens if I stop paying for LumiCloud?

The Abstract Enterprises Security Systems page cites Luminys saying you keep local access to cameras and recorders if a LumiCloud license expires. You lose the cloud features of that plan, which is why footage should live on the local recorder first.

Can I put a Luminys camera on the front of a landmarked brownstone?

Possibly, with approval. Landmarks guidance prefers cameras off the primary facade, so side, rear, areaway and vestibule spots are checked first.

The Abstract Enterprises Security Systems page publishes a Luminys price list by borough. Cable routes, wall type, recorder size and how much of the old system can be reused decide the final quote.
