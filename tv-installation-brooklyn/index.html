TV Installation Brooklyn pricing moves with the wall more than with the TV. The same 65-inch set costs a different amount to hang on a stud wall in a new Williamsburg tower, on plaster in a Crown Heights pre-war, on the brick party wall of a Bed-Stuy row house or over a parlor-floor fireplace in Park Slope. Which Brooklyn wall do you have?

NYC forums are full of people whose stud finder beeps at nothing on a pre-war wall. In old Brooklyn buildings that is expected, not a broken tool. A quick guide:

  • Plaster over wood lath (brownstones and pre-war apartments). Thick, hard and brittle. Studs exist but scanners struggle to find them. Wrong anchors or an impact driver can crack it. This is the plaster line.
  • Plaster applied straight onto brick or block (many party walls and exterior walls). No studs at all. It needs masonry fasteners through the plaster into the brick, which is billed as brick.
  • Exposed brick (lofts, renovated row houses). Hammer drill and masonry anchors set into solid brick, with wires run in a surface raceway because there is no cavity to hide them in.
  • Poured concrete (DUMBO and Williamsburg loft conversions). Sleeve anchors or concrete screws. Strong, but slow to drill.
  • Drywall on metal studs (newer condo towers). Light-gauge steel does not hold lag screws the way wood does, so bigger TVs often need a plywood backer behind the mount.

If you cannot tell, choose "not sure" and the wall gets confirmed before anyone drills.

The parlor-floor fireplace

Many parlor floors in Park Slope, Fort Greene and Clinton Hill have a marble or brick mantel, and owners want the screen right above it. Three things decide whether that works. Height: on a tall mantel the screen ends up high enough to strain your neck from the sofa, so a pull-down mount that lowers the set to viewing height is worth its extra cost if you watch from a sofa close by. Heat: if the fireplace is used, the TV needs clearance from the hot air coming off the opening, so check your set's manual for its limits. Wires: brick chimney breasts have no cavity, so cables either go in a raceway painted to match or route sideways to a cabinet. Many decorative mantels in Brooklyn are sealed and unused, which removes the heat question but not the height one.

Co-op, condo and walk-up logistics

Brooklyn co-ops and condos, from Ocean Parkway to the Downtown towers, usually ask for a certificate of insurance before any contractor works in the building, often naming the building and managing agent. Send the management office's requirements when you book so the COI arrives ahead of the visit. Many buildings also limit contractor hours and require a service elevator reservation, so check the house rules before choosing a time. In walk-ups the issue is the stairs: a large TV up four flights takes two people, which is why the walk-up carry is a separate line.

What the base price does not include

  • Patching or painting holes, including holes left by an old bracket.
  • Electrical work: outlets are not added or moved. The in-wall concealment kit is the code-friendly way to get power behind the TV without a new outlet.
  • Moving heavy furniture and picture calibration.

Knowing these ahead keeps the day from turning into a surprise. If you are adding sound, the NYC soundbar installation guide covers bar placement and wiring.

Renting? Think about move-out now

A properly mounted TV leaves a few small holes that patch easily, and most Brooklyn leases tolerate that if you repair them at move-out. Read your lease first. If drilling is off the table, a floor stand or tension-pole mount gets the TV off the dresser without touching the walls.

Frequently asked questions

Plaster wall and the stud finder finds nothing: can it still hold a TV?

Yes. Plaster over lath usually has studs that need deep scanning or test holes to find; plaster applied straight onto brick has none, so the mount goes into the brick with masonry anchors. Either way the wall is identified before drilling.

What does an over-the-mantel TV mount cost in a Brooklyn brownstone?

The Abstract Enterprises Security Systems price builder starts at $179.99 for screens from 32 up to 55 inches, with +$130 for an over-fireplace mount or +$160 for a pull-down fireplace mount, plus +$65 if the wall is brick or stone. Larger TVs and brackets add to that.

My co-op needs a certificate of insurance. Is that extra?

No. The COI for your building is listed as free. Send the management office's requirements when you book so it arrives before the visit.

Will you move my outlet behind the TV?

Outlets are not added or moved. The in-wall cable concealment kit (+$169) gets power and cables behind the screen without new electrical work.

Is there an extra charge for a fourth-floor walk-up?

Yes, a walk-up carry to the third floor or higher with no elevator is listed at +$40.
