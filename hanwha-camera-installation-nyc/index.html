Hanwha Camera Installation NYC projects go best when the owner makes two decisions before anyone climbs a ladder: which Hanwha series belongs at each spot, and what records the video. Hanwha Vision, the Korean company behind the Wisenet name, sells cameras in several tiers and two quite different recording approaches, and in a city building the wrong pairing either wastes money or leaves a weak spot at the one door that matters. This guide works through those choices, then shows what an install day in a New York building usually looks like. For pricing by borough and a site survey, see Hanwha Camera Installation NYC.

Why buildings are asking for Hanwha now

The short version is federal policy. Hikvision and Dahua sit on the FCC Covered List, and in 2026 the FCC moved beyond refusing new approvals to restricting the import and marketing of older authorized models too. Hanwha is not on that list, and Hanwha Vision says its products are NDAA compliant and made in Vietnam and South Korea. So when a co-op board, a commercial lease or an institutional tenant says "no covered brands," Hanwha is one of the names that keeps coming up. Nobody is being forced to remove a working system, but the next camera on the wall is increasingly expected to be one nobody will question.

Some owners know the brand by an older name. Hanwha bought Samsung Techwin in 2015, renamed it Hanwha Techwin, and became Hanwha Vision in 2023. A super who asks for "the Samsung cameras" is usually asking for this family.

Match the series to the spot

Hanwha sorts its cameras into tiers. The Q Series is the budget line, X Series is the everyday workhorse, and P Series sits at the top with more analytics built into the camera. You don't need the top tier everywhere. A practical way to assign them in a typical building:

LocationWhat the camera has to doSensible tier
Stairwell landings, back hallwaysShow who passed and when; even, indoor lightQ Series is usually enough
Lobby, vestibule, mailroomIdentify faces against bright glass doors; catch package handlingX Series with good backlight handling
Front door or storefront facing the streetHeadlights, sun glare, night shadows, people at speedX or P Series, chosen view by view
Garage ramp, loading dock, wide yardLong distance, vehicles, low light, fewer false alertsP Series where on-camera analytics earn their cost
Elevator cabCompact, vandal-resistant, wide field of viewA small dome from whichever tier fits the budget

If you plan to switch on face or other biometric features in a store, restaurant or venue, remember the city's biometric signage rule for commercial establishments. In residential buildings, also think about tenants before turning on anything beyond plain recording.

Pick what records the video

This is where Hanwha quotes differ most. There are two common paths.

A Wisenet NVR

A dedicated recorder that powers the cameras over PoE and stores video on internal drives. Users on the r/videosurveillance forum point out that this route, viewed through Hanwha's Wisenet Viewer, avoids per-camera licensing, which makes it the value choice for walk-ups, brownstones, small stores and most apartment buildings.

Wisenet WAVE

Video management software running on a server. It brings more integrations, multi-site viewing and finer user control, and Abstract Enterprises Security Systems notes, citing Surveillant's Hanwha pricing guide, that each camera on WAVE needs a license bought once, not renewed every year. It fits property managers with several buildings, larger commercial sites, and anyone who wants cameras from more than one brand on one screen.

Either way, size the drives before you buy: camera count, resolution and how many days you must keep footage decide the storage. The calculator linked below helps.

What install day looks like in a city building

  1. Paperwork first. Boards, managing agents and commercial landlords typically ask for an insurance certificate, a written scope and approved work hours before the crew shows up. A freight elevator may need booking.
  2. Test the old cable. If a previous system ran on network cable, each run is tested. Clean runs can often carry a new Hanwha PoE camera, which saves the hardest labor in a masonry building. Old coax from an analog system generally has to be replaced.
  3. Preserve old footage. If an insurance claim or dispute is open, clips are exported before the old recorder is disconnected.
  4. Mount and aim. Each camera is set for the view agreed on the survey, with the light at that hour in mind.
  5. Configure. Fixed addresses, changed default passwords, current firmware, retention set, and remote viewing for the people the owner names.
  6. Label and hand over. Cables labelled at both ends, a port map left at the rack, and a walk-through with whoever runs the building.

The landmark question

New York has thousands of designated buildings and more than 150 historic districts, and the Landmarks Preservation Commission's rules treat security cameras as objects to keep off primary facades where possible. On a landmarked brownstone block, expect cameras to end up beneath the stoop, down in the areaway or at the back of the house, and choose small domes in a finish that blends in.

Questions to ask any Hanwha installer

  • Which series are you proposing at each position, and why that one?
  • NVR or WAVE, and if WAVE, how many licenses are on the quote as separate lines?
  • Will you test my existing cable before quoting new runs?
  • Will you list every device's make and model for a compliance file?
  • Who gets admin access at handover, and who gets view-only?

FAQ

Is Hanwha the same company as Samsung security cameras?

Yes, in lineage. Hanwha took over Samsung Techwin in 2015 and renamed it Hanwha Techwin, and in 2023 the business became Hanwha Vision. The Wisenet camera name carried through, so Samsung, Wisenet and Hanwha usually refer to the same family.

Are Hanwha cameras NDAA compliant?

Hanwha Vision states its cameras, components and systems are NDAA compliant and that it manufactures in Vietnam and South Korea. Hanwha is not on the FCC Covered List, unlike Hikvision and Dahua.

Do I need Wisenet WAVE, or is a Hanwha NVR enough?

For most buildings a Wisenet NVR viewed through Hanwha's own viewer is enough and carries no per-camera license. WAVE software suits sites that want a server, more integrations or multi-site management; it uses a one-time license per camera.

Do I have to rip out my Hikvision or Dahua cameras now?

No. The 2026 FCC actions restrict importing and marketing covered equipment; they do not require you to remove cameras already installed. Many owners replace in stages, unless a lease, insurer or contract sets a deadline.

Can Hanwha cameras go on a landmarked building in NYC?

Often, but placement matters. Landmarks Preservation Commission rules discourage cameras and similar hardware on the main street-facing facade of a designated building, so installers usually look to the stoop, the areaway or the rear.
