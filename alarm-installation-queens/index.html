Zone map: where the devices go in four Queens buildings

The door people skip is the one beside the driveway on a detached or semi-detached house. It is out of sight from the street and often left on a spring latch, so give it its own contact even if nobody in the family uses it. On an attached row, the party walls do the job on two sides; the front and the yard are where the effort goes.

One panel or two in a two-family?

Two households under one roof is the most common Queens arrangement, and it changes the design. Most professional panels can be split into partitions: the upstairs unit and the downstairs unit each arm and disarm their own area with their own codes, while the shared hallway and the cellar sit in a common area that arms only when both are away.

  • One partitioned panel: one monitoring account, usually in the owner's name, one bill, separate codes per household.
  • Two separate systems: each household has its own panel and its own monitoring. More hardware and two bills, but nobody depends on the other floor.
  • Tenant turnover: delete the departing tenant's code the day they leave, and give the new tenant a fresh one rather than passing the old code on.

Close to Jamaica Bay: keep the brains of the system dry

In Howard Beach, Hamilton Beach, Broad Channel and across the Rockaways, many cellars took water in Hurricane Sandy, and a panel screwed to the cellar wall can be lost in one storm. In those blocks, put the panel, the cellular communicator and the standby battery in a first-floor closet. Cover the cellar doors with sealed or wireless contacts, add a water sensor at the lowest point and a low-temperature sensor if the house sits empty in winter, and ask how many hours the standby battery is rated to run. Phone and cable lines can drop in a storm, which is why the signal path should be cellular.

Sirens, neighbors and the 15-minute cutoff

On an attached row or a two-family, your siren is your neighbor's siren too. The city's noise code (Administrative Code §24-238) requires a building's audible burglar alarm to shut itself off within fifteen minutes of going off, and neighbors can report an alarm that sounds too long or too often through 311. Three settings keep you out of that position:

  • An entry delay long enough to reach the keypad from the door you really use, often the side door.
  • Pet-immune motion detectors, or no motion zone at all where a dog sleeps.
  • A call list at the monitoring center that starts with someone who lives within a few minutes of the house.

Bought a house with a keypad already on the wall?

Queens homes change hands with old systems still in place. Before you pay for a new one, find the metal panel box (usually in the cellar near the electric meter) and note the brand. Ask the seller which company monitored it and whether the equipment was owned or leased; leased hardware stays with that company. If the panel is locked by a dealer code or still dials out over a landline, the usual fix is a new panel and cellular communicator on the existing wiring, which is the $800–$1,500 line above, rather than tearing out every contact.

Frequently asked questions

Does a Queens homeowner have to register or permit a burglar alarm?

Queens does not have the homeowner alarm permit that Nassau and Suffolk counties require. What does apply is the city noise rule that a building siren must stop on its own within 15 minutes, and New York State requires the installer to hold an alarm installer license.

Can a tenant in a Queens two-family put in an alarm?

Yes. A wireless system with adhesive contacts and a plug-in panel needs no drilling and leaves with the tenant. Anything wired into the building needs the owner to agree, and an owner can also give the tenant their own partition on a shared panel.

What happens to the alarm if the power or internet goes out?

A professionally installed panel keeps running on its standby battery, and a cellular communicator still sends signals to the monitoring center without internet or a phone line. Ask the installer how many hours your battery is rated for.

How much is alarm monitoring in Queens?

The listed Queens rates are $30 monthly for keypad-only service and $45 monthly when app control is added, month to month rather than a multi-year contract.

Will an alarm lower my homeowners insurance?

Plenty of carriers lower the premium when a central station watches the house. Call your insurer first and ask what proof it wants, usually a monitoring certificate, so the installer can provide it on install day.
