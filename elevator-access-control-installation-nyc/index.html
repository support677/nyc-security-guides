Elevator Access Control Installation NYC projects involve two trades in the same small cab. A low-voltage security contractor installs the readers, panel and credentials; the building's licensed elevator company makes the connection to the controller. Boards and managers who understand that split early get faster approvals and fewer surprises at sign-off. This guide explains what the system changes about a ride, who is allowed to do which part, the fire-service and privacy rules that shape the design, and the starting prices published for Elevator Access Control Installation NYC by Abstract Enterprises Security Systems.

What changes for the rider

There are three common ways to set it up, and the choice is mostly about how much friction residents or tenants will accept:

  • Floor select only. Anyone can ride, but buttons for restricted floors stay dark until a fob, card or phone is presented in the cab. The lobby and any shared floors stay free.
  • Hall call control. A reader at the landing must be used before the car can even be called, which is typical for a garage, cellar or amenity level.
  • Time schedules. Floors are open during business hours and locked after, common in offices and medical buildings.

Residents on a homeowners' forum describe the same trade-off: requiring a fob to call the car is more secure but more annoying for guests, aides and deliveries, while restricting floor selection keeps the lobby easy. Most residential buildings land on floor-select control with a free lobby and a staff override.

Who is allowed to touch what

Since January 1, 2022, New York State has required anyone performing elevator work, including installation, alteration and repair, to hold a license from the State Department of Labor. A security installer who is not a licensed elevator mechanic should not be wiring into the controller. The practical division looks like this:

Security contractorLicensed elevator company
Readers in the car or at the landing, keypads, mobile credential readersTie-in of floor-enable signals at the controller or car panel
Access panel, relay or interface board, power supply with batteryAny new conductor in the traveling cable, or a wireless link in the shaft
Cable from the reader to the interface pointTesting fire service and independent service with the new inputs
Credentials, floor groups, schedules, intercom and camera linksAny filing or sign-off the elevator work needs

Ask your elevator maintenance company for a price on their half early. It is billed separately and can be the slowest part of the schedule.

Fire service has to win, every time

In New York City, elevators are governed by the ASME A17.1 safety code with the city's own amendments in its building code. When firefighters' emergency operation is triggered by a lobby smoke detector or a recall key, the cars go to the recall floor and normal service ends. An access system must not interfere with that, so the restriction relays have to drop out when emergency operation starts. Settle three things in writing before installation:

  1. What the floors do if the access panel loses power.
  2. Where the manual override is and who holds the key or login.
  3. How a resident in a wheelchair reaches the reader from inside the car.

Fobs in an apartment building: Local Law 63

If the building is a residential multiple dwelling and uses fobs, cards, apps or biometrics for entry or common areas, the city's Tenant Data Privacy Law (Local Law 63 of 2021) applies. According to HPD, owners must give tenants a privacy and data retention policy, get express consent before collecting reference data, collect only the minimum needed, and destroy authentication data within 90 days unless it is anonymized. The law also bars using the system to track tenants outside the building or to harass or evict them, and it bars requiring a smart credential to enter a tenant's own apartment. For a co-op or rental, that usually means card or fob readers in the elevator rather than face or fingerprint readers, and a short ride log.

A sequence that keeps the cars running

  1. Survey with the super and, ideally, the elevator mechanic: controller type, spare conductors, machine room space and power.
  2. Agree the floor groups, schedules, override and power-loss behavior with the board or owner.
  3. Install the panel and cable in the machine room or riser while cars stay in service.
  4. Take one car out at a time for the reader and the controller tie-in.
  5. Test fire recall with the elevator company before the car returns to service.
  6. Enroll credentials, then hand over the admin login and a written override procedure.

Questions a board should put to any installer

  • Which licensed elevator company will make the controller connection, and have you worked with them before?
  • Will the system use the same credentials as our front door, or a second platform?
  • What is the recurring software or license cost per year, if any?
  • How will you meet Local Law 63 in our building?
  • Can each line of the quote be shown separately, including the elevator company's part?

FAQ

Can a fob be required to call the elevator, or only to choose a floor?

Both are possible. Hall-call readers require a credential before the car can be called; floor-select control lets anyone ride but keeps restricted floor buttons inactive until a valid credential is presented in the cab.

Does our elevator company have to be involved?

Yes. In New York State, work on the elevator itself requires a Department of Labor elevator license, so the controller connection and fire-service testing are done by the licensed elevator company while the security contractor handles readers, panels and programming.

What happens to elevator access control during a fire?

When firefighters' emergency operation begins, the elevator must ignore the access system. The interface is designed so the restriction drops out and the cars respond to recall.

Does Local Law 63 apply to elevator fobs in an apartment building?

If the building is a residential multiple dwelling using smart access for entry or common areas, the Tenant Data Privacy Law applies, including a privacy policy, consent, minimal data collection and deletion of authentication data within 90 days.

What does elevator access control cost in NYC?

Published starting prices run from $1,500 per car in Brooklyn and the Bronx to $1,875 per car in Manhattan for a card, fob or keypad reader, and from $2,500 per car for a biometric reader. Floor-by-floor control is quoted after a survey.
