Ubiquiti Camera Installation Long Island projects are mostly about distance. A UniFi camera on a Manhattan storefront might sit thirty feet of cable from the recorder; on a Nassau corner lot or a Suffolk property with a pool house and a long driveway, the far cameras can be several hundred feet away, across lawn, pavers and sometimes a second building. Get the cable plan right and UniFi Protect is a calm, subscription-free system that runs for years. Get it wrong and you spend the first winter chasing cameras that drop offline. Below is how we think through a Long Island property before quoting it; the published rates and the quote form are on the Ubiquiti Camera Installation Long Island page.

The 100-meter rule and the far end of the lot

A single Ethernet run, which carries both data and PoE power to a UniFi camera, is good for about 100 meters, roughly 328 feet, including the slack inside the walls. Measured along the path the cable actually takes, not in a straight line, that limit arrives sooner than people expect on a bigger property.

Running copper between two buildings has a catch beyond length: separate buildings can sit at slightly different ground potential, and a lightning strike nearby can travel along the cable. Fiber between buildings avoids that entirely, which is why it is our default for a garage or pool house that will carry several cameras.

Weather, salt and lightning near the water

A lot of Long Island sits within a few miles of salt water, from the South Shore canals to the North Fork. Salt air eats cheap hardware, and summer storms come in fast. The details that keep an outdoor UniFi camera working:

  • Stainless or coated mounting screws, not plain zinc, on anything facing the water.
  • Junction boxes under turrets and bullets so the cable connection is sealed, with a drip loop so rain runs off before it reaches the connector.
  • Surge protection on long outdoor runs where they enter the house, bonded to the building ground.
  • Cameras tucked under soffits and eaves where possible, out of direct rain and the worst of the afternoon glare.

Where the Protect console should sit

UniFi Protect runs on a console, which can be a Cloud Key, a UniFi NVR or one of the Dream Machine routers, with the hard drive inside it. In a typical Long Island house that means the basement or a utility room near where the internet comes in. Three things decide the exact spot: it should be dry (finished basements near the South Shore can take water in a heavy storm, so keep it off the floor), it should be on a battery backup along with the PoE switch, and it should not be somewhere an intruder will see on the way in. A recorder that walks out the door with the burglar takes the footage with it.

Seasonal homes on the East End and North Fork

A summer house that sits empty from October to May is the place UniFi earns its keep, because the owner checks it from the city. That only works if the system can tell you when something is wrong while nobody is there:

  1. Turn on alerts for a camera or the console going offline, not just for motion.
  2. Set the console and switch to come back on by themselves after a power cut, and test it by pulling the plug.
  3. Consider a cellular backup for the internet so a dead cable line does not blind the whole system.
  4. Give a caretaker or neighbor limited view access in the Protect app, with their own login.
  5. Look at the recordings once in a while in the off-season; a full or failing drive is easy to miss when nobody is watching.

What it costs on Long Island

The Long Island page puts a UniFi system at roughly $480–$850 per camera installed, with the recorder, cable, setup and the phone app all counted in, and it puts a whole-house perimeter at about $3,200 to $6,500. Large lots, long runs and East End addresses push the number up. Recording to the console itself carries no subscription. Final pricing follows a free site visit, so treat those figures as a planning range rather than a quote.

How a typical install day runs

  1. Walk the property with the owner and mark each camera's view on a phone screenshot, not just on the wall.
  2. Set the console and PoE switch first, update firmware, and adopt one test camera.
  3. Pull and terminate cable, starting with the longest runs so any surprises show up early.
  4. Mount and aim cameras, then set detection zones for people and vehicles so the yard's trees and the street do not trigger alerts all night.
  5. Set retention, recording schedules and user accounts, then walk the owner through finding and exporting a clip on their own phone.

Frequently asked questions

How far can a UniFi PoE camera be from the switch?

About 100 meters, or 328 feet, of cable in a single Ethernet run. Past that, or into a separate building, you add a switch at the far end fed by fiber or a wireless bridge.

Can I put UniFi cameras on a detached garage or pool house?

Yes. The usual setup is a small PoE switch in the outbuilding, connected back to the house by fiber or a UniFi wireless bridge, with the cameras wired to that switch. Wired PoE cameras are the more dependable choice outdoors.

Do UniFi cameras have a monthly fee?

Not for recording at home. Video lives on the drive inside the Protect console, and you watch it in the UniFi Protect app at no charge.

How do I watch an empty summer house with UniFi Protect?

Enable offline alerts for the console and cameras, make sure everything restarts after a power outage, put the network on battery backup, and think about a cellular internet backup so one dead line does not take the whole system offline.

Roughly what will a UniFi system cost me on Long Island?

The Long Island page lists roughly $480–$850 per camera installed and a full home perimeter often around $3,200–$6,500, with large lots and East End properties higher. The exact figure comes after a free walk-through of the property.
