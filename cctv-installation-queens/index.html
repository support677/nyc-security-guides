CCTV installation Queens jobs usually start with an incident rather than a shopping list: someone trying car doors at 3 a.m., a package gone from a shared stoop, a register that came up short. Before comparing cameras, decide what the footage has to prove, whether that is a face at the door, a plate in the driveway or a hand at the till, because that choice sets the resolution, the lens and where each camera goes. The published packages for CCTV Installation Queens are wired systems with a recorder on site and no monthly fee. Here is how to choose among them and what to settle before install day.

Old coax in the walls: keep it or start over?

Plenty of Queens houses and storefronts already carry an older analog system: cameras that went up years ago, a DVR in the cellar, black coax stapled along the joists. That cable is worth testing before you decide anything. HD-over-coax recorders, the type in the A, F and C packages, can often run on sound existing coax, which spares you pulling new cable through a finished basement or plaster walls. IP systems such as the E and D packages need network cable, usually Cat6, carrying both data and power.

The driveway question

Car theft is a real worry in the driveway neighborhoods of eastern and southeastern Queens. In May 2025 the Queens District Attorney announced indictments against a crew accused of stealing 126 vehicles, and 52 of those were taken in Queens. Cameras won't stop a determined thief, but they decide whether police have anything to work with afterward.

  • Aim one camera along the driveway at roughly head height so it records faces, not just the tops of hoods.
  • Add a tighter view at the curb cut to catch a plate as a car turns in or out. This is where the 4K packages earn their price.
  • Watch the light. A camera staring into a streetlight or headlight path records glare; mount it so light falls on the subject, not into the lens.
  • Keep views on your own property and the street. Aiming into a neighbor's windows or yard starts arguments that are hard to undo.

Where the recorder should live

The recorder holds the evidence, so place it with that in mind. In a house the cellar is the natural spot because cable runs are short, but southeast Queens and the Rockaways know what standing water does to electronics. Put the recorder on a shelf well above the floor, away from the water heater, and on a small battery backup so a power blip doesn't interrupt recording. In a store, keep it in a locked back office rather than under the counter, where whoever grabs the cash can grab the recorder too. In a two-family, agree before go-live who holds the admin login, usually the owner, and whether the tenant gets a view-only account for the shared entrance.

Storage is easy to underestimate. The A Series is set for roughly two weeks, which works if you review footage soon after an incident. If you travel often, or a landlord or insurer may ask for older video, plan for more. The security camera storage calculator shows how camera count, resolution and days kept translate into drive size.

Install day, in order

  1. Walk the property together and confirm each camera position and what it should see.
  2. Test any existing coax or network cable that might be reused.
  3. Mount the recorder and battery backup, then run and terminate the cables.
  4. Mount and aim the cameras, checking both daylight and infrared views on the recorder screen.
  5. Set recording schedules, motion areas and retention, then load the phone app on your devices.
  6. Hand over the camera map, cable labels, recorder model and drive size, and the admin account details in person.

Ask for that last step on paper. A camera map kept beside the recorder saves time on every later service call, and the admin password should never live only with the installer.

What Queens owners ask about CCTV

How much does CCTV installation cost in Queens?

Four-camera packages on the Queens page start at $950 for the 1080p A Series and reach $1,392 for the 4K C or D Series. Four to eight cameras with an NVR typically run $1,062 to $2,500, and business systems begin around $1,500 and can pass $5,000, with no monthly fee.

Can I keep the coax cable from my old cameras?

Often, yes. HD-over-coax DVR systems can usually reuse coax that is intact and reachable at both ends. Cable that is crushed, water-damaged or cut is better replaced, and IP camera systems need network cable instead.

Will I pay a monthly fee after the install?

Not for these systems. Footage records to the recorder at your property and you view it in the app, so there is no subscription.

Can my cameras look at the street or my neighbor's property?

Covering your own entrances, driveway and the public sidewalk is common. Avoid aiming into a neighbor's windows or yard; it is a privacy problem and a quick way to start a dispute.

What warranty comes with a CCTV installation?

The page lists a 30-day warranty on the work, with longer coverage of one year or more available as a paid add-on. The service agreement has the full terms.
