Night Owl Camera Installation NYC: Four Checks Before You Drill, Then a Clean Install Day

By Abstract Enterprises Security Systems — licensed NYS low-voltage contractor, in security since 2000 · Updated 2026-11-11

Night Owl Camera Installation NYC is a search that usually starts after the purchase. Night Owl markets its wired systems for self-installation, and its representatives have said on retailer question pages that the company does not install them. So the box arrives, the cameras come out, and the owner of a Brooklyn rowhouse or a Bronx deli discovers that the hard part is not the app. It is getting cable from each camera to the recorder through brick, plaster and somebody else's hallway. This guide covers the four checks to make before you drill, what happens on a professional install day, and what to do if a system that is already up stops recording. The model number on the recorder settles it. This matters most in older stores and walk-ups that already have coax from a previous system. A DVR kit may be able to use it; a PoE kit cannot, so the job becomes a cabling job.

Check 2: measure the real route, not the straight line

Abstract Enterprises Security Systems notes that Night Owl ships its BNC kits with a 60-foot cable per camera and says extra cable can stretch a run to 160 feet. In a city building the route is never the distance on the floor plan. From a stoop camera to a cellar recorder the cable may climb the facade, enter through a window frame, cross the basement ceiling and detour around the boiler. Walk the route with a tape measure or a length of string before you decide which cameras the included cable will reach.

If you need more length on a BNC kit, Night Owl's answers on retailer pages describe joining extension cables with BNC couplers. That works, but each joint is another place for water and corrosion outdoors, so keep couplers indoors and sealed where possible. On a PoE kit, cable cut to the actual length is cleaner than chaining patch cords.

Check 3: confirm who owns each wall

  • Renters control the inside of the apartment. Hallways, lobbies, facades and yards belong to the landlord, and cameras there need permission.
  • Co-op and condo owners usually need board or managing-agent approval for anything on a common wall, and drilling may fall under the alteration rules in the proprietary lease.
  • Owners in historic districts should check Landmarks Preservation Commission rules before mounting on a primary facade.
  • Shop tenants should confirm with the landlord before drilling a storefront or running cable into the cellar.

Check 4: decide where the recorder lives

The kit assumes the recorder sits next to your router. That is convenient, but not required. Night Owl's support material notes that the recorder keeps recording without internet; the internet connection is only for viewing on your phone. Pick a spot that is out of sight to a burglar, ventilated, near power and as close as practical to the middle of the camera layout, then run one network cable from there to the router. Shortening the longest camera run is often worth more than any accessory.

Also think about storage before install day. The hard drive in the box fills and overwrites on a cycle that depends on camera count, resolution and motion settings. The free security camera storage calculator gives a rough idea of how many days a given drive will hold.

What happens on a professional install day

  1. Bench test. Each camera gets plugged into the recorder and checked on the floor first, so a faulty unit turns up before it is bolted to brick.
  2. Mark positions. Each camera spot is confirmed against what it needs to show: a face at the door, a package on the stoop, a plate in the driveway.
  3. Drill and run. Masonry holes are made with the right bit and sealed afterward; cable is run and fastened along the agreed route.
  4. Mount and aim. Cameras go up, then get aimed at night as well as by day, because a porch light or streetlamp can blind a lens after dark.
  5. Recorder setup. Date and time, recording schedule, motion zones and the drive are configured.
  6. App and handover. The Night Owl app is linked to the owner's phone and the owner is shown how to find and export a clip.

If a system that is already up stops recording

When something has just been stolen, the instinct is to reboot everything. Resist it. Do not factory-reset the recorder, do not keep power-cycling it and do not pull the drive, because the footage you need may be overwritten or lost. Check the recorder's own screen first; an empty app screen is not proof the recorder missed the event. Then call a technician and describe the model and what changed.

Frequently asked questions

Does Night Owl install its own camera systems?

No. Night Owl's representatives have said on retailer question pages that installation is not included and is not offered by the company, so owners either install the kit themselves or hire a local installer.

Can I extend the BNC cables that came with my Night Owl kit?

Yes, with BNC extension cables joined by couplers, and Night Owl says runs can reach up to 160 feet per camera. Outdoors, keep joints sealed or indoors, since each coupler is a point where moisture can get in.

Does a Night Owl system keep recording if the internet goes down?

Yes. The recorder stores video on its own hard drive. The internet connection is only needed to view cameras and get alerts on your phone.

Can my old coax camera wiring work with a new Night Owl system?

Only with a DVR kit that uses BNC cable. Night Owl's PoE NVR systems need Ethernet, so coax left from an older system will not carry them.

Can I put a Night Owl camera in my apartment building hallway?

Usually not without the landlord's or board's permission, because the hallway is a common area. A camera inside your own unit or a doorbell-style view from your door is a better starting point.
