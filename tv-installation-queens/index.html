TV Installation Queens quotes swing on one thing most people only learn when a drill bit tells them: what the wall is made of. Queens puts poured concrete in Long Island City, plaster on wood lath in Jackson Heights, brick chimney breasts in Forest Hills and plain drywall over wood studs in Bayside, sometimes within a 20-minute drive of each other. Each one takes a different anchor, a different time on the wall and a different line on the bill. Below are five common Queens rooms, a way to price your own job from the published rates, and what to have ready before the crew arrives. Booking and the live price builder are on the TV Installation Queens page.

Five Queens rooms, five mounting plans

1. The Long Island City tower living room

Newer towers mix poured-concrete walls with metal-stud partitions. Concrete takes sleeve anchors and a masonry bit; metal studs need snap toggles, or a plywood backer board when the TV is large. Floor-to-ceiling glass throws glare across the screen, so a tilt mount often beats a fixed one. Management will ask for a certificate of insurance and a freight elevator booking.

2. The Jackson Heights or Sunnyside pre-war co-op

Plaster over wood lath is hard, uneven and confuses stud finders, a complaint that comes up again and again from NYC renters trying it themselves. The fix is toggle bolts through the lath or a backer board that spreads the load. A flat or tilt mount puts less leverage on plaster than a long full-motion arm.

3. The Forest Hills Tudor or Kew Gardens fireplace wall

Here the TV wants to go on a brick or stone chimney breast. Anchors go into solid brick, not the mortar joints. The mantel pushes the screen high, so a tilt or pull-down mount brings it back toward eye level. Before you commit, run the fireplace for an hour and feel the wall where the TV will hang. If it is hot to the touch, use the next wall over.

4. The Bayside or Fresh Meadows family room

Drywall on wood studs is the easy case and carries no wall surcharge. These rooms are big enough for 85-inch and larger screens, which take a two-person crew. In a house with an unfinished basement below, cables can drop through the floor to a cabinet instead of running down the wall.

5. The Astoria or Ridgewood rental

Older row buildings often have a brick party wall behind the plaster. If you rent, get the landlord's okay in writing and plan for move-out: patching and painting old holes is not part of the mounting price.

Wires: inside the wall, on the wall, or off to a cabinet

On hollow drywall, in-wall concealment hides the HDMI and power behind the screen. In New York City, power inside a wall has to run in armored cable rather than the plastic-sheathed cable sold at big-box stores, which is why the concealment kit includes a code-compliant power kit. Brick and concrete cannot be fished, so the choice there is a paintable raceway on the surface or a run of up to 25 feet to a cabinet (+$99). Adding or moving an outlet is electrical work and is not part of a TV mounting visit.

The 48-hour checklist

  • TV and bracket on site, and the bracket rated for the TV's size and weight.
  • Certificate of insurance requested from the managing agent, with the agent's exact wording.
  • Elevator or freight booking confirmed for the arrival window.
  • Wall area cleared, and heavy furniture moved out of the way.
  • A list of what plugs in: cable box, streaming stick, console, soundbar.

The policies ask for building approvals, elevator booking and the COI at least 48 hours ahead, and a $150 return-trip fee applies if the crew cannot start because something is missing.

Frequently asked questions

Can a TV go on plaster walls in a pre-war Queens co-op if I can't find the studs?

Yes. Toggle bolts through the plaster and lath, or a plywood backer board that spreads the load, hold a TV without relying on studs. A flat or tilt mount is kinder to plaster than a long full-motion arm.

Does my co-op or condo need a certificate of insurance before the installer comes?

Most managed buildings do. Ask the managing agent for their exact wording at least 48 hours ahead; the Queens price builder lists the COI as free.

Can a TV hang over a fireplace that I actually use?

It can be if the wall stays cool. Run the fireplace for an hour and touch the spot; if it is hot, choose another wall. Use a tilt or pull-down mount so the screen is not too high to watch.

How high should a wall-mounted TV be?

About 42 to 48 inches from the floor to the center of the screen for normal seating. Over a fireplace, a tilt or pull-down mount compensates for the extra height.

Can wires be hidden in a brick or concrete wall?

Not inside the wall. On brick and concrete the options are a paintable raceway on the surface or a cable run to a nearby cabinet.

Can someone mount my TV today in Queens?

Same-day and next-day appointments are offered in Queens when the schedule allows. Have the TV, bracket and any building paperwork ready so the visit is not lost.
