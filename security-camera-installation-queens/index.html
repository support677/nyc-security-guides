Security Camera Installation Queens jobs change more from block to block than almost anywhere else in the city. The borough has attached brick two-families in Ridgewood and Woodside, detached houses with driveways in Bayside, Douglaston and Howard Beach, garden co-ops in Jackson Heights and Forest Hills, and miles of storefronts along Roosevelt Avenue, Main Street and Jamaica Avenue. Each of those needs a different number of cameras, a different cable route and a different person's approval. Attached houses: aim before you mount

On an attached row, your front camera sees the sidewalk, the stoop next door and part of the street, and your rear camera often looks over a shared yard or a common alley. That is normal, but aim each camera at your own entry, driveway or yard and away from a neighbor's windows. Most recorders let you black out part of the image with a privacy mask, which is the easy fix when a corner of the frame lands on the house next door. Microphones are worth a second thought too: New York's eavesdropping statute covers capturing other people's private talk, which is why plenty of Queens owners switch off the microphone on street-facing cameras.

Two-family owners who rent one unit should also think about the tenant. Cover the shared entrance, stairs and yard, keep cameras out of the tenant's own space, and tell them where the cameras are.

Who has to say yes

  • You own the house: no permit is needed for a standard low-voltage camera system, and the decision is yours.
  • You rent: get the landlord's written OK before anything is drilled into the facade. Inside your own apartment, a plug-in camera is a different conversation.
  • Co-op or condo: the board or managing agent usually wants an alteration request, a certificate of insurance and a description of where cables will run. Abstract Enterprises Security Systems provides that paperwork.
  • Landmarked block: parts of Jackson Heights, Ridgewood and other Queens neighborhoods are historic districts, where exterior changes to the facade can need review, so keep mounts discreet and ask before drilling on the street side.

Getting the cable from the basement to the front of the house

Most Queens houses have a basement or cellar, and that is where the recorder and the PoE switch usually go. From there, cable runs up through closets or along the joists to the front and rear walls, and exits through a small hole behind each camera mount. On brick, mounts are anchored into the masonry and any exterior run goes in a color-matched cover rather than stapled across the face of the house. Wired PoE is the default because brick and plaster walls weaken Wi-Fi between floors, and a single run to each camera handles power as well as the picture.

South Queens: keep the recorder above the water line

In Howard Beach, Broad Channel and the Rockaways, a basement recorder is a risk. Storm surge and heavy rain have flooded cellars there before, and a recorder sitting on the basement floor takes your footage with it. In those neighborhoods, put the recorder on the first floor or on a high shelf, put it on a battery backup, and use outdoor cameras and junction boxes rated for wet weather near the water.

A typical install day in a two-family

  1. Walk the house with the owner and confirm each camera's view on a phone or tablet before drilling.
  2. Set the recorder and switch in the basement or on the first floor, near an outlet and the router.
  3. Pull cable to the front, rear and side positions, and seal every wall penetration.
  4. Mount and aim the cameras, add privacy masks where a frame reaches next door.
  5. Set up recording, motion alerts and the phone app, and show you how to find and export a clip.

Queens owners and renters ask

What does a camera system cost to install in Queens?

Installed house-brand packages start at $950. The Queens page lists $1,062 to $2,500 for a typical 4–8 camera home system and $1,500 to $5,000 and up for commercial systems.

Do I need a permit for security cameras on my Queens house?

Not for a standard low-voltage system on a house you own. Renters need the landlord's approval, co-op owners need the board's, and historic district facades can need extra review.

Can my camera see my neighbor's property?

Some overlap with the street or a shared yard is hard to avoid. Aim at your own entries and use privacy masks so the camera does not look into a neighbor's windows.

Are wired or wireless cameras better for a Queens brick house?

Wired PoE. Brick and plaster weaken Wi-Fi between floors, and a cable to each camera delivers power and a steady signal.

Where should the recorder go in a flood-prone part of Queens?

Above the basement floor. In Howard Beach, Broad Channel and the Rockaways, put it on the first floor or a high shelf with a battery backup.

How long does a Queens home installation take?

Most residential installs take four to eight hours, finished in one visit with phone viewing set up before the crew leaves.
