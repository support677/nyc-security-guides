First Alert Camera Installation Hudson Valley usually goes wrong in two places nobody looks at while shopping for cameras: the monthly video plan and the internet line that feeds it. First Alert VX cameras from Resideo keep their clips in the Total Connect cloud, not on a box in your basement, so a camera on a Dutchess barn or a Putnam lake house is only as good as the upload speed at that spot and the plan it is enrolled on. This guide walks through matching each spot to a model, doing the plan math before you buy, and building an outage plan into the job. Why the cloud matters more north of the city

In a Westchester village near the train line, a long internet outage is rare. Farther up the Taconic, it is not. Many valley roads are served by a single line strung on poles, and an ice storm, a fallen oak or a snowplow hitting a pole can take it out while the house still has power. A First Alert camera that cannot reach the cloud cannot save a new clip, so the camera keeps its light on and records nothing. That is not a defect; it is how cloud cameras work. It just means the network gear, the upload speed and a battery behind the router deserve as much attention as the camera itself.

Do the plan math before you buy the fifth camera

First Alert video plans are counted in clips and days, not hard-drive terabytes. Dealer documentation describes a 7-day plan holding up to 350 clips and a 30-day plan holding up to 1,500, with each package covering up to four VX cameras. Two things follow from that:

  • Camera five costs more than the camera. A sixth or seventh camera usually means a second package every month. It is often smarter to start with four well-placed cameras on the doors, the driveway and the garage, and add the barn and gate later.
  • Busy views eat clips. A camera that watches a county road, a working farm gate or trees moving in the wind can burn through a clip allowance fast. Tight motion zones and person-only alerts keep the clips for things that matter.

If you want weeks of continuous footage from many cameras on a large lot, a cloud-clip system may simply be the wrong tool, and a recorder-based system is worth pricing alongside it.

Build an outage plan into the install

  1. Put the network on a battery. A small UPS behind the modem, router and any PoE switch rides through the short power blips that are common in storm season, so the cameras do not drop off every time the lights flicker.
  2. Know what the alarm panel does on its own. If your alarm reports over a cellular communicator, the burglary and fire signals keep working even when the cameras cannot upload. Cameras add evidence; they are not the alarm.
  3. Ask your provider about a backup path. For a second home or a business that needs footage during an outage, a cellular backup internet connection is the real fix.
  4. Test the recovery. After a planned power-off, every camera should come back online by itself. If one needs a manual restart, that spot needs a stronger signal or a cable.

Weekend houses: a checklist before you drive back to the city

  • Check live view on your phone over cellular data, not the house Wi-Fi, before you leave.
  • Set the indoor VX5 shutter schedule so it watches when the house is empty and closes when you are there.
  • Give a local neighbor or caretaker a user login, so someone nearby can check a clip after a storm.
  • If you rent the house out, tell guests where the cameras are and keep indoor cameras out of bedrooms and bathrooms.
  • Note who to call if the internet line goes down, since the cameras will go quiet until it is repaired.

What happens on install day

We confirm the Total Connect account and who the dealer of record is, then walk every planned mount with a signal meter and a speed test. Mounts that fail get a cable or a wired access point before any camera goes up. On fieldstone or brick we use a back box and conduit rather than chiseling a channel, and we seal every penetration against frost. Frequently asked questions

Will First Alert cameras record if the internet goes out?

No. Every VX camera uploads its clips to the Total Connect cloud, so while the connection is down there is nowhere to save new footage, even with power on. A battery backup for the modem and router covers short outages; a cellular backup connection covers long ones.

Why did my old Honeywell Total Connect cameras stop working?

Resideo ended support for its first-generation Total Connect IP cameras, which quit working on the platform once July 31, 2025 passed. The recommended swap is the First Alert VX line: a VX5 where an old indoor model hung, a VX3 where the old outdoor one did.

How many cameras fit on one First Alert video plan?

Dealer documentation lists up to four VX cameras per video package, so a property with five to eight cameras typically needs a second package. Plans are also limited by clip count, such as up to 350 clips on the 7-day plan.

What internet speed do First Alert VX cameras need?

Resideo asks for a continuous upload of at least 2 Mbps and a Wi-Fi signal of -65 dBm or better at the mount. Test at the exact spot the camera will hang, not at the router.

Can a First Alert camera cover a barn or gate far from the house?

Yes, usually on network cable with PoE. A single run is good for about 328 feet, so for a far gate the cable often starts from the barn or another building closer to it instead of the house.

Can my VX cameras show up on the alarm touchscreen?

Yes, on supported Resideo touchscreens. The PROA7PLUS and PROA7PLUSC can display VX3 video once they are on firmware 03 or later, which lets the keypad by the door show the driveway view.
