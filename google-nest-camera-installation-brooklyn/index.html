Google Nest Camera Installation Brooklyn jobs make the most sense when you walk the house from the bottom up, because in a tall, narrow Brooklyn house the trouble usually starts in the cellar and shows up three floors later at the stoop. Below is that walk, floor by floor, followed by how to set up Google Home when a house has more than one household, what the published price lines are, and what to do with older Nest devices still on the old app.

Start in the cellar: where the internet lands

In most brownstones and brick rowhouses the internet service comes in at the cellar or garden level, often near the front. That spot is convenient for the cable company and bad for Nest cameras. Every outdoor Nest Cam and every Nest doorbell is a Wi-Fi device, and the signal has to climb through floors and push back through long rooms with brick party walls on both sides.

The cellar has a second problem: water. During Hurricane Ida in 2021, rain in New York fell far faster than the sewers could take it, and southern Brooklyn is among the parts of the city flagged as most flood-prone. A router sitting on a cellar floor takes every camera offline the moment it gets wet. Lifting it onto a shelf, or moving it to a central spot on the parlor floor, is often the cheapest improvement in the whole job.

Parlor floor and stoop: the doorbell

The front door of a brownstone sits a full flight above the sidewalk. A doorbell mounted at that door looks down on the tops of heads and may not see the bottom step, which is where couriers leave packages. Owners usually choose one of two fixes: tilt the doorbell with a wedge so it sees further down the stoop, or add a second, compact camera tucked below the stoop or inside the areaway to cover the bottom steps.

Power is the next question. Many Brooklyn front doors have no working bell wire at all, or an old button wired to a buzzer system that cannot power a modern doorbell. That points to a battery Nest doorbell, or to running proper low-voltage power, which is more work on a masonry front. In a walk-up, the street door and its buzzer panel belong to the building, so a tenant's Nest doorbell normally goes at the apartment door, or is replaced by an indoor Nest Cam looking at the inside of it.

If the house is in one of Brooklyn's historic districts, the front facade is also the Landmarks Preservation Commission's business. The Commission's guidance prefers low-key locations, such as beneath the stoop or out of sight inside the areaway, with screws going into mortar joints or wood trim and never into carved stone.

Upper floors and the rear wall

An outdoor Nest Cam on the back of the house looking down the yard is the second most common request after the doorbell. The rear wall is usually plain brick, which is easier to work with than the front, but it is also the furthest point from a front-cellar router. Before a camera goes up there, check the signal at that exact spot with the camera or a phone in hand.

Signal at the camera spotUsual fix
Strong and steadyMount the camera as planned
Weak but usableMove the router higher and more central, or add a mesh point on the floor nearest the camera
Drops in the evening or disappearsA wired access point near the back of the house, fed by a network cable from the router

Indoor Nest Cams are simpler. Many just sit on a shelf. Where one needs to be fixed high on plaster, it needs anchors that reach past the plaster to the brick or lath behind, and the cord should run neatly along the trim rather than being chased into an old wall.

Two households, one front door: setting up Google Home

A large share of Brooklyn homes are in two- and three-family buildings, and many have one stoop and one front door for everybody. A Nest doorbell belongs to one Google Home account. Decide before installation who owns that account, whether the upstairs tenant is added as a member so they can answer their own visitors, and whether each household's indoor cameras live in their own separate home in the app. Mixing them means one family's alerts and clip history land on the other family's phones.

Renters generally need the landlord's written approval for anything outdoors or anything that involves drilling. Audio is another setting to decide deliberately; on cameras that face shared hallways or the sidewalk, many owners switch audio recording off.

Old Nest app devices and the subscription question

If you inherited a house with older Nest cameras, they may still be on the original Nest app. Google's support pages describe transferring older Nest cameras and doorbells to the Google Home app, and once moved they are managed there. It is worth doing during a service visit, because it is also the moment to remove a previous owner's account and set up yours. Video history beyond the short free event window is a paid Google plan, now sold as Google Home Premium; check what your devices include before you buy one.

Frequently asked questions

Why does my Nest camera on the back of my Brooklyn rowhouse keep going offline?

Usually distance and brick. The router is often in the cellar at the front, and the back wall sits at the edge of the signal. Moving the router higher and more central, or adding a wired access point near the back, normally fixes it.

Can I put a Nest doorbell on my brownstone stoop if there is no doorbell wire?

Yes. A battery Nest doorbell needs no wire. A wired model needs proper low-voltage power, which is more work on a masonry front, and an old buzzer button usually cannot power it.

How do two households share one Nest doorbell?

The doorbell belongs to one Google Home account. The owner can add the other household as a member so they can answer their visitors, while each household keeps its indoor cameras in its own home in the app.

What does Google Nest camera installation cost in Brooklyn?

A visit that only pairs and tunes cameras already mounted is $225 for its first hour on the published Brooklyn list, then billed in 30-minute blocks. Mounts on brick or stone add $65, plaster adds $45, and a paintable cord cover is $59. Devices are priced per item from the full Brooklyn list.

My older Nest cameras are still on the Nest app. Should I move them?

Google supports transferring older Nest cameras and doorbells to the Google Home app. Doing it during a visit is also the moment to remove a previous owner's account and set up your own.
