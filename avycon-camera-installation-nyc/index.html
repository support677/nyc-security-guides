AVYCON Camera Installation NYC jobs succeed or fail on choices made before a single camera leaves its box. AVYCON builds network video recorders that feed each camera power and video over one network cable. That is a dependable design, but in a city full of prewar masonry, co-op boards and landmark blocks, the recorder location, the cable route and the phone app deserve more of your attention than the camera spec sheet. This guide covers those choices so you can brief any installer, and Abstract Enterprises Security Systems keeps an AVYCON Camera Installation NYC listing with prices set out separately for each borough.

Three decisions that matter more than the camera model

1. Where the recorder will live

Every camera cable runs back to the recorder, so its spot fixes how long each run is and how many walls it crosses. Good homes for it: a locked cellar room, a back office, or the super's closet, with a steady outlet, some airflow and a connection to the router. A standard network cable run tops out around 100 meters (328 feet); a roof camera on a long walk-up, or a second building across a yard, may need a PoE switch partway.

2. How much power the recorder can hand out

A PoE recorder has a power budget shared by all its ports. Ordinary fixed cameras sip power; cameras with heaters, strong infrared or motorized zoom draw more. Ask for the budget in writing and how close your planned cameras come to it. Running near the ceiling is how you get cameras that reboot on cold nights.

3. Who needs the app, and with what rights

An owner, a super and a managing agent rarely need the same access. Separate logins with limited permissions are safer than one shared password taped inside the closet door, and they make it easy to cut off someone who leaves.

Install day in a five-story walk-up

Picture a prewar walk-up with no shafts, an owner who dislikes exposed wire and a super who holds the keys. A sensible day runs like this:

  1. Confirm roof, cellar and yard access with the super, and the working hours the building allows.
  2. Set the recorder and battery backup in the cellar near the internet connection.
  3. Run cable up the stair hall in a slim raceway painted to match, or through an old chase if one exists.
  4. Label both ends of every run and test it with a cable tester before any camera is hung.
  5. Hang cameras covering the front entry, the mail area, the cellar entrance, the back yard and the roof bulkhead; aim hall cameras down the corridor, not into apartment doors.
  6. Set recording schedules, motion areas and privacy masks.
  7. Install the AVYCON ONE app on each phone that needs it, test remote viewing off the building Wi-Fi, and leave a written map of every camera and cable.

When the AVYCON ONE app goes blank

Most "the cameras disappeared" calls trace back to a network change rather than a broken camera. Typical culprits:

  • A new internet provider or router. The recorder's address on the network changes and the app loses its path. The recorder has to be reconnected and, in some cases, re-added to the app.
  • A power outage. Without battery backup, the recorder and switch may come back in the wrong order or not at all.
  • An outdated app or phone. Reviews on the App Store mention the app hanging; testing it on every phone before the installer leaves catches most of this.

Keep the recorder's serial number, QR code and admin login in a sealed envelope somewhere other than the camera closet.

Building rules to clear first

  • Co-ops and condos: boards commonly want a certificate of insurance, a scope showing where each new hole goes, and work limited to set hours.
  • Historic districts: anything fixed to a street facade can fall under Landmarks Preservation Commission rules, so check before mounting on the front.
  • Face recognition: New York City requires businesses that collect biometric identifiers from customers to post a clear notice at entrances. Treat that feature as a legal decision, not a checkbox.
  • Licensing: security camera installation in New York State is licensed work. Ask any installer for their state license.

Questions to ask any installer

  • Will every run be tested and labeled at both ends?
  • What is the recorder's PoE budget, and how much of it will we use?
  • How many days will we keep at our resolution and settings? (Check it yourself with the security camera storage calculator.)
  • Who sets up the app, on how many phones, and with what permissions?
  • What happens if we change internet providers next year?
  • Will you leave a written camera and cable map?

Frequently asked questions

Can an AVYCON recorder record cameras from another brand?

Usually, if both the recorder and the camera support ONVIF. AVYCON states that third-party ONVIF cameras can be added. ONVIF may need to be enabled on the camera first, and brand-specific smart detection may not work across brands.

Why did my AVYCON cameras vanish from the app after I switched internet providers?

A new router changes the network the recorder sits on, so the app loses its route to it. The recorder needs to be reconnected to the new network and sometimes re-added in the AVYCON ONE app. The cameras themselves are often fine.

How many days of footage will an AVYCON recorder keep?

It depends on the hard drive size, the number of cameras, resolution, frame rate and whether you record all the time or only on motion. Plug your numbers into a storage calculator before choosing a drive.

What does a co-op board usually need from the installer?

Typically a certificate of insurance, a written scope showing where cable will run and where holes will be drilled, and work kept to the building’s allowed hours. Ask the managing agent for the alteration rules early.
