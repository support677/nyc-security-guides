AV Sound Installation Brooklyn projects in Brooklyn are shaped less by the gear list than by the building. A brownstone stacks four very different floors, a converted loft has concrete and glass that bounce sound everywhere, and almost every home shares at least one wall or floor with someone else. This guide plans a system the way the house is built, floor by floor, then covers wiring, neighbors and the price bands Abstract Enterprises Security Systems publishes. The full service list is on the AV Sound Installation Brooklyn page.

Floor by floor in a brownstone

Garden level. Often the best room for movies: low windows, a solid floor and fewer neighbors below. The ceiling joists overhead can take in-ceiling speakers, and the cellar underneath is the easy route for speaker cable and the equipment rack.

Parlor floor. High ceilings, plaster walls, a fireplace and big front windows. This is usually background music and a TV with a soundbar or a discreet left-center-right set, not a full theater. Decorative ceilings and plaster medallions limit where in-ceiling speakers can go.

Upper floors. Bedrooms and a home office, typically one or two zones of music controlled from a phone.

Back yard. Weather-rated speakers on the rear wall, aimed into your own yard and kept at a modest volume, because the next yard is a few feet away.

Loft concrete or exposed deckPendant or surface speakers, cable in planned racewayHard, reflective room that needs acoustic treatment

Keeping the bass on your side of the wall

Online forums are full of people asking whether surround sound belongs in an apartment at all, usually after a neighbor's subwoofer kept them awake. Low frequencies travel through structure, not just air, so a few rules matter more than any product. Keep subwoofers off the party wall and off the floor above a neighbor; isolation pads help. Do not put in-ceiling speakers in a ceiling that is someone else's floor. Calibrate the system so dialogue is clear at a lower volume instead of turning everything up. In co-ops and condos, check the alteration agreement before cutting into walls or ceilings, since many require board approval for that work.

One system or several zones?

Most Brooklyn households want two different things: a room for movies and music in the rest of the house. Abstract Enterprises Security Systems installs Sonos for casual whole-home audio and separate amplifiers and speakers for dedicated rooms, and says plainly that the two are not the same job. A practical plan is a multi-room system for kitchen, bedrooms and yard, plus one room with a receiver and wired surround speakers. In a two-family, keep each household's zones on its own network and its own app account so one family's playlist does not end up in the other's kitchen.

Before the walkthrough

  • List the rooms and what each is for: movies, music, calls, or background.
  • Note which walls and ceilings you share with neighbors.
  • Find where the internet enters and where a small equipment rack could live.
  • Check your lease, co-op or condo rules on cutting walls and ceilings.
  • Decide whether you want to control everything from one app.

Frequently asked questions

Can I have surround sound in a Brooklyn apartment without bothering neighbors?

Yes, with care. Keep subwoofers off shared walls and floors, use isolation pads, avoid in-ceiling speakers in a ceiling that is someone else's floor, and calibrate so the system sounds full at a lower volume.

Can in-ceiling speakers go into a brownstone ceiling?

Usually, between the wood joists of an interior floor. Decorative plaster ceilings, medallions and joists that run the wrong way for the cable route can limit placement, so it is checked on site.

How much does whole-home audio cost in Brooklyn?

The Abstract Enterprises Security Systems Brooklyn page lists about $12,000 to $30,000 for a whole-home multi-room system with in-ceiling speakers and app control.

Is a dedicated home theater realistic in a brownstone?

Often on the garden level, which has a solid floor and fewer neighbors below. The published Brooklyn range for a full dedicated theater room is about $40,000 to $140,000.

Do I need acoustic treatment?

For casual listening, usually not. For a dedicated theater or a hard loft with concrete and glass, treatment makes a large difference to clarity and how loud you need to play.

Does a co-op need to approve speaker installation?

Cutting into walls or ceilings is commonly covered by the alteration agreement, so check with the board or managing agent first. On-wall and bookshelf speakers usually avoid that step.
