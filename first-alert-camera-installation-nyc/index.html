First Alert Camera Installation NYC: Settle the Panel, the Power and the Permission First

By Abstract Enterprises Security Systems — licensed NYS low-voltage contractor, in security since 2000 · Updated 2026-11-09

First Alert Camera Installation NYC starts with a fact that surprises a lot of people: the First Alert name on today's cameras is not the smoke-detector product line most New Yorkers picture. The cameras belong to Resideo's First Alert VX Series — a video doorbell plus indoor and outdoor cameras built to sit alongside Resideo's professional alarm panels. That shapes everything about the install. You are usually adding video to a security system, not hanging a standalone gadget, so a few decisions need to be made before anyone drills into brick.

Decision 1: is there a panel in the picture?

Look for a keypad by the door. If it says Honeywell, Resideo, VISTA or ProSeries, you likely already own the hardware VX cameras are designed to pair with. Resideo states that the VX line integrates with ProSeries and VISTA panels, so camera events and alarm events can reach the same people in the same app. Dealers typically set the cameras up through Resideo's professional tools and the customer views them in the Total Connect app, with cloud video tied to a video service plan from the alarm dealer.

  • You have a compatible panel: First Alert cameras are a natural add-on, and the main work is power, placement and linking them to your existing account.
  • You have no panel and want monitoring later: they can be a reasonable first step toward a full system.
  • You just want a doorbell camera on an app: a consumer brand may be simpler. Ask the installer to say so plainly if that is the case.

Decision 2: how each camera gets power

Power is where old city buildings make or break the plan. The published requirements for the VX models are specific:

ModelPowerWhere it fits in NYC
VX1 video doorbell16–24 VAC doorbell transformer, 10 VA minimum; chime adapter includedHouse and brownstone doors that still have a working bell circuit
VX3 outdoor camera12 VDC adapter or 802.3af PoERear yards, alleys, roof doors, storefronts — PoE where a cable path exists
VX5 indoor camera12 VDC plug-in adapterApartment entries, vestibules seen from inside, small offices

The VX cameras are dual-band Wi-Fi, but a brick party wall between the router and a rear-yard camera can make Wi-Fi a weak link. Where a cable can reach, PoE gives one wire for power and data and keeps recording steady. Where it cannot, a properly placed access point beats a camera living on one bar. Pre-war doorbell wiring needs to be metered before a VX1 goes up; a buzzer line from a building intercom is not a doorbell circuit, and the two should not be mixed. Any new outlet or line-voltage work belongs to a licensed electrician and should be named on the quote.

Decision 3: who has to say yes

In a city where roughly two-thirds of homes are rentals, the wall you want to use often is not yours. Work through permission in this order:

  1. Landlord or managing agent for anything on a door frame, hallway, stoop or facade.
  2. Co-op or condo board for common areas; many have written camera policies and want a contractor's insurance certificate.
  3. Landmarks Preservation Commission if the building is designated or in a historic district. Calling 311 or checking LPC's map confirms status. Cameras are usually steered away from the primary facade.
  4. The super for the day itself: service elevator bookings, contractor hours and access to the cellar or riser.

Aim the lens at your own space. A hallway camera that covers foot traffic is on firmer ground than one that sees into a neighbor's apartment each time their door opens, and video of a private space taken without consent is a crime in New York. Businesses using facial recognition must post signs at customer entrances under the city's biometric identifier law; ordinary recording does not trigger it.

Common snags by building type

Brownstones and rowhouses

Masonry fronts, plaster inside and, on landmarked blocks, little room on the facade. The vestibule, areaway or rear wall often carries the camera instead, and old bell wiring may or may not still work.

Walk-ups

Narrow stairs, brick party walls and a small mailbox vestibule. A clean cable route up a riser or along a hallway raceway decides whether lobby cameras can be wired.

Elevator buildings and co-ops

Thick plaster, masonry floors and boards with alteration rules. PoE runs through risers or drop ceilings work well once the pathway is approved.

Storefronts

Roll-down gates can block a camera mounted inside the glass at exactly the hours the shop is closed. Mounting beside or behind the gate line, and scheduling alerts for after hours, solves most of it.

Questions to ask before you book

  • Will these cameras link to my existing panel and account, or start a new one?
  • Which cameras will be PoE and which Wi-Fi, and why?
  • Is a UPS on the router and PoE switch included, so recording survives short outages?
  • What video plan does cloud storage need, and who bills for it?
  • What paperwork will you send the board or managing agent?

Frequently asked questions

Are First Alert cameras the same company as First Alert smoke detectors?

The cameras sold today are Resideo’s First Alert VX Series, a professional line that includes a video doorbell, indoor and outdoor cameras designed to work with Resideo’s ProSeries and VISTA alarm panels.

Do First Alert VX cameras need a subscription?

Cloud video for VX cameras is typically tied to a Total Connect video service plan through the alarm dealer. Ask your installer which plan your cameras will use and who bills for it before install day.

What power does the First Alert VX1 doorbell need?

The VX1 runs on a 16–24 VAC doorbell transformer with a 10 VA minimum and comes with a chime adapter for mechanical and digital chimes. It is not designed to run on an apartment intercom or buzzer line.

Can I put a First Alert camera on my apartment door in a NYC building?

Usually on your own door with the landlord’s or board’s consent, aimed at your doorstep rather than into other apartments. Hallway walls, stoops and facades are common areas the owner or board controls.

Can a First Alert camera go on a landmarked building in NYC?

Often, but usually not on the primary facade. The Landmarks Preservation Commission generally prefers less visible spots like the vestibule, areaway or rear wall; check the building’s status before mounting outside.
