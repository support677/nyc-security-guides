Capture Camera Installation NYC searches come from two kinds of people. Some already own, or have been quoted, Capture equipment, the surveillance line sold through the security distributor ADI. Others are looking for a camera that "captures" license plates at a gate or garage ramp. Both end up needing the same thing: a recorder in a sensible place, real cable to every camera, and a plan built around what each lens must prove. This guide covers both meanings, then gives you a checklist for comparing installers. What "Capture" means on an equipment list

Capture is ADI's exclusive brand, and its Capture Advance range covers dome, turret, bullet and eyeball cameras, network and coax recorders, monitors, mounts and junction boxes. ADI markets the Advance products as NDAA compliant, which matters to some commercial tenants, schools and landlords whose insurers or corporate policies ask about where camera hardware comes from. Viewing runs through the Capture ADV phone app and a PC client.

The important split for a New York building is not camera style; it is which kind of recorder sits in the cellar.

Coax DVR or network NVR: pick the path first

ADI sells Capture kits both ways: an HD-over-coax DVR with eyeball cameras, and a PoE NVR with IP turrets, including 16-channel 4K network recorders. The choice usually follows the cable you already have.

Do not assume old coax is usable. Cellars here often hold several generations of dead cable, and runs that were stapled, spliced or water-soaked can look fine and still produce rolling or noisy pictures. Have each run tested and labeled before you commit to the coax route.

The other "capture": plate cameras at gates and ramps

A general-purpose camera watching a driveway records cars; it rarely records readable plates at night. Plate capture is a narrow, purpose-set job: the camera is aimed tight on the spot where every vehicle slows, with a fast shutter and a lens chosen for that distance, so headlights and motion do not wash out the characters. In this city that usually means a garage ramp under a Manhattan co-op, a Queens two-family's shared driveway, a Staten Island small-business lot, or a Bronx loading gate. Expect one camera for overview and a second set only for plates. Abstract Enterprises Security Systems installs both Capture systems and plate-capture cameras, so say which problem you are solving when you ask for a quote.

Building rules that shape where the cameras go

  • Who owns the surface. Renters need written landlord approval, co-op shareholders need board sign-off, and a shop leasing ground-floor space typically needs the owner's consent before mounting anything on the front of the building.
  • Landmarks. Inside a historic district, the Landmarks Preservation Commission generally wants equipment kept off the primary facade where an alternative exists; when a camera must go there, keep it small and painted to match.
  • Permits. Under Local Law 128 of 2024, in force from late December 2025, qualified people may run low-voltage camera cabling without a city electrical permit, but a new 120-volt outlet is still licensed-electrician work.
  • Neighbors. In semi-attached Queens homes and shared alleys, set privacy masks so the camera covers your door and drive, not the house next door.

Size the recorder before you buy it

A 2 TB drive in a kit sounds large until you put eight 5 MP cameras on continuous recording. Retention depends on camera count, resolution, frame rate, compression and whether the recorder writes around the clock or on motion. Landlords responding to a package theft, or a store owner reviewing a weekend break-in, need footage that is still there days later. Before trusting the box label, run a Capture setup through the free security camera storage calculator and check it covers the number of days your landlord or insurer will ask about.

Questions to ask any installer

  1. Will you test and label every existing cable before deciding to reuse it?
  2. Is the quote for a DVR or an NVR, and why that one for my building?
  3. What exactly will each camera show? Ask for a written list: entrance faces, register, package shelf, gate plates.
  4. Where will the recorder live, and is it off the floor, away from the boiler and leaks, and locked?
  5. Who gets the admin password at handover, and will remote viewing be set up on the Capture ADV app for the people who need it?
  6. Will you handle the board, landlord or managing-agent paperwork before the first hole is drilled?

If you need footage from an existing system today

Do not reboot or unplug the recorder; on some systems that can interrupt recording or the export you are trying to make. Write down the date and time of the incident, note the recorder's brand and model if visible, and find out who has the admin password. Then call, because recorders overwrite old footage on a loop and the clock is running.

Frequently asked questions

What is a Capture camera?

Capture is a surveillance brand sold exclusively through the distributor ADI. Its Capture Advance line includes cameras, coax DVRs, network NVRs and kits that ADI describes as NDAA compliant.

Should I choose a Capture DVR or NVR for my building?

If the building has coax runs that test good, a coax DVR can replace an old analog system with less wall work. If new cable is going in, or you want room to add cameras later, a PoE NVR is usually the better fit.

Can a regular security camera read license plates?

Usually not reliably, especially at night. Plate capture needs a camera aimed tightly at the point where cars slow, with settings and a lens chosen for that distance.

How do I watch Capture cameras on my phone?

Capture Advance recorders are added to the Capture ADV mobile app, and ADI also offers a PC client. The installer should set this up and hand over the admin credentials.
