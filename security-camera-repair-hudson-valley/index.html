The first 15 minutes: a safe triage order

  1. Count what is down. One camera out points at that camera, its cable or its connector. Every camera out at once almost always points upstream: the power supply, the PoE switch or the recorder.
  2. Look at the recorder. Is it powered? Is it beeping? A beeping Hikvision or Dahua recorder is usually warning that its hard drive is failing.
  3. Check what tripped. After a storm, look for a tripped breaker, GFCI outlet or surge strip feeding the recorder or switch before assuming the hardware is dead.
  4. Separate the app from the system. If the monitor at home shows live video but the phone app does not, the problem is usually the router or remote-access settings, not the cameras.
  5. Walk the outdoor runs in daylight. Note cables pulled off siding, chewed jackets near a garage or barn, or water sitting in junction boxes. Do not open camera housings in the rain.
  6. Write down the brand and model from the recorder label. It speeds up the diagnosis and tells you quickly whether parts are available.

Why repairs up here look different from the city

Hudson Valley systems tend to be spread out: a house, a detached garage, maybe a barn or a gatehouse, connected by long outdoor cable. That layout exposes more wire to frost, more equipment to lightning, and more cable to animals. AESS mentions deer pulling cable off siding, squirrels chewing jackets in outbuildings and raccoons moving into outdoor switch boxes. A repair plan that only swaps the camera will miss the cause if the run itself is the problem, so ask the technician to test the cable and the power at the switch, not just the camera.

What cannot be repaired in the field

Ring, Nest, Arlo and Wyze cameras are sealed consumer devices. AESS does not service them, and what usually remains is either the maker’s warranty process or an upgrade to wired cameras. Most recorder-based brands, including Hikvision, Dahua, Lorex, Swann, Reolink, Amcrest, Uniview and Night Owl, can be repaired regardless of who installed them.

FAQ

What should I budget for security camera repair in the Hudson Valley?

AESS charges a $190 to $230 service call depending on the county, applied to the repair if you proceed. Typical repairs run from a $200–$450 drive swap up to $500–$1,170 for NVR work.

My DVR is beeping. What does that mean?

On Hikvision and Dahua recorders a steady beep usually means the hard drive is reporting a fault. Check the storage or HDD status in the menu; if it shows a warning or error, the drive likely needs replacing.

Several cameras died after a thunderstorm. Is the whole system ruined?

Not necessarily. Surges often damage the switch, the recorder or a few cameras while the rest survive. A technician should test each part, then add surge protection so the next storm does less damage.

Can you repair Ring or Nest cameras?

No. Ring, Nest, Arlo and Wyze are sealed products without field-serviceable parts. The options are a warranty replacement from the manufacturer or upgrading to a wired system.

Why does my night vision turn white in winter?

Infrared light reflects off snow, glass storm doors and close walls, washing out the picture. Re-aiming the camera, changing its settings or using a color night-vision model usually fixes it.

Will you repair a system someone else installed?

Yes. AESS works on any brand and any installer’s system, except the sealed consumer cameras above.
