Hikvision Security Camera Installation NYC: Check the Use Rules, Lock Down the Network, Then Pick the Cameras

By Abstract Enterprises Security Systems — licensed NYS low-voltage contractor, in security since 2000 · Updated 2026-11-10

Hikvision Security Camera Installation NYC questions usually arrive in the wrong order. People ask which model to buy first, when the questions that actually decide the job are whether the brand is allowed for your kind of property, and whether the system will be configured so strangers can't log into it. This guide takes them in the order a careful installer would. Step 1: Is Hikvision a fit for this property at all?

Hikvision is on the FCC's Covered List. A public notice the FCC released on June 26, 2026 (effective July 16, 2026) bars importing and marketing the company's surveillance equipment for four uses: public safety, government facilities, physical security surveillance of critical infrastructure, and other national security purposes. The FCC said it would hold off enforcing the critical-infrastructure part until it defines that term. The notice does not make owners rip out cameras they bought legally, and it leaves sales for ordinary private uses alone. These rules have changed several times since 2022 and are still being litigated, so check the FCC Covered List page or ask a lawyer if your project is close to the line. Nothing here is legal advice.

Step 2: Plan the security of the cameras themselves

Hikvision is one of the most widely deployed camera brands, so it gets probed constantly. In the stories about hijacked feeds, the cause is nearly always a recorder left on its factory password with ports open to the internet. Before install day, ask the installer to commit to these in writing:

  • A unique admin password on the NVR and every camera. Don't let the installer keep it as the only copy. You get it at handoff.
  • Cameras on their own subnet or VLAN, or plugged straight into the NVR's PoE ports, so they never touch the building Wi-Fi.
  • No port forwarding on your router. Remote viewing goes through the Hik-Connect app, set up under an account you own.
  • Current firmware on the NVR and cameras, plus a note of the version installed.
  • A separate user login for each manager, super or family member, so you can take one away without resetting everyone.

If you already have an older system and nobody knows the password, see the Hik-Connect app support guide. Account ownership is usually what's actually broken.

Step 4: Understand what moves the price

The Abstract Enterprises Security Systems page shows real ranges. It puts installed Hikvision cameras at roughly $400–$750 each depending on model and site. The tiers it lists are $1,800–$3,500 for a small 4–6 camera system, $3,500–$8,000 for 8–16 cameras, and $8,000 and up for large commercial jobs with PTZ, plate capture or multiple buildings. Final pricing follows a free site visit. Within those ranges, these things move the number most in the city:

  • Cable distance and difficulty. A run through a finished pre-war hallway with plaster ceilings costs more labor than one through an open cellar.
  • Reusable wiring. Existing cable from an older system can sometimes be reused, which cuts the cost of an upgrade.
  • Specialty cameras. Each PTZ or plate camera costs more than a fixed turret and takes longer to aim and tune.
  • Recorder size. Leaving spare NVR channels now is cheaper than replacing the recorder when you add cameras later.

Step 5: What install day looks like

  1. Walk-through with the super or owner to confirm each camera position against the plan.
  2. Cable runs and mounts, with outdoor penetrations sealed.
  3. NVR placed somewhere locked and ventilated, never on top of a boiler-room shelf.
  4. Cameras aimed, detection zones drawn and recording schedules set.
  5. Passwords changed, firmware checked, network isolation confirmed.
  6. Hik-Connect set up on your phone and a test export of a clip, so you know how to hand footage to police or an insurer.

Post a recording notice at commercial entrances. Treat audio separately, because recording audio carries stricter consent rules than video, and many owners just leave the microphones off.

FAQ

Is Hikvision banned in NYC?

No blanket ban applies to private buyers. Under the FCC's June 2026 public notice, Hikvision equipment may not be imported or marketed for public safety, government facilities, critical-infrastructure surveillance or national security uses, and equipment already bought legally can stay in use. Private homes and ordinary businesses generally fall outside those uses, but check current FCC guidance for edge cases.

Do I have to remove Hikvision cameras I already own?

According to the FCC notice as quoted by Hikvision, no. It does not require owners to stop using legally purchased, previously authorized equipment. Government or grant-funded sites may have separate contract rules, so check those.

What does a Hikvision camera system cost to install in NYC?

The Abstract Enterprises Security Systems page lists roughly $400–$750 per camera installed, with small systems at $1,800–$3,500, mid-size at $3,500–$8,000 and large commercial jobs at $8,000 and up. A site visit sets the final price.

Is Hik-Connect safe to use?

It's the normal way to view these cameras remotely, and it's safer than forwarding router ports. Use an account you own, a strong unique password and separate logins for each person.

Does a Hikvision NVR work without internet?

Yes. The NVR keeps recording on its own drives. Internet only matters for viewing on your phone and getting alerts.

Can an installer reuse my old camera wiring?

Often, yes. Existing cable can sometimes carry new cameras, which saves labor. The installer has to test each run first.
