Uniview Camera Installation NYC requests mostly come from people who want serious commercial features without paying premium-brand prices: the deli owner who needs readable faces at the register, the landlord of a mixed-use building with a lobby and a cellar, the small office that wants a clean recorder in a closet. Uniview, often sold as UNV, fits that space well. This guide covers which Uniview lines suit which New York spaces, why the app situation confuses people, and what working inside an occupied city building actually involves. Pricing and the quote form are on the Uniview Camera Installation NYC page.

Who Uniview tends to suit in the city

Uniview's strength is value per camera on recorder-based systems. That makes it a good match for:

  • Stores and delis that need several cameras on a counter, aisles, a stockroom and the sidewalk door, recording around the clock to a local NVR.
  • Small residential and mixed-use buildings where the owner wants lobby, mailroom, stair and cellar coverage without a monthly fee per camera.
  • Offices and showrooms with drop ceilings, where small domes and turrets disappear into the grid.
  • Parking areas and loading docks where color at night and vehicle detection make the footage searchable.

It suits less well when someone wants a battery camera stuck to a window or a phone-only system with no recorder. Uniview is built around wired PoE cameras and an NVR.

EZView, UniEase and the recorder's own screen

The most common Uniview question we hear is which app to use. Uniview has had more than one phone app over the years. Older installs were often set up in EZView; newer recorders are commonly managed in UniEase, and owners on forums point out that some settings, such as notifications, are easier to change there. Uniview's sister brand, Uniarch, uses its own app, ArchLive, which adds to the confusion. On top of that, the NVR has its own web interface and a monitor menu for deeper settings. At handover we write down which app your recorder uses, which account it is tied to and who has admin rights, so nobody is guessing two years from now.

Adding Uniview to a system you already have

Many NYC jobs are not new systems. A building may have an old DVR with coax cameras, a mix of brands, or a recorder nobody has the password for. Uniview cameras and recorders support ONVIF, the industry standard that lets different brands talk to each other, but mixing brands usually means losing some smart features such as person and vehicle detection on the other brand's cameras. When the existing cameras are analog, the clean path is replacing the recorder and cameras in stages, reusing the cable routes where they are sound and pulling new Cat6 where they are not.

Working inside an occupied New York building

The camera work is the easy part. The building is what takes planning:

  • Managing agents and boards often need to approve work in common areas, and many ask for a certificate of insurance before the first visit.
  • Work hours are usually set by the building, and a store that cannot close means drilling and ladder work before opening or after close.
  • Cable paths in older buildings run through cellars, risers and hallway ceilings; drop ceilings help, plaster and brick take longer.
  • Privacy: cameras cover the entrance, lobby and common areas, not inside apartments or across into a neighbor's windows, and we mask anything that does not belong in the frame.
  • The recorder goes somewhere locked and off the main path, ideally on a battery backup with the network gear.

What the NYC page shows for price

The NYC page puts an installed Uniview camera at roughly $400 to $700 each, recorder, cable, setup and app included, and the figure per camera falls as the job grows. Four cameras for a small home or shop tend to come in near $1,800 to $3,000. Add a PTZ, plate-reading cameras or a big recorder and the total climbs. Recording locally has no subscription, and the final quote follows a free visit to the site.

Before the technician leaves: five things to confirm

  1. You can see every camera live and play back yesterday on your own phone.
  2. The recorder's admin password is yours, written down and stored somewhere other than the recorder closet.
  3. Recording runs continuously, and you know roughly how many days the drive holds.
  4. Motion or person alerts are set only where you want them.
  5. You know how to export a clip for the police or an insurer.

Frequently asked questions

Which app do Uniview cameras use?

It depends on the recorder and when it was installed. Older systems were often set up in EZView, while newer Uniview recorders are commonly managed in UniEase. Uniview's Uniarch line uses a separate app called ArchLive. Your installer should tell you which one your system uses and which account owns it.

Can Uniview cameras work with my existing recorder or other brands?

Usually, through ONVIF, the standard most IP camera brands support. Basic video works, but smart features like person and vehicle detection may not carry over when brands are mixed.

Are Uniview cameras good for a small store in New York?

They are a popular value choice for stores and delis because they record around the clock to a local NVR without a monthly fee, and the low-light models give color at night in dark rear areas.

I forgot my Uniview recorder password. Can it be reset?

Yes. Uniview has a reset procedure tied to the device, or the owner can regain access from the app account the recorder is registered to. After recovery, set a new admin password and keep a copy somewhere safe.

What should I budget for a Uniview system in New York City?

The NYC page lists about $400–$700 per camera installed, and a small 4-camera system often around $1,800–$3,000. Bigger commercial jobs cost more, and the firm price comes after a free site survey.
