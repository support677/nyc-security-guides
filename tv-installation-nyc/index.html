TV Installation NYC pricing and difficulty come down to one thing most people never think about until the drill is in their hand: what the wall is actually made of. A 55-inch set on new drywall over wood studs is a quick job. The same set on pre-war plaster, a high-rise metal-stud partition or a brick party wall needs different hardware and more time. This guide helps you identify your wall, sort out building paperwork, and read a quote line by line. Sort out the building before you book

Most NYC landlords accept a mounted TV; a typical bracket leaves four to six bolt holes that patch easily at move-out. Co-ops and condos add a step:

  • Certificate of insurance (COI). Many managing agents require one from any contractor entering the building, often naming the building as additional insured. Abstract Enterprises Security Systems lists a COI for your building at no charge.
  • Elevator and work hours. Some buildings require a reserved service elevator or limit drilling to set weekday hours in the house rules. Find out before booking.
  • Timing. The Abstract Enterprises Security Systems booking policy asks that approvals, elevator reservations and COI requests be handled at least 48 hours ahead so the crew can start on arrival.
  • Stairs. A walk-up above the second floor with no elevator carries a small carry-up charge on the calculator ($40).

Reading the quote, line by line

On the Abstract Enterprises Security Systems page the starting labor price is $179.99, which covers a 32-to-55-inch TV on a flat or tilt mount on standard drywall. From there each choice moves the number:

  • Screen size: 56–65 inches adds $30, 66–75 adds $60, 76–85 adds $100, and 86 inches or more brings a second technician.
  • Mount style: full-motion adds $40, ceiling $85, over a fireplace $130.
  • Wall type: see the table above.
  • Cables: a paintable on-wall cord cover is $59; in-wall cable concealment with the kit is $169.
  • Bracket: bring your own rated bracket at no charge, or add one from the list.

Read the "included" box as carefully as the price. The listed work covers finding studs, mounting and leveling, connecting power and up to two of your devices, tidying cables behind the set and cleaning up dust. It does not include patching or painting old holes, moving or adding electrical outlets, or a bracket unless you add one. If the tech finds something you did not select — brick behind the drywall, for instance — the policy is to quote it before doing it.

What happens on install day

  1. The tech confirms wall type and scans for pipes, conduit and wires before drilling.
  2. Your bracket is checked against the TV's size and weight; an underrated bracket will not be used.
  3. Height is measured and marked with you in the room.
  4. The bracket goes up with hardware matched to the wall, then the TV is hung and leveled.
  5. Power and your devices are connected, cables are tidied or concealed, and dust is cleaned up.

Renting? Plan your move-out now

Photograph the wall before mounting. Keep the bolt holes inside the bracket footprint. When you leave, spackle and touch-up paint usually handle it — your super may have the matching paint. If your lease forbids wall holes entirely, a floor stand or TV-on-stand setup avoids the question.

FAQ

Can I mount a TV on a plaster wall in a pre-war NYC apartment?

Yes. Plaster over wood lath holds a TV well once the studs are found with a deep scanner; bolts go into the studs or toggles through the lath. The mistakes are using magnet stud finders or plain drywall anchors in old plaster.

Do I need my landlord's permission to mount a TV in NYC?

Check your lease's alterations clause. In practice most landlords accept a mounted TV because the bolt holes patch easily. Co-ops and condos more often require a certificate of insurance from the installer rather than a formal approval.

What does TV mounting cost in NYC?

On the Abstract Enterprises Security Systems calculator, labor starts at $179.99 for a 32-to-55-inch TV on a flat or tilt mount on standard drywall. Larger screens, full-motion or fireplace mounts, harder walls and cable concealment each add a listed amount.

Can you mount a TV on metal studs in a newer building?

Yes, but not with lag bolts, which pull through thin metal. Heavy-duty toggle bolts that grip the back of the stud are used instead, and very large TVs may need a backer board.

How high should a wall-mounted TV be?

For seated viewing, put the center of the screen roughly 42 to 48 inches off the floor. Above a fireplace or dresser, use a tilt mount so the screen angles down toward you.

Does TV installation include hiding the wires?

Basic installation tidies cables behind the TV. A paintable on-wall cover or in-wall cable concealment is a separate add-on, priced on the Abstract Enterprises Security Systems calculator.
