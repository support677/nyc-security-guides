Walk your home like a burglar would

Before any quote, spend ten minutes listing every way in. In NYC that list is usually longer than people expect:

  • Front door, and any second door from a vestibule or hallway.
  • Windows that open onto a fire escape. They're the classic weak spot in walk-ups.
  • Garden-level and basement doors, including the one to the trash area.
  • Rear windows reachable from a yard, roof or neighboring extension.
  • Roof hatches and skylights on top-floor units and row houses.

Each of those becomes a door/window sensor, or it falls inside a motion or glass-break sensor's coverage. That count, more than square footage, sets the size of the system.

What it costs, using published numbers

Abstract Enterprises Security Systems publishes these figures on its page. They aren't industry averages:

  • Apartment or small home: starts at $600 for a wireless panel, door and window sensors, a motion sensor, a keypad, a cellular communicator, app setup and training.
  • Whole house: typically $2,000–$6,000 with more entry points, hardwired or hybrid sensors, smoke and CO, cameras and a video doorbell.
  • Existing system check: a $250 diagnostic visit, which counts toward the job if you hire them.
  • Payment: a written quote first, then 50% down to get on the calendar and the remainder at completion.

If you're adding cameras, decide how many days of footage you want to keep. The security camera storage calculator shows what that means for drive size.

A common thread on renter forums is people giving up on big-name companies because of long contracts, or because the company wouldn't install for tenants at all. You own the equipment in a no-contract setup, so you can start with self-monitoring and add professional monitoring later without new hardware.

Renting? Here's what usually works

Wireless sensors mount with adhesive or small screws and come down cleanly when you move. Abstract Enterprises Security Systems notes that it doesn't do electrical work or wall patching, which is another reason wireless is the cleaner choice for most tenants. Read your lease before anything goes on the outside of the unit door. A video doorbell or camera in a shared hallway can need the landlord's OK, and the building's own entry intercom is off limits. A cellular communicator matters in apartments because the alarm keeps reporting even if someone cuts the internet line in the basement.

What install day looks like

  1. The tech arrives in a scheduled window. Abstract Enterprises Security Systems books 2-hour windows between 8 AM and 8 PM, seven days a week.
  2. The panel and keypad go near the main entry, with the communicator placed for good cell signal.
  3. Sensors go on every listed door and window, and each one is tripped and tested.
  4. Motion and glass-break sensors are placed and walk-tested.
  5. Cameras and the doorbell go on the same app, if you ordered them.
  6. Everyone in the household learns arm, disarm and the duress code before the tech leaves.

A typical apartment or small home is usually wrapped up in one trip of a few hours. To get a written price for yours, go to the Home Security System Installation NYC page.

FAQ

How much does a home security system cost to install in NYC?

Abstract Enterprises Security Systems publishes a starting price of $600 for an apartment or small home and $2,000–$6,000 for a typical whole-house system. Optional professional monitoring is $30 or $45 a month.

Can I get a security system as a renter in NYC?

Yes. Wireless sensors and a cellular panel install without running wire through walls and come with you when you move. Check your lease before mounting anything outside your unit door.

Do I need a monitoring contract?

Not with an owned system. Self-monitoring in the app costs nothing, and central-station monitoring can be added month to month.

Can an old ADT, Brinks or Honeywell system be reused?

Often, yes. If the wired door and window sensors still work, a new panel and communicator can sometimes take over. A $250 diagnostic visit, credited toward the work, tells you what's reusable.

How long is install day?

A typical apartment or small home needs a single visit lasting a few hours, and that includes app setup and a household walkthrough.

Should cameras be part of the system?

They're optional, but most people add a doorbell camera at least. Putting cameras, the doorbell and the alarm in one app means you can see what tripped a sensor before you react.
