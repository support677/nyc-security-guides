Lorex Camera Installation Long Island jobs are decided less by the camera than by the house. A Lorex wired kit is the same box in Massapequa and in Riverhead, but getting four or eight cables from a recorder to the eaves is a different puzzle in a slab ranch, a cape with knee walls, or a high ranch whose levels do not line up. Tell an installer your house style, your town and whether a person can get into the attic, and most of the plan follows. This guide goes house type by house type, then covers prices, bring-your-own kits, storm recovery and what to know about Lorex as a company. Full prices for both counties are on the Lorex Camera Installation Long Island page.

Why the house style comes first

Wired Lorex cameras need one network cable each, running back to the recorder, which also powers them. The camera positions on most Long Island homes are similar: driveway, front door, back door, one side yard. What changes is the path inside the walls and ceilings between those corners and the closet where the recorder sits. That path is most of the labour, and labour is most of the difference between two quotes.

Pre-made kit cables with molded plugs are a common snag. They need large holes, the lengths rarely match the house, and a kit cable may stop a few feet short of the far corner of a colonial. Installers usually run their own cable and terminate it on site.

Already bought the kit at the club store?

Plenty of Island garages hold a Lorex box bought on sale. That is fine: the equipment drops off the bill and you pay for time, plus any longer cable needed. Before the visit, photograph the label under the recorder and the model printed on one camera. Lorex sells several generations and apps that do not all mix, so confirming the recorder and cameras belong together saves a wasted trip. A Long Island Reddit thread on home cameras makes the same point from the owner side: the cables in the box are often the first thing to go.

After the storm: power, water and a recorder that will not start

Many urgent Lorex calls on the Island follow a PSEG Long Island outage or a wet basement. Three habits prevent most of them.

  1. Raise the recorder. Off the basement floor at minimum; upstairs in a closet if the house has ever taken water.
  2. Put it on a battery backup along with the modem, so short outages do not reboot everything.
  3. Check the app the morning after a storm. A recorder that came back with the wrong time or a dead drive is easier to fix in a day than after a month of missing video.

Lorex the company: what to weigh

Lorex was owned by Dahua until it was sold to Skywatch in late 2022. Since 2025 three state attorneys general have sued Lorex over alleged continued reliance on Dahua parts, which Lorex disputes. Lorex's own help pages steer federal agencies, projects paid for with federal money and NDAA-covered contractors to other products. For a family home this is a judgement call for the owner. For a business on Route 110 that sells to the federal government, a fire district or a grant-funded nonprofit, pick a different brand.

Questions to settle before anyone drills

  • Which room will hold the recorder, and is it heated and dry?
  • Is there an attic hatch or crawl space a person can move through?
  • Does each camera spot have a route that avoids finished ceilings?
  • Is any run going outside to a garage, gate or pool house?
  • Who will keep the app's owner login once the installer leaves?

Frequently asked questions

How much does Lorex camera installation cost on Long Island?

On the Abstract Enterprises Security Systems list, four wired Lorex cameras with a recorder, installed, are $1,938 in Nassau and $1,985 in Suffolk. Labour-only for a kit you already own is $230 per camera in Nassau and $240 in Suffolk.

Will you install a Lorex kit I bought at Costco?

Yes. You pay for labour and any extra cable. Send a photo of the recorder label and a camera model first, so the visit is planned around the right generation of equipment.

How do you run camera cables in a ranch on a slab?

Up through the attic and down inside closet walls to the recorder, rather than through the slab. Blown-in insulation and shallow eaves are what take the time.

Are Lorex cameras banned in the US?

Not for homeowners. Lorex tells federal agencies, federally funded projects and NDAA-covered contractors not to use its products, and some Lorex products fall under 2026 FCC import and marketing limits.

What should I do if my Lorex recorder flooded?

Do not power it back on wet. A new recorder can usually be fitted upstairs and the surviving cameras plugged back in; on the Long Island list a recorder with setup starts at $493 in Nassau and $500 in Suffolk.
