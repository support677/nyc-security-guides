The model families you will see on a Dahua quote

Dahua sorts its cameras into families, and the family name usually tells you more than the megapixel count. A Brooklyn quote for a house or a small shop normally mixes two or three of them.

Family on the quoteWhat it addsWhere it earns its place in Brooklyn
WizSenseSorts people and vehicles from leaves, cats and headlightsStoops on busy blocks, where plain motion alerts fire all night
Full-ColorKeeps color after dark with a wide lens and soft white light instead of infraredRear yards, cellar stairs and side passages with no streetlight
TiOC (active deterrence)Red-and-blue flashers and a siren or voice warning when a person is detectedGated yards and closed storefronts after hours; think twice on a block of light sleepers
PTZMotorized pan, tilt and zoom, often with auto-trackingOpen loft floors, warehouse yards and parking areas in the industrial waterfront
NVRThe recorder that powers cameras over one cable, stores video and talks to the phone appEvery system; sized to camera count and how many days you want kept

If a quote lists only megapixels and no family, ask which family each camera belongs to. Two cameras with the same resolution can behave very differently at 2 a.m. on an unlit garden level.

Placing each camera, building by building

Brownstone or rowhouse

The usual plan is a turret at the parlor door looking down the stoop to the sidewalk, a second at the garden-level entrance under the stoop, a Full-Color camera on the rear wall covering the yard, and one inside the cellar door. Mounts go into mortar joints rather than the face of the brick or brownstone, and cable drops inside through a closet or an old chase so nothing runs down the front.

Two- or three-family house

Here the question is less where the cameras go and more who sees them. One NVR can carry the front door, the driveway or side alley and the yard, with a separate limited login for tenants that shows the entrance only.

Converted loft building

Long corridors, freight elevators and a shared street door. A PTZ on the loading side and fixed WizSense cameras in each corridor usually beat a camera at every apartment door, and the recorder belongs in a locked room, not in a hallway closet.

Avenue storefront

One camera over the register aimed at faces rather than the tops of heads, one inside the door looking in, one on the stockroom, and one outside under the sign. Against bright window glass, ask for a model with strong wide dynamic range so afternoon clips are not silhouettes.

Reading the Brooklyn price range

Per camera, the service page puts installed Dahua work in Brooklyn at roughly $400 to $700, with the recorder, the PoE wiring, setup and the phone app counted inside that figure, and a lower per-camera cost as the system grows. A four-camera brownstone is listed at about $1,800 to $3,000. Loft and apartment-building systems run higher. Those are the page's own ranges; the firm number comes after a free site visit, because the wall material and the cable path change the labor more than the camera does. There is no monthly fee for recording to a local NVR.

What should happen on install day

  1. Walk the plan again. Confirm each camera spot against the quote before any drilling, including what each lens will and will not see.
  2. Set the recorder's home. A shelf off the floor, away from the boiler, near the router, with a small battery backup if you asked for one.
  3. Pull and test cable. Each run gets terminated and tested before a camera goes on the end of it.
  4. Mount and aim. Cameras are aimed with you looking at the live picture, not from the ladder alone.
  5. Configure detection. Zones drawn to keep the sidewalk crowd or a neighbor's yard out of the alerts.
  6. Hand over. DMSS installed on your phone, a test clip exported, and the admin credentials given to you on paper.

Six settings to check before the crew leaves

Dahua recorders have been a favorite target for internet scanning bots for years, so the setup matters as much as the hardware.

  • The default admin password is gone, and you, not the installer, hold the new one.
  • Firmware on the NVR and cameras is current as of the install date.
  • No ports are forwarded on your router for the recorder; remote viewing runs through the app's P2P service or a VPN.
  • UPnP is switched off on the recorder.
  • Cameras plug into the NVR's own PoE ports, which keeps them off your home Wi-Fi network.
  • The recorder's clock is synced, so every clip carries the right time.

When Dahua is the wrong pick

Dahua is legal for private homes, apartment buildings, offices and stores. Federal rules under the NDAA and related FCC actions keep it out of federal government work and certain federally funded or critical-infrastructure projects, so a school, a nonprofit with federal grant conditions or a tenant with a government lease should say so up front and get a different brand specified. On landmarked blocks such as Brooklyn Heights, Fort Greene or Park Slope, the number of cameras visible from the street and how they attach can need Landmarks Preservation Commission sign-off, which favors small housings and fewer front cameras. And on rowhouse blocks where yards touch on three sides, point each rear camera at your own lot and black out the rest of the frame; New York's Civil Rights Law section 52-a lets a neighbor sue over a camera installed to record their backyard without written consent.

Frequently asked questions

What does a Dahua camera system cost to install in Brooklyn?

Published figures run about $400 to $700 a camera, with the recorder, cabling, setup and the phone app folded in. A four-camera brownstone is listed at roughly $1,800 to $3,000; a free site visit sets the final number.

Is it legal to install Dahua cameras on a Brooklyn home or store?

Yes for private property. Federal NDAA and FCC restrictions apply to federal government work and certain federally funded or critical-infrastructure projects, not to private homes, apartment buildings, offices or stores.

Which Dahua camera works best on a brownstone stoop?

A compact turret from the WizSense or Full-Color family, mounted into a mortar joint at the parlor door. It sees down the stoop to the sidewalk and sorts people from passing cars.

Do I pay a monthly fee for a Dahua system?

Not for recording. Video records to the NVR in your building and you view it free in the DMSS app.

Can a Dahua camera record my neighbor's yard?

It should not. On rowhouse blocks, rear cameras belong pointed at your own lot with privacy masks over the neighbors, and New York's Civil Rights Law section 52-a lets a neighbor sue over a camera set up to record their backyard without written consent.
