Google Nest Camera Installation Hudson Valley jobs succeed or fail on three tests that take about twenty minutes and are usually skipped: how much upload speed the property really has, whether the doorbell transformer can feed a video doorbell, and what the wall is made of where the camera will hang. Nest cameras keep their video in Google's cloud, so the internet line is part of the camera. Many houses between Yonkers and Saugerties were built long before video doorbells existed, so the transformer and the wall are part of it too. Run the three tests first and the mounting is the easy part. County pricing and what a visit covers are on the Google Nest Camera Installation Hudson Valley page.

Test 1: the upload speed, per camera

Google's specifications recommend at least 2 Mbps of upload per camera for good 1080p video, and note that DSL may not qualify. Its bandwidth chart for the current Nest Cam (battery) and Nest Cam (wired) shows about 0.7 Mbps at the High setting, rising to about 2 Mbps while an event is being recorded, and roughly 3 Mbps during events on Max Video. Events tend to happen at the same moment on several cameras, such as a car pulling into the driveway, so add the cameras together.

Measure from inside the house, on the line the cameras will use, and look at upload, not download. In parts of rural Ulster and northern Dutchess the wired options are thin and some addresses are still waiting on state-funded fiber. If the upload cannot carry the plan, it is better to hear that before the cameras are bought than after the first week of blurry clips.

Test 2: the doorbell transformer

The wired Nest Doorbell expects a chime and a transformer rated between 16 and 24 VAC with 10 to 40 VA of capacity. Plenty of older Westchester and Putnam houses still have the small transformer that was fitted for a mechanical bell decades ago, sometimes tucked beside the boiler or behind a basement ceiling panel. On Google's community forum, plenty of people with the second-gen wired model describe a 10 VA transformer causing restarts and dropped connections that went away after an upgrade to 30 VA. A meter reading at the button tells you which camp you are in. If the house has no usable bell wiring at all, the options are the battery doorbell or Google's indoor power adapter, and on a masonry front the battery model often wins.

Test 3: what the wall is made of

Vinyl and clapboard are forgiving. Stone and brick are not, and the valley has a great deal of both, from fieldstone farmhouses to brick rowhouses in the river cities. Three habits keep an old facade intact:

  • Drill the mortar joint, not the stone or brick face. Mortar can be repointed; a cracked face cannot be undone.
  • Size the hole for the connector. The outdoor Nest power cable ends in a bulky plug, and owners on Google's forum report it needs more than a half-inch opening to pass through a brick wall. Seal the hole with a grommet and exterior sealant.
  • Read the district rules first. Newburgh's East End guidelines ask that security features not obscure or damage historic fabric, and Kingston's preservation commission has asked owners to paint surface-run lines to match the facade. If your street is in a local district, ask before drilling.

Cameras on a house you rent to guests

Second homes in the valley often double as short-term rentals, and the platform rules are stricter than many owners expect. Since April 30, 2024, Airbnb has not allowed any camera or recording device that monitors indoor space, even if it is switched off, and hosts must disclose exterior cameras and their general location before guests book. Outdoor cameras also cannot cover places with a higher expectation of privacy, such as an enclosed outdoor shower or a sauna. In practice that means an outdoor doorbell and driveway camera are fine when disclosed, the indoor Nest Cam from the owner's own weeks gets unplugged and stored, and the camera angles avoid the hot tub.

Install day, step by step

  1. Run the upload test and walk the Wi-Fi to every planned spot with a phone.
  2. Meter the doorbell transformer and replace it if it is undersized.
  3. Choose each mounting point with the wall type and any district rules in mind.
  4. Pair each device in the Google Home app before it goes up the ladder.
  5. Mount, seal every penetration, and confirm a clean live view at the mounted spot.
  6. Set activity zones, quality settings that fit the upload, and who in the family gets alerts.

Frequently asked questions

How fast does my internet need to be for Google Nest cameras?

Google recommends at least 2 Mbps of upload speed per camera for good 1080p video, and says DSL may not qualify. Four cameras therefore want roughly 8 Mbps of upload. Measure the upload at the house, not the download.

Why does my wired Nest Doorbell keep restarting or going offline?

In older houses the usual cause is an undersized doorbell transformer. The wired Nest Doorbell expects 16 to 24 VAC with 10 to 40 VA of capacity, and many owners report that replacing a 10 VA unit with a 30 VA one stopped the restarts.

Can I put Nest cameras inside my Hudson Valley Airbnb?

No. Since April 30, 2024, Airbnb bans cameras that monitor any indoor space, even when they are turned off. Exterior cameras are allowed if they are disclosed before booking and do not cover private areas such as an outdoor shower or sauna.

How do you mount a Nest camera on a stone or brick house?

Drill through the mortar joint rather than the stone or brick face, size the hole for the bulky power connector, and seal it with a grommet and exterior sealant. In a local historic district, check the commission rules before drilling.

Will a Nest camera work on a barn far from the house?

Nest cameras connect only over Wi-Fi, and the signal usually fades across a long yard. The reliable fix is a wired or point-to-point link to an access point in the barn, then the camera joins that network.
