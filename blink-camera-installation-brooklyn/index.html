Blink Camera Installation Brooklyn jobs are decided less by the camera you picked and more by the building you live in. A Blink system is wireless in two hops: each camera talks to a small Sync Module by radio, and the module talks to your router. In Brooklyn both hops have to get through brick, brownstone, plaster and steel apartment doors. Below: the setup that tends to hold up in each common building type, who can say no, and what a visit costs. The service itself is on the Blink Camera Installation Brooklyn page.

Two hops, and why Brooklyn walls eat the signal

Blink rates its Sync Modules for roughly 100 feet to a camera, but that is an open-air number. Brick, concrete, stone and metal are exactly the materials Blink tells owners to keep out of the path, and a Brooklyn house is built from all four. A doorbell on a steel door ten steps from the module can struggle more than a yard camera much farther away with only a window in between. Three habits help:

  • Place the module last. Decide where each camera goes, then put the module where it can see most of them, usually on a middle floor.
  • Use a second module in tall or deep houses. Blink supports it for weak camera signal: one toward the street, one toward the yard, each on its own outlet with good Wi-Fi.
  • Fix weak Wi-Fi separately. If the router signal fades on the floor where the module belongs, a mesh node or wired access point there is the cure. Carrying the module down to the router just moves the weak link to the cameras.

Keep the system's brain above the water line

A lot of Brooklyn internet service enters in the cellar, and the router stays wherever the cable company left it. After the basement flooding that came with Hurricane Ida in 2021, that is a poor home for the equipment every camera depends on. At minimum, get the router up onto a shelf well clear of the boiler and water heater. Better, run the network up a floor and plug the Sync Module in on the parlor level. A Sync Module 2 can also keep clips on a plugged-in USB stick, and that stick sits wherever the module does, so a dry spot protects saved footage too.

Who gets a say before anything is mounted

  • NYCHA. The house rules allow a wireless camera or doorbell on your apartment door if it sits over the peephole or is fixed with adhesive. Drilling or altering the door, frame or surrounding wall is not allowed, so a battery doorbell on adhesive is the setup that fits.
  • Private rentals. The hallway outside your unit is a common area, and putting equipment there needs the landlord's permission. Keeping the device on your side of the door, with a no-drill mount, avoids most of the argument; a short note to the landlord covers the rest.
  • Co-ops and condos. Some boards want written approval for door cameras, some decide case by case, and many have no rule yet. Ask the managing agent first; a certificate of insurance goes to them before the visit.
  • Landmarked blocks. The Landmarks Preservation Commission's permit guidebook lets staff approve cameras that meet its criteria: small buildings get at most two per 25 feet of street frontage, placed discreetly (under the stoop, below a band course, behind an areaway fence) and fixed into mortar joints or plain masonry rather than cast iron or ornament. Battery Blinks need no conduit, which helps.

The doorbell button: battery or wired?

In a walk-up, the button beside your door is almost always part of the building's intercom. It belongs to the landlord and is not a power source for a Blink, so renters get the Battery Doorbell 2K+. Where a single-family rowhouse still has a bell transformer in the cellar, a wired Blink doorbell can work if the transformer falls inside Blink's supported 16 to 24 volt AC range, which gets measured before anything is connected. Note that the Wired Doorbell 2K+ will not ring an old mechanical chime. Where there is no bell wiring at all, Blink sells a plug-in adapter, but that doorbell goes dark whenever the plug is pulled or the power fails.

A Brooklyn example

Picture a four-story brownstone where boxes keep vanishing from the vestibule and the router sits on the cellar floor. The plan: a Battery Doorbell 2K+ at the parlor door, an Outdoor 2K+ fixed into the rear brick (the brick line applies), and a Mini inside the vestibule glass watching the mail ledge. The owner already has a mesh kit, so one node goes on the parlor floor and the Sync Module plugs in beside it, two floors above any water. Each device is tested in place before anything is drilled.

When Blink is the wrong tool

Battery Blinks record clips when they see motion; they are not a recorder keeping every hour on file. That is fine for a front door at home or an office that is empty after dark. A busy storefront that needs continuous footage is better served by a wired system; see the Brooklyn commercial camera guide. Frequently asked questions

Is a Blink doorbell allowed on a NYCHA apartment door?

Yes, if it is wireless and mounted over the peephole or with adhesive. NYCHA house rules do not allow holes or changes to the door, frame or surrounding wall, so a Blink battery doorbell on an adhesive or peephole mount is the setup that fits.

How do you mount a Blink outdoor camera on brick?

With masonry anchors set into the brick or, on a landmarked front, into the mortar joints, in a discreet spot such as under the stoop. The published Brooklyn list adds $65 for mounting on brick or stone.

Where should the Sync Module go in a brownstone?

Usually on a middle floor such as the parlor level, placed after the camera positions are fixed and kept off the cellar floor. In a tall or deep house a second Sync Module, one toward the street and one toward the yard, is often the fix.

Will a Blink doorbell work with my apartment building buzzer?

No. The button by an apartment door is part of the building intercom and belongs to the landlord, so it is not a power source for a Blink. Renters use the battery doorbell instead.

Can Blink cameras save clips without the cloud?

Yes, with a USB stick plugged into a Sync Module 2, which stores clips locally. Live view and phone alerts still need the module and your internet connection, so keep the network on and somewhere dry.

What does a Blink installation visit cost in Brooklyn?

The published list starts at $225 for the first hour of a setup visit, then 30-minute blocks, plus $45 for plaster or $65 for brick or stone mounts where they apply.
