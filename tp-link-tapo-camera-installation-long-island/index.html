Getting a signal to the garage behind the house

On Long Island, a garage set back from the house is what stretches a Tapo visit more often than anything else. Most Tapo cameras join the home network on 2.4 GHz, which carries farther than 5 GHz but still loses strength through brick, block foundations, aluminum siding and wet leaves. A few newer models also do 5 GHz, but that band gives up even sooner over distance. There are four realistic ways across the yard:

  • A mesh node by a back window. Cheapest, and often enough if the garage is close and in line of sight of that window.
  • An outdoor access point on the house wall. Better range, aimed at the garage. It needs a network cable to it, which usually means one run from the basement up the outside wall.
  • A buried or aerial cable to the garage. The most reliable answer when the garage will also get a light, a door opener or more cameras later. Outdoor runs use outdoor-rated, UV-resistant jacket, and anything that touches mains power for a floodlight camera goes to a licensed electrician, not to the camera installer.
  • A point-to-point wireless link. Two small dishes, one on each building, when trenching across a driveway is not an option.

Whichever route you choose, settle the network first. A camera bolted up before the signal is fixed is a camera that drops offline on the first windy, rainy night, which is exactly when you wanted it recording.

Battery and solar Tapo cameras on bigger Suffolk lots

Farther east the lots get wider and the outbuildings farther apart, and battery cameras start to make sense. A battery Tapo sleeps until motion wakes it, so it records events rather than every minute of the day. The first second or two of an event can be missed. In December, a panel shaded by oaks or a north-facing roofline may not keep up, so the camera needs an occasional charge. For a shed or a pool gate that is fine. For the main entrance to the house, a camera with constant power is the better choice.

When the storm takes the power or the internet

Long Island loses power in hurricanes, nor'easters and the odd summer thunderstorm, and a plug-in camera goes dark with the house. Two things help. A microSD card in each camera lets it keep recording locally during an internet outage, provided the camera itself stays powered; the clips are there to review once the connection comes back. A small battery backup (UPS) on the modem and router, and on any PoE switch, keeps the network alive through short outages. Tapo Care, TP-Link's cloud plan, stores clips off site, which matters if someone takes the camera, but it cannot upload anything while the modem is down.

Renters, HOAs, villages and towns

Long Island is a patchwork of towns, incorporated villages and hamlets, and each one can have its own approach to exterior work. A few cameras on a single-family house rarely raise questions, but it is worth knowing who else gets a say:

  • Renting a house or an accessory apartment: get the landlord's okay in writing for any holes in siding or brick. Peel-and-stick or clamp mounts and plug-in cameras can usually come down without a trace.
  • Condo or HOA communities: covenants often control what goes on the outside walls and may require the board's approval for exterior cameras.
  • Neighbors: aim at your own property. Tapo's privacy masking lets you black out a neighbor's window or yard that falls inside the frame.
  • Stores and offices: commercial landlords often want a certificate of insurance and limit drilling to certain hours, so send their requirements when you book.

Frequently asked questions

My garage Tapo camera keeps dropping offline. What causes that?

Almost always because the garage is at the edge of the Wi-Fi. The camera connects during setup when the phone is close, then drops when the signal fades with weather or traffic on the network. Extending the network to the garage with an outdoor access point, a mesh node or a cable usually fixes it.

Can a Tapo video doorbell use the old doorbell wiring on my house?

Often, if the existing transformer supplies enough power for the doorbell model and the wire to the button is intact. Older houses sometimes have a weak transformer or a broken run, and then the fix is a new transformer or a battery doorbell instead.

Will a Tapo camera record if the internet goes out during a storm?

Yes, if it still has power and has a microSD card in it. It keeps recording to the card and you can review the clips once the connection is back. Cloud recording through Tapo Care cannot upload while the internet is down, and nothing records if the camera loses power.

Is a solar battery Tapo camera good enough for the back of a big lot?

For a shed, pool gate or back fence it is usually fine. It wakes on motion rather than recording all day, and in winter a shaded panel may not keep it fully charged. For the front door or driveway, a camera with constant power is more dependable.

Can I have Tapo cameras installed on a house I rent on Long Island?

Usually, with the landlord's written permission for anything that drills into siding or masonry. Plug-in cameras and removable mounts can be taken down without damage when you move out.
