Start with the power at each spot

Every Ring product is designed around one power source: doorbell wires, a rechargeable battery, a plug, or the junction box of an existing outdoor light. Look at each spot on the house and the right device usually picks itself.

Install day, in the order it really happens

  1. Meter before unboxing. Older Island houses often still have their original low-output transformer, mounted near the breaker box downstairs or hidden inside the chime housing. If it cannot carry the new doorbell, it is replaced before anything goes on the door frame.
  2. Walk the Wi-Fi. With a phone at every mounting point, check the signal the Ring app reports under Device Health once a device is online. A weak reading at the front step is fixed now, not after the doorbell starts dropping out.
  3. Mount and aim. Wedge and corner kits turn the doorbell toward the walkway rather than the street, so it catches faces and the package spot instead of passing cars. Brick, stucco and block get proper anchors, and every penetration is sealed.
  4. Set up under your account. The devices are registered to your Ring login, never the installer's, so you keep full control.
  5. Tune motion. Zones and sensitivity are set, then tested with a walk in from the sidewalk and a car pulling into the driveway.
  6. Hand over. You get a walkthrough of live view, history and sharing on your own phone before the ladder goes back on the truck.

Long Island houses that need an extra step

  • Split-levels and hi-ranches. The router sits on the lower level and the front door is half a flight up through framing and ductwork. A Chime Pro, which doubles as a Wi-Fi extender for Ring devices, or a mesh node near the entry fixes most doorbells that keep going offline.
  • Slab ranches. With no basement to fish through, a new doorbell wire has to come down from the attic. When that route is blocked, a battery doorbell is the tidier answer.
  • Storm doors. Glass in front of the doorbell blinds its motion sensing and fogs the picture, so the doorbell moves to the side of the frame on a wedge.
  • Seasonal homes on the East End. An empty house needs alerts that still arrive when the internet drops. Ring Alarm can report over cellular backup on the right plan, and the modem and router belong on a battery backup.
  • Condo and HOA communities. Exterior walls are often common elements, so check the bylaws or ask the board before anything is drilled.

App settings to change before the installer leaves

  • Add family members as Shared Users instead of handing out one password.
  • Confirm two-step verification is on for the owner account.
  • Draw motion zones that stop at your property line, and add privacy zones over a neighbor's windows or yard.
  • Turn off audio recording on cameras that face the sidewalk if you would rather not capture passing conversations.
  • Set Modes so the backyard camera stays quiet while you are home and arms itself when you leave.
  • Decide whether to switch on end-to-end encryption; it protects video but turns off some features, so choose knowingly.
  • Review any face or person recognition options and switch off whatever you do not want.

Ring questions from Nassau and Suffolk homeowners

Do I need a new transformer for a Ring doorbell in an older Long Island house?

Often, yes. Many houses built in the 1950s and 1960s still have the original doorbell transformer, and it may not deliver the voltage a wired Ring doorbell needs. Metering it takes a minute; if it falls short, the transformer is replaced so the doorbell does not reboot or lag.

My house has no doorbell wiring. Can I still get a Ring doorbell?

Yes. A battery doorbell mounts without wires and rings a plug-in Chime indoors. If you would rather never charge it, a new low-voltage wire can be run from a transformer, usually through the attic on a slab ranch or the basement in a Cape.

Can my battery Ring doorbell run off the old doorbell wires?

Many battery models accept doorbell wires to trickle-charge the battery, which stretches the time between charges a great deal. The transformer still has to be healthy for that to help.

Our hi-ranch doorbell drops off Wi-Fi every few days. What fixes that?

The router is usually on the lower level and the front door is half a floor up, behind framing and ductwork. A Chime Pro or a mesh Wi-Fi node placed near the entry gives the doorbell a strong local signal and fixes most of these dropouts.

Does Ring keep recording when PSEG Long Island power goes out?

Battery cameras keep detecting motion, but clips only reach the cloud while the modem and router have power. Putting those two on a battery backup keeps the system online through short outages, and Ring Alarm's base station has its own backup battery.
