Elevator Access Control Installation Brooklyn projects usually begin at a board meeting or a managing agent's desk, not in the machine room. Someone reports strangers riding to the roof, a delivery worker knocking on doors three floors above the package room, or a fob list that nobody has cleaned since 2015. The fix is a reader in the cab, and sometimes a reader at the garage or cellar call button, so the car only moves for people the building has approved. This worksheet is for the people who have to approve, budget and explain that change: co-op boards from Bay Ridge to Brooklyn Heights, owners of rent-stabilized elevator buildings in Flatbush and Crown Heights, and managers of the new rental towers around Downtown Brooklyn and the waterfront. Name the problem before you pick the hardware

Three different complaints lead to three different scopes, and mixing them up is how buildings overspend.

  • "Anyone who gets through the front door can ride anywhere." A single reader inside each car, where a valid fob or code unlocks the panel, usually solves this. Once a rider is approved, any floor the car serves is available.
  • "Residents should only reach their own floor, the gym and the roof deck." That is floor-by-floor restriction, with a relay or interface output for each locked floor in each car. It is a bigger job and is quoted after a survey.
  • "People come in through the garage or the cellar door and call the car from there." That calls for a reader at the hall call station on those levels, so the car will not even come down without a credential.

Write down which of the three your building is actually suffering from. Bring that sentence to the survey; it shapes the quote more than any brand name will.

Brooklyn checks to run before anyone signs

Is the building rent-stabilized?

Brooklyn has already had a public fight over this. At Atlantic Plaza Towers in Brownsville, tenants organized against an owner's plan to swap key fobs for facial recognition, and reporting at the time noted that owners of older rent-regulated buildings may need state approval through Homes and Community Renewal before changing a building service. Nobody on an installation crew can give you a legal answer, so put the question to the owner's counsel early. The safest designs add a checkpoint without taking anything away: every tenant gets a physical fob, nothing requires a smartphone or a face scan, and the stairs stay usable.

Who will handle the data?

New York City's Tenant Data Privacy Act (Local Law 63, passed in 2021) covers smart access systems in New York City multiple dwellings, and the elevator is a common area. Owners need a plain-language privacy policy, should collect only what is needed, and must delete a departed tenant's data within 90 days. Decide now who does that deletion at move-out and make it a line on the turnover checklist.

How old are the fobs at the front door?

Many Brooklyn buildings installed their door systems in the 2005 to 2018 window, often on older 125 kHz proximity fobs. If the elevator is going to share credentials with the lobby, find out whether those fobs are worth extending or whether the elevator project is the moment to re-badge the building.

Who maintains the controller?

Since 2022, elevator work in the city requires city and state elevator licensing, so the controller side belongs to your elevator company. Get their name, contract number and a contact before the survey. Jobs stall most often waiting for that visit.

A short scenario: one car, six floors, no doorman

Picture a 1930s co-op off Ocean Parkway with one small car, a part-time super and a lobby that delivery riders treat as a hallway. Several shareholders are older and have home health aides on rotating schedules. A sensible plan here is the basic level: one cab reader that accepts the fobs residents already carry for the front door, PIN codes for aides tied to the apartment they serve, and a short list of contractor codes that expire. Floor restriction would add cost without fixing the real complaint, which is strangers riding at all. The board's own homework is the shareholder notice, the aide list and a contact at the elevator company.

Switch-on week, step by step

  1. Two weeks out: post the notice in the lobby and in the elevator, explaining what changes and who to call for a fob.
  2. Install days: readers, panel and cable go in with the car kept in service as much as possible; the elevator company makes its connection on its scheduled visit.
  3. Joint test: every floor, in every car, with one allowed and one refused credential, plus firefighters' recall. Under DOB Buildings Bulletin 2022-010, add-on devices must drop out when firefighters' service starts, so recall has to bring the car down and ignore the reader.
  4. Soft launch: run a few days with logging on and restrictions off, so missing fobs surface before anyone is stranded in the lobby.
  5. Go live: restrictions on, super trained, admin logins handed to whoever manages the building.

Ask the installer how the floors behave if the access panel loses power. Most apartment buildings choose to free the floors in that case, and the decision should be written down, not assumed.

Frequently asked questions

Can guests still get upstairs once the elevator needs a fob?

Yes, if the system is set up for them. The usual options are intercom floor release, where the resident answers the lobby call and the car is released to their floor, or temporary codes that expire after a set time.

How much does elevator access control cost in Brooklyn?

Published pricing is from $1,500 per car for a reader in the car and from $2,500 per car for biometrics. Floor-by-floor restriction is quoted per building after a survey, and the elevator company's visit is billed separately.

Do we need our elevator maintenance company for this?

Yes. In this city the connection at the elevator controller is elevator work and belongs to a licensed elevator contractor, who coordinates with the access control installer and tests recall with them.

Do rent-stabilized tenants have to agree to an elevator reader?

It depends on the building and the change, which is why owners of older rent-regulated buildings should ask their counsel whether state approval is needed. Designs that add a reader without removing anything tenants already have are the easiest to defend.

Will the elevator still work for firefighters?

It has to. City guidance says devices added to cabs and hall stations must stop working when firefighters' emergency operation begins, and that behavior should be tested before the system goes live.
