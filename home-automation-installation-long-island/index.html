Home automation installation in Long Island houses usually starts with a simple count: how many light switches will become smart dimmers. That number, more than the brand on the box, tells you which lighting platform fits, what the job will cost and whether it makes sense to do it now or wait for the dormer, kitchen or basement project you already have planned. Abstract Enterprises Security Systems publishes its Long Island figures on the Home Automation Installation Long Island page; this guide walks through how to use them.

Step one: count the switches you actually use

Walk the house with a notepad. Count every switch that controls lights you use every day: kitchen cans, island pendants, family room, stairs, hallways, exterior coach lights, landscape lighting. Skip closets and the boiler room. A typical Long Island Cape Cod lands somewhere between 15 and 25 switches once you include the outside lights. A center-hall colonial in Huntington or Massapequa often lands higher, and a newer Hamptons or North Fork build with open plans, exterior scenes and pool lighting can pass 50.

That count matters because the main lighting platforms are priced and designed around it.

The jump between tiers is not just more devices. RadioRA 3 is a wireless system that installs into existing switch boxes, which makes it a natural fit for finished houses. HomeWorks QSX supports wired keypads and panel-based dimming, which is easier to do well when walls are open. That is where renovation timing comes in.

Step three: line it up with the renovation

A lot of Long Island houses are always in the middle of something: a second-floor dormer on a Cape, a ranch going up a level, a basement being finished, a kitchen being gutted. If you are opening walls anyway, that is the cheapest moment to run low-voltage wire for keypads, shades, speakers and Wi-Fi access points. Fishing the same wire through finished plaster or drywall later costs more and leaves patches.

  • Walls open now: run wire for every keypad location, every shade pocket and every ceiling speaker, even if you only install part of it this year. Label the ends.
  • Walls closed, no project planned: stay with a wireless platform and keep keypads in existing switch boxes.
  • Project in a year or two: start wireless in the rooms you live in now, and plan the wired rooms around the renovation.

Tell your general contractor early. The low-voltage rough-in needs to happen after framing and electrical rough, and before insulation and drywall. Missing that window by a day means fishing wire later.

What different Long Island houses tend to need

Capes and expanded Capes

Low knee walls and finished attics make it hard to route wire to the second floor once the dormer is closed. If a dormer is coming, that is the moment to pre-wire upstairs shades and a keypad at the top of the stairs.

High ranches and split-levels

The half-flights between levels put switches in odd places. A scene keypad at each landing ("downstairs off," "kitchen evening") cuts down on walking back to switch things off.

Colonials

Long hallways and separate formal rooms mean many three-way switches. Smart dimmers replace both ends, so count each three-way location once on the master side and once for the companion.

East End and waterfront builds

Big glass facing the bay or ocean makes motorized shades the main reason for automation, along with exterior and pool lighting. Those projects usually land in the HomeWorks tier.

Questions to ask before you sign

  • Which platform do you recommend for my dimmer count, and why not the next tier up or down?
  • Will I get the admin login and the programming file when the job is done?
  • Which rooms should be wired now, during the renovation, even if devices come later?
  • Do any of my switch boxes lack a neutral wire, and how does that change the device choice?
  • Is there a monthly fee for anything? (The published Abstract Enterprises Security Systems approach is a one-time install, with no monthly contract.)

A short scenario

A family in a Nassau County Cape is adding a full second-floor dormer. Downstairs they count 14 switches they use daily; the new upstairs will add about 10 more. Instead of buying a large system now, they install RadioRA 3 dimmers in the finished downstairs rooms, and during the dormer framing they pre-wire the upstairs for two keypads, four shade pockets and a ceiling access point. When the dormer is finished, upstairs dimmers join the same system. Nothing has to be torn out, and they stay within the RadioRA 3 tier.

Frequently asked questions

How much does home automation installation cost on Long Island?

Abstract Enterprises Security Systems publishes about $8K to $15K installed for a Lutron RadioRA 3 lighting system in a home under roughly 3,000 square feet with fewer than 25 dimmers, and about $25K to $60K for Lutron HomeWorks QSX with more dimmers, audio, shades and outdoor scenes. Large estates are quoted per project.

Should I wait until my renovation to automate?

If walls will be open within a year or two, plan the wired parts (keypads, shades, ceiling speakers, access points) around that project and start with wireless dimmers in finished rooms now.

Can smart dimmers go in my existing switch boxes?

Usually yes with a wireless platform like RadioRA 3. Some older boxes have no neutral wire, which limits the device choice, so the installer should check a few boxes before quoting.

Are there monthly fees?

Not for the lighting system itself. Abstract Enterprises Security Systems installs smart home systems as a one-time job with no monthly contract.

What happens if I switch installers later?

Ask for the admin login and the programming file at handover. Without them, a dealer-programmed system is hard for anyone else to service.
